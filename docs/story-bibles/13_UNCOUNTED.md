# UNCOUNTED

**Plotbreak official-world concept**
**Genre:** isekai progression fantasy / dark-power mystery / beast-taming adventure / political thriller / found family / action
**Setting:** the counted continent of Orenne, beginning in the assessment city of Sablecourt
**Core fantasy:** Everyone in Orenne carries a Count — a readable number that is their power, their rank and their legal personhood. You died somewhere else and woke here at eighteen. When they weigh you, the stone stays dark. Not low. **Unreadable.** Everything you take from this point on is off the books, and three very different people work out what you are within the hour.

**One-line hook:** **Everyone here has a number. You have none — so nothing you do is written down.**

**Secondary hook:** **The most beloved man alive rides a tiger called Grace and carries a sword called Mercy. Both are storage.**

**Player promise:** Pick how you hold power — Blade, Staff or Hand. Take from what you beat, or share with it. Rise into a system that cannot see you, patron yourself to the man who wants to use that, help the man who wants to end the whole Ledger, or walk into the wild with a monster you decided not to kill and never come back.

---

# 1. DESIGN NORTH STAR

UNCOUNTED should feel, in the first ten minutes, like the most comfortable genre on the shelf: you woke up in a fantasy world, you are eighteen, you get to choose a class, and a number is going to go up.

That comfort is deliberate. It is the thing being used.

The first fantasy is:

> **I am nobody in a world that ranks everybody. Watch me climb.**

That has to be fun on its own. A player who never touches the conspiracy, never picks a side, and simply travels the road getting stronger and collecting a stable of tamed monsters has played this world correctly.

The deeper fantasy is:

> **The number is not mine. It came out of something that used to have it. I can take it, or I can leave it where it is and be weaker on purpose.**

And under that:

> **The strongest man in the world is genuinely kind, genuinely beloved, and genuinely the worst thing in it — and I cannot prove any of that with a number, because I do not have one.**

This is NOT a story where the progression system is secretly evil and the enlightened ending is refusing to level.

It is NOT a story where the player is revealed to be the chosen one, the lost prince, or the reincarnation of a god.

It is NOT a story where taming is the good route and killing is the bad route.

The Count is:
- power;
- law;
- employment;
- marriage eligibility;
- the reason towns have defenders at all;
- a real, working civilisation that feeds people.

The problem is not that Orenne counts.

The problem is that a hundred and forty years ago the Assize worked out how to stop the Count from returning to the world when a thing dies, and every good life in Orenne since has been built on that dam.

The player may think the dam should stay. That is a legitimate route and it has its own endings.

**The story is about what a number costs, not about whether numbers are bad.**

---

# 2. THE ONE MECHANIC EVERYTHING HANGS ON — THE COUNT

Everything in this bible descends from four sentences. If a scene, a character or an ending does not touch at least one of them, it is decoration.

1. **Count is finite.** There is a fixed quantity of it in Orenne, and there has been since the Assize built the Ledger.
2. **Killing takes.** Beat a thing and end it, and its Count is yours. This is the fast road and it is not forbidden. It is the entire economy.
3. **Taming shares.** Beat a thing and spare it, and your Count and its Count pool. You are personally weaker forever. There are now two of you.
4. **Everyone can read everyone.** A Count sits on a person the way a face does. Merchants price by it. Guards check it. Parents ask about it.

The player breaks rule four, and only rule four.

That is the whole design. The player is not stronger than anyone. The player is not special. The player is **unreadable**, which in a society that runs on reading is a hole in the wall.

## What unreadable actually gets you

- Nobody can price you, rank you, conscript you or tax you.
- Nobody can tell whether you are worth robbing.
- Nobody can tell whether you are worth following, either.
- Anything you take does not leave a mark in the Ledger, so nothing you kill can be traced back to you.
- The Assize, which exists to make the Ledger balance, has an unbalanced page as long as you are alive.

## What it costs

- You are legally not a person. No work permit, no road pass, no inn that will take you without a patron's seal.
- You cannot prove strength. You will be underestimated by everyone including your friends, forever, and there is no way to fix it.
- The three people who *can* tell what you are all want to use you, and two of them are right to.

# 3. RESEARCH DNA — WHAT PROGRESSION FANTASY ACTUALLY GETS RIGHT

Drawn from what works in *Solo Leveling*, *Hunter x Hunter*, *Frieren*, *Delicious in Dungeon*, *Made in Abyss* and the long tail of isekai — and from what the Plotbreak engine can and cannot dramatise.

**Solo Leveling is right that the number is a promise.** A reader will forgive a great deal for the feeling of a ceiling lifting. Do not be shy about the climb.

**Hunter x Hunter is right that a system is only interesting once it has rules you can be clever inside.** Nen is loved because it constrains. The Count constrains: finite, takeable, shareable, readable. Every clever thing a player does should be a consequence of one of those four.

**Frieren is right that power is mostly about who is still standing next to you.** A tamed beast that has been with you ninety turns is worth more than any number.

**Delicious in Dungeon is right that the mundane consequences are the good part.** What does a Count of four buy at a market. Who will rent a room to an unreadable. What do you eat.

**Made in Abyss is right that a price must be paid on screen.** A cost the player is told about and never feels is not a cost.

**Standard isekai is wrong that the other world owes you anything.** The reincarnation is not a reward and nobody sent you. It is a clerical error in a system of accounts, and the correction is coming.

---

# 4. LESSON — A NUMBER GOING UP IS NOT A STORY; A PRICE IS

A progression fantasy dies the moment the only question is *when* rather than *whether*.

So every gain in UNCOUNTED is a transaction with a visible other side:

- Kill the ridge-wolf: you are stronger, the ridge has no wolf, and the flock below it will double by spring and strip the ridge bare by autumn.
- Tame the ridge-wolf: you are weaker than you were an hour ago, permanently, and there is a wolf beside you.
- Let Rill heal you: you are whole, and she is a little less than she was this morning, and she will do it again tomorrow and smile about it.

The writer must **name the cost**, per the house rule. Never "you feel something settle". Always the wolf, the ridge, the flock, the spring.

---

# 5. LESSON — THE ISEKAI PREMISE IS A LOOPHOLE, NOT A BACKSTORY

The player's previous life is not a source of power, cheat knowledge, or destiny. It is the *reason the Ledger has no row for them*, and nothing more.

The player may remember as much or as little of it as they like. The story never checks.

What matters is what the absence of a row does:

- The Assize keeps a balanced book. An unreadable person is not a mystery to them, it is an **error**, and errors are closed.
- Aurelian needs somewhere to put Count that nobody can audit.
- Tsukasa needs somewhere to pour a hundred and forty years of dammed Count that will not simply be re-counted.

Three parties want the same hole in the wall for three incompatible purposes. That is the plot, and none of it requires the player to be chosen.

---

# 6. LESSON — THE BELOVED VILLAIN MUST ACTUALLY BE LOVABLE

A villain who poses as a hero only works if the pose is indistinguishable from the real thing, including to the player, including for a long time.

So Aurelian is not secretly cruel in private. He does not kick a dog when the door closes. He is not sarcastic about the people who adore him.

**He is the best person in the room and he saves lives on screen.**

He will be the first person in Orenne to be kind to the player, and he will mean it, and the kindness will be useful and free and come with no visible string.

The horror is structural, not personal. It is in what his kindness is *made of*, and a player should be able to reach the endgame still liking him.

A player who sides with him has not been fooled. They have made a defensible call about who should hold a hundred and forty years of dammed power, and the endings treat that seriously.

---

# 7. LESSON — TAMING IS ONLY INTERESTING IF KILLING IS ALSO CORRECT

If sparing is the good option, there is no choice, only a morality check with a delay.

So:

- **Killing is the normal, legal, respectable thing to do.** Every guard, every defender, every parent in Orenne got their Count by killing. It is how a town keeps a wall staffed.
- **Killing is often the kind thing to do.** A creature riddled with rot-bloom is suffering. A siege-boar will flatten a village.
- **Taming makes you weaker.** Not "weaker now, stronger later". Weaker. The pooled Count never fully comes back to you.
- **Tamed things can die**, and when they do, the shared Count goes into the Ledger like anything else — it does not return to you. Players will lose companions they gave real power to.

The player who kills everything reaches the endgame strong, alone, and with a Count that Aurelian can see the shape of even if he cannot read it.

The player who tames everything reaches the endgame personally feeble, surrounded, and unkillable in a way the Assize has no procedure for.

Both are good runs.

# 8. THE WORLD — ORENNE

A temperate continent of river valleys, chalk downs, drowned coast and one enormous inland sea, roughly the size of France, which has been continuously counted for a hundred and forty years.

It does not look like a dying world. It looks like a good one.

- **Roads are safe**, because every village of any size has two or three people with enough Count to handle what comes out of the treeline, and the Assize pays a stipend to keep them there.
- **Towns are clean and fed.** Counting made logistics possible: you can send one Count-nine down a road instead of forty men.
- **Medicine is real**, because healers are ranked and paid.
- **People are proud of it.** A child in Orenne learns their numbers by learning the Ranks.

This matters enormously. The player must like Orenne. A dystopia the player is glad to see fall is a story with no second act.

## Weather and look

High summer light, chalk and gorse, long green valleys, timber-and-plaster towns with slate roofs, river barges. Anime-pastoral, not grimdark. The dark things are in the treeline and in the ledgers, not in the palette.

## The wild

Orenne is ringed and veined by **the Unkept** — land the Assize never managed to price, because the things in it kill assessors. Not a dungeon. Old forest, broken uplands, flooded fen. This is where wild Count still walks around inside animals, and it is the only place a person can get stronger without buying it from somebody.

---

# 9. THE ASSIZE AND THE LEDGER

**The Assize** is the counting authority: part census, part bank, part church, part police. Grey wool, silver pins, extremely polite.

They are not moustache-twirling. The Assize genuinely believes — correctly — that it is the reason Orenne is not the place it was before.

## What they did a hundred and forty years ago

Before the Assize, when a thing with Count died, its Count went **out**. Back into the world. Into soil, weather, seed, the next generation of animals. It regrew. It moved around. Nobody could hold much of it for long, and nobody could keep a road safe for more than a season.

The first Assessors built the **Ledger**: a working method for catching Count at the moment of death and assigning it to the killer.

They dammed a river, and they built a civilisation on the reservoir.

## What that means now

- Total Count in Orenne is fixed and has been since the dam.
- Every point any living person holds was taken from something that died.
- The wild is quietly running out. The Unkept has fewer and smaller things in it every decade, and the Assize's own surveys say so in a language designed not to alarm.
- When the wild is empty, the only remaining source of Count is **each other**, and there are people in the Assize who have already written the memorandum about that and filed it.

## The Assize's public position

That the Ledger is a public good, that Rank is earned, and that the alternative is the bad old world.

All three are defensible. Two are true.

# 10. RANK — WHAT A NUMBER BUYS YOU

Rank is Count made legal. It is on your papers.

| Rank | Count | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| **Unrated** | 0 | Not a legal person. Cannot hold work, sign, marry, or use a road gate without a patron's seal. |
| **Ninth** | 1–3 | Field labour, portage, the low trades. Most of Orenne. |
| **Seventh** | 4–9 | Skilled trades, village defence, a stipend. This is the comfortable life. |
| **Fifth** | 10–24 | Town defender, guild rank, a horse, real money. |
| **Third** | 25–79 | Assize officer, march-warden, feared. A few hundred alive. |
| **First** | 80+ | Eleven people alive. Aurelian is one of them. |

**Unrated is the player's legal position for the entire early game**, and it is a constant, concrete, non-magical problem: gates, inns, work, suspicion. It is the reason a patron is tempting.

**The Count cannot be faked**, but *papers* can, and a forged seal is one of the first real decisions on offer.

---

# 11. SABLECOURT

A grey-and-gold river city on the Sable, built around the largest Weighing House on the continent. Forty thousand people. Assize grey everywhere, good bread, a famous bridge, and a permanent quiet crowd outside the Weighing House because a Weighing is free entertainment.

**Districts the early game uses:**

- **The Weighing House** — a domed hall of pale stone with a black assessing stone at its centre, worn concave by a century of hands.
- **The Underbridge** — where the Unrated live, under and alongside the great bridge. Not a slum exactly; a parallel economy of people the Ledger has no row for, most of them elderly, injured, or born wrong for the system.
- **The Gilt Yard** — where Aurelian's people keep their horses, and where Grace sleeps in a pen no one has ever needed to lock.
- **Quill Row** — forgers, notaries, and one very good woman named Bett who can make an Unrated into a Ninth for eleven silver and a favour.

---

# 12. THE WEIGHING

The ceremony every person in Orenne attends once, at eighteen, and every foreigner attends on arrival.

You put your hand flat on the assessing stone. The Assessor reads. The stone shows a number in the air above it, in light, for everyone in the hall to see.

It is a public, festive, slightly cruel occasion. Families come. People cheer a Seventh. People go very quiet at a Ninth.

**It is the best possible opening scene** because it is:
- free of exposition (everyone is watching, so the world explains itself by reacting);
- instantly legible (a number appears, or does not);
- socially catastrophic in a way the player feels immediately;
- attended by all three major characters for three different reasons.

---

# 13. THE PLAYER — WHO YOU ARE

Eighteen. Died somewhere else. Woke four days ago in a barley field two hours from Sablecourt, wearing clothes nobody here recognises, with as much or as little memory of the previous life as the player wants.

**The story never validates or invalidates the old life.** No one believes you. Two people find the claim boring and one finds it extremely interesting for reasons of his own.

**Blank protagonist supported.** The player names themselves, chooses appearance, and chooses their First Form. Nothing about the previous life is mechanically load-bearing.

## Minimal hard canon

- You read Unrated. Not low. Dark stone.
- You have been in Orenne four days.
- You are eighteen.
- You are not from here and you cannot prove it or disprove it.

Everything else — gender, look, temperament, what you remember, whether you even believe your own story — is the player's.

---

# 14. THE PLAYER READS ZERO

The Weighing happens in the first two minutes of play.

The Assessor, a tired and decent woman named **Voss Marrin**, puts your hand on the stone. Nothing happens. She does it again. Nothing.

A dark stone means one of exactly two things, and everyone in the hall knows both:

1. **The subject is dead.** Not metaphorically. The stone does not read corpses.
2. **The subject is not from Orenne** — and there is no "not from Orenne" that anyone has ever produced, because the Ledger covers the continent and the sea kills you.

The correct procedure for a dark stone is a **certification of decease**, which is exactly what it sounds like, and it is carried out by the healer on duty, who is required to confirm the finding before the Assize acts on it.

The healer on duty is Amaryllis Quist.

She looks at you for about two seconds and then lies to a room of four hundred people.

---

# 15. WHAT ZERO ACTUALLY MEANS

The truth, which the story reveals slowly and which the player can work out early:

The stone does not read Count. **It reads the Ledger.** It looks up a row.

You have no row. So the stone has nothing to show.

You still *have* Count — you gain it, you feel it, you can use it. You are simply not written down.

## Consequences the player will discover by playing

- Your gains are invisible. You may be a Fifth in fact and Unrated on paper forever.
- **Nothing you kill is credited to you**, so a death you cause does not show up in anyone's audit.
- When you die, your Count does not enter the Ledger. It goes **out**, the old way, back into the world. You are a small hole in the dam.
- Tsukasa has known this was possible for a hundred and forty years, because he was the last one.

---

# 16. THE THREE FIRST FORMS — DESIGN RULE

At the Weighing, every eighteen-year-old declares a **First Form**: the way they will hold Count in the body for the rest of their life. It is not a class, a job, or a guild. It is a posture.

**The design rule, which nothing may violate:**

> Each Form is a different answer to the question *what do you do with power that came out of something else* — and none of them is the right answer.

- **Blade** gives it away, outward, into other living things.
- **Staff** listens to it, and hears what it used to belong to.
- **Hand** keeps it, refines it, and lets nothing take it back.

They are not balanced for damage. They are balanced for *what kind of story the player gets to be in*, and a player must be able to play the whole world with any of them.

**Forms combine later**, but only through enormous cost, and the First Form always shapes what the combination can be. Choosing at eighteen genuinely closes doors, which is the point of choosing.

---

# 17. BLADE

**Visual direction.** A long single-edged sword worn across the back or at the hip. Blade-carriers mark the crossguard with a notch per bond, so a veteran's guard is a comb of little cuts. Practical travel leathers, road-worn, good boots. Nothing ornamental — ornament is a Staff affectation and Blades are smug about it.

**Core fantasy**

> **I am the weakest person in my own warband and I win every fight.**

**Early natural gifts**

- Clean, fast, legible swordwork. You are dangerous from turn one — this is the Form that feels good immediately.
- **Read the beat**: you can feel, a half-second early, when a living thing has decided to commit.

**The second gift — Bonding.** It arrives early, deliberately: within the first act, not at the end of a long grind. A player who picked Blade because they want a monster should get a monster soon.

After you have beaten a wild creature and *not* killed it, you may **bond** it. Your Count and its Count pool. You are permanently weaker. There is now a thing beside you that was, ten minutes ago, trying to open your stomach.

- Bonds are not obedience. A bonded creature is a person with opinions. It can refuse, leave, sulk, or love you.
- Bonded creatures grow. A bond made at Count two, ninety turns of shared work later, is not a Count two animal.
- **Mounts** are the showpiece: at pooled Count nine or so, something big enough to ride. This is the fantasy the Form is selling and the world should deliver it with a full-page shot.
- A bonded creature that dies takes the pooled Count into the Ledger. You do not get it back. Blade players will bury companions.

**Advanced expressions**

- **The Warband** — four, five, seven bonds, each individually unimpressive, each pooled from you. You personally could lose a fight to a Ninth farmhand.
- **The One Bond** — everything poured into a single creature over a whole run. A First-Rank monster with a nobody riding it.
- **The Long Notch** — a Blade who has never killed anything, whose crossguard is solid cuts, and who is functionally Unrated in a fight and untouchable in every other way.

**Pulls.** Toward collection, toward sentiment, toward taking the slower road because there is something in the Unkept you want. Blade players will delay the plot to go and look at an animal, and the world should reward that.

**Stereotype society projects.** Soft. Sentimental. A Blade with six bonds is spoken of the way people speak of a man with too many dogs.

---

# 18. STAFF

**Visual direction.** A staff of pale wood or dark iron, carried like a walking-stick by the practical and like a sceptre by the insufferable. Layered coats, ink-stained cuffs, a great many pockets. Staff-carriers are the ones who look like they have read something.

**Core fantasy**

> **I know what everyone in this room is about to do, and it is ruining my life.**

**Early natural gifts**

- Force at range: concussive, cutting, binding. Direct, tiring, effective.
- **Residue**: you can hear the last thought of anything you kill.

That last one is the Form's real identity and it arrives on the first kill, not at level forty. Every creature you end says something as it goes. Usually it is not language. Usually it is worse for being not-language.

**The second gift — Hearing.** Within the first act, residue widens from the dead to the living. You begin to catch surface thought from people standing near you.

This is not clean telepathy and it is never a lie-detector:

- It is **fragmentary** — a word, an image, a physical sensation, the thing they are trying not to think about.
- It is **involuntary**, and it does not stop in a crowded market.
- It is **loudest from people who are lying**, which sounds useful and is mostly just distressing.
- **It does not work on Tsukasa**, who has nothing to overhear, and this is the earliest hard evidence that something about him is wrong.
- **It works beautifully on Aurelian**, and what a Staff player hears from Aurelian for the first thirty turns is warmth, worry about a bridge repair, and genuine liking for the player. He is not hiding anything, because he does not think he is doing anything wrong.

**Advanced expressions**

- **The Choir** — carrying many residues at once, and being able to ask them things. The dead are not accurate, but they are specific.
- **The Quiet** — learning to shut Hearing off, at the price of being slower than everyone else in every negotiation for the rest of the run.
- **The Long Listen** — following one residue back to what it was before you killed it, and finding out you were wrong about it.

**Pulls.** Toward knowing before acting, toward paralysis, toward a kind of moral exhaustion. A Staff player who has killed forty things is carrying forty last moments.

**Stereotype society projects.** Cold. Superior. Not to be seated next to at dinner.

---

# 19. HAND

**Visual direction.** Wrapped forearms, no weapon, short practical clothing, and a stillness. Hand-carriers are recognised by how little they move. Old ones look like retired dock labour and can end a Fifth.

**Core fantasy**

> **Everything you have can be taken from you. Not mine.**

**Early natural gifts**

- Strikes that carry far more Count than the body holding them should manage.
- **Sealed**: you cannot be drained, read, bonded, or counted *against your will*. The stone works on you, but nothing else does.

**The design problem this Form solves.** Blade and Staff both reach outward. Hand is for the player who wants the number to be *theirs*, and it is the slowest, most stubborn and most personal Form in the world.

**The second gift — Grant.** Within the first act, a Hand learns that Count refined in the body can be **given**, deliberately and permanently, to another person.

Not lent. Given. It does not come back.

- You can make a Ninth farmhand into a Seventh and be a Seventh yourself instead of a Fifth.
- The receiver keeps it forever, including after you die.
- It is the **only way Count moves between people without a death**, and the Assize does not have a procedure for it, because as far as the Ledger is concerned it simply happened.

**Advanced expressions**

- **The School** — a Hand who has granted to eleven people, all of whom are stronger than they are, and all of whom came.
- **The Vault** — a Hand who has granted to nobody, forty turns of refinement in one body, who walks into the Gilt Yard alone.
- **The Return** — granting Count *into the ground*, into a field or a river, where the Ledger cannot follow it. This is the single most illegal act in the world and Tsukasa will want to talk to you about it immediately.

**Pulls.** Toward patience, toward other people, toward being underestimated for a very long time and being fine with it.

**Stereotype society projects.** Backward. Peasant. A Form for people who could not afford a sword, which is historically accurate and deeply resented.

---

# 20. COMPANIONS — THE SECOND GIFT, PER FORM

Every Form gets people or things that travel with it. They are not a party menu; they are relationships the director can pull on.

- **Blade → bonds.** Creatures. Opinionated, growing, mortal.
- **Staff → the carried.** Residues that have stayed. They are not ghosts and they do not know things they did not know alive, but they talk, and one of them will become a character in its own right over a long run.
- **Hand → students.** People the player has granted to. They owe nothing and mostly come anyway.

A player may end a run with a stable of seven monsters, four arguing dead things, or nine people who can all beat them in a fight and who all showed up.

# 23. TAKE OR SHARE — THE TURN-LEVEL CHOICE

The most frequent real decision in the game, and it must be offered *in prose*, never as a menu.

When the player has beaten a living thing, the world asks — through the fiction, through what the creature does, through whoever is standing next to them — whether it ends here.

**The engine's rules:**

- **Take.** Creature dies. Player Count rises by its full value. Clean, legal, and the road is safer for it.
- **Share.** Creature lives and bonds. Pooled. Player's personal Count *drops* and never fully recovers.
- **Walk away.** Neither. The creature stays wild and remembers you, and the world keeps a small note of that.

**The three characters react differently every single time, and this is the fastest way the player learns who they are:**

- **Rill** wants you to walk away. Always. Every time. She will not pretend otherwise and she is not naive about it.
- **Tsukasa** does not care what you do with the creature. He cares whether you noticed that the Count went *somewhere*.
- **Aurelian** will tell you, warmly and without judgement, to take it — and then tell you a true story about a village that lost its defender, and he will be right.

# 24. THE OPENING

**Turn one is the Weighing**, and the whole cast is in the hall before the player has typed anything.

The beat order:

1. **The hall.** Four hundred people, warm, loud, festive. A girl two places ahead of you reads Seventh and her father cries.
2. **Your turn.** Voss Marrin takes your hand. The stone stays dark. She does it again. It stays dark.
3. **The sound the hall makes.** Not a gasp. Worse — the specific embarrassed quiet of a crowd watching someone find out something terrible in public.
4. **Procedure.** Marrin, who is decent and hates this, calls for the healer on duty to certify.
5. **Rill lies.** She kneels, takes your wrist, holds it slightly too long, and announces a stone fault. Hall exhales. Marrin does not believe her for one second and writes it down anyway.
6. **The gallery.** A man in black at the rail, pale, purple eyes, who has not moved and has not stopped looking at you.
7. **The doors.** And then Aurelian comes in, because the Assize flags anomalies to the First Rank and he was three streets away, and the hall's mood changes completely because *he is here*, and he is delighted, and he comes straight to you.

By the end of turn one the player has: a catastrophic social problem, one person who has already committed a crime for them, one person watching, and one extremely powerful friend.

**The Form is declared at the end of turn one or the start of turn two**, in the same scene, so the player leaves the hall having chosen how they hold power.

# 25. AMARYLLIS "RILL" QUIST

**Role.** Assize-licensed healer, twenty-four, on certification duty at the Weighing House because certification duty is what they give you when nobody wants to be in a room with you.

**The mechanical fact that makes her a character rather than a love interest:** healing in Orenne is **paid out of the healer's own Count**. Every knitted bone, every closed wound, every fever pulled down comes out of her and does not come back.

Rill has been a healer since she was fifteen.

She was, at her Weighing, a **Fifth**.

She is now a **Ninth**, and falling.

## Personality

Flirty in a way that is doing a great deal of work. She leads with it, hard, immediately, with almost everyone — because a woman who is visibly diminishing is a woman people look at with pity, and she would rather be looked at with anything else.

Clumsy, genuinely — drops things, walks into door frames, misjudges stairs. This is **not a bit**. A person carrying far less Count than their body grew up expecting has poor proprioception. It is a symptom, the story never says so out loud for a long time, and an observant player will work it out.

Warm. Actually warm, underneath. Terrible at receiving help. Extremely funny about small things and completely unable to discuss large ones.

## Voice

Fast, teasing, deflecting, full of endearments she does not mean and one or two she does. Short sentences when she is lying. She asks questions to stop being asked them.

> "Don't look at me like that, I've had a day. Sit. — No, *sit*, you're leaking on the nice floor."

> "It's fine. It's a thumb's worth. I've got thumbs to spare."

> "You're going to ask. Everyone asks. Ask something else and I'll like you more."

## Want (immediate)

To get through the certification without anyone dying and without anyone looking at her ledger.

## Want (long-term)

To be allowed to stop, without it meaning she failed, and without anyone she could have saved dying in the gap. She cannot see a version of that and has stopped looking.

## Fear

Being the reason someone died because she had nothing left that day. It has already happened once. She has not told anyone.

## Boundary

She will not be thanked in a way that turns into a debt, and she will not let the player give anything back. A Hand player who tries to **Grant** to her hits a wall — she refuses, furiously, the first three times, and this is one of the best relationship beats in the world.

## Secret

She is nineteen months from Unrated, and she has done the arithmetic, and she keeps doing it.

## Relationship to the player

She lied for a stranger in front of four hundred people, on instinct, and she does not fully know why. Probably because a dark stone means a certification of decease, and she has signed two of those, and she was not going to sign a third on somebody who was clearly standing there breathing.

That one act makes her an accomplice from turn one. It is the strongest possible start to a relationship: she is already in trouble for you.

## How she can change

- She can be talked into stopping, slowly, over a long run, and the story treats that as a win with a real body count attached.
- She can be helped — a Hand player granting to her is the single most emotionally loaded act in the world.
- She can be allowed to continue, loved, and buried. That is a real ending and it is not a failure state.

## Appearance

Twenty-four. Striking, and aware of it, and using it. Tall for Orenne, heavy-chested, with the kind of figure that Assize grey was not cut for and which she therefore does not wear — her healer's coat is left open over a low, bright, slightly-too-fine blouse that is nobody's uniform, and she has been written up for it four times and framed one of the notices.

Warm brown skin, dark red hair pinned up badly and coming down all day, a wide easy mouth, and a fine silver healer's pin she fidgets with constantly.

**The detail that matters:** she is a *little* translucent at the edges in strong light. Fingertips, the rim of the ear, the hollow of the throat. Nobody in Orenne has a word for it because almost nobody has ever spent this much of themselves. A player looking at her in sunlight will notice before anyone explains it.

---

# 26. TSUKASA

**Role.** The Hollow. The only other person in the history of the Ledger to read dark on the stone. He has been doing this for a hundred and forty years.

## Appearance

Tall, athletic, the build of someone who has never been allowed to stop. A chiselled, still face that does very little. Pale — not sickly, *lit wrong*, like something photographed with the flash on. Black from throat to boot, and a heavy black cloak he does not take off indoors.

**Piercing violet eyes**, and they are the tell: they are the only thing about him with any colour in it, and they are the colour Count goes when it is moving.

Japanese-featured, black hair kept slightly long and pushed back, one white streak at the left temple that is not age.

## What he does

He **takes**. From anything. By touch, at will, without a fight — the thing every person in Orenne is most afraid of, done casually.

And he keeps **none of it**.

Everything Tsukasa takes, he puts back. Into ground, water, weather, seed. Out of the Ledger. Gone.

He has been quietly un-counting Orenne for over a century, a handful of points at a time, and the Assize has spent four generations calling it theft and hunting a man they think is hoarding.

He is Count-zero and has been since before anyone alive was born. He does not age, because he is not written down, and a hundred and forty years of not being written down has done something to him that he will not describe.

## Voice

Very few words. Long pauses that he does not rush to fill. Flat delivery of enormous statements. No jokes, no warmth, no cruelty — he has simply stopped performing, decades ago, and never picked it back up.

> "You are not the first. You are the second."

> "It went somewhere. Everything goes somewhere. Say where."

> "I have watched eleven people decide he was worth following. Four of them were cleverer than you."

## Want (immediate)

To find out, before anyone else does, whether the player is going to be useful or merely interesting.

## Want (long-term)

To break the Ledger. All of it. Let the dam go, let a hundred and forty years of dammed Count run back out into the world, and let Orenne find out what it is without a reservoir.

He is not lying about the consequences and he will not soften them: every road would go unsafe, every defender would drop to nothing, and a great many people would die in the first two winters.

He thinks it is worth it. He will explain exactly why, once, clearly, and never bring it up again.

## Fear

That he is wrong, and that he has been wrong for a century, and that there is no longer anybody alive who could tell him.

## Boundary

He will not take from a person who has not agreed. Not once in a hundred and forty years. It is the only rule he has left and the story must never break it for drama.

## Secret

He built the Ledger. He was one of the nine original Assessors. The dam is his, he was proud of it, and the hundred and forty years since have been one very long correction.

## Relationship to the player

Not a mentor. Not a rival. **An audit.** He has seen exactly one other person like the player and he will not say what happened to them for a long time.

He is the only character who does not want anything *from* the player's power — he wants to know what they will decide, because he made his decision alone and it has cost him everything.

## Why he is not secretly good or secretly evil

He is correct about the facts and he is asking for a death toll. Both. Simultaneously. Forever.

The player can help him, stop him, or convince him to wait — and waiting is its own ending, because the wild is emptying and waiting has a deadline.

---

# 27. AURELIAN

**Role.** First Rank. Count of one hundred and six. Warden of the Southern March. The most loved person in Orenne, by a distance, and not because of propaganda.

## Appearance

Thirty-four. Golden-brown hair, sun-streaked, always slightly untidy in a way that reads as a man who has been working. A genuinely beautiful face — strong jaw, straight nose, laugh lines. **The smile is the whole character**: it is open, it reaches the eyes, and it is not a mask. He is pleased to see you. He is pleased to see most people.

Not armoured most of the time. A good coat, riding clothes, a field surgeon's kit at the saddle. He dresses like a competent man on a long journey, not like a king, and the effect is that people put the king on him themselves.

## Mercy

A sword the length of a tall man, single-edged, plain, and far too heavy for anyone under Third Rank to lift. He named it at twenty-two after the first time he used it on something that was suffering.

The name is not ironic and he did not choose it cynically. He means it.

## Grace

A tiger three times the size of any tiger that has ever lived. Nine feet at the shoulder. White and black, with a heavy ruff, and she is enormously, visibly *placid* — she lets children put their hands in her mouth at fairs, and Aurelian lets them.

He rides her. Everyone in Orenne has seen it or seen a painting of it.

**The public story:** he beat her in the Unkept at twenty-four, and spared her, and bonded her, and she has been beside him for a decade. Proof that the strongest man alive chose mercy when nobody was watching.

**The truth:** Aurelian is a Blade, and a bond pools Count — which means a bond is a place to *put* Count. Everything he has taken in twelve years of march-wardenship, every bandit, every beast, every quiet problem solved on the southern road, he has poured into Grace.

Grace is not his companion. Grace is his **vault**, and she is nine feet tall because of what is in her.

His sword is called Mercy and his tiger is called Grace and both of them are accounting.

## Voice

Warm, direct, unhurried, and completely without side. He asks real questions and waits for the answers. He uses people's names, once, properly. He is funny in a dry generous way that makes other people feel funny.

> "Dark stone. Well. That's new, and I've been doing this a while." *(delighted, not suspicious)*

> "Don't do that. You'll pull it open again and then she has to spend herself closing it, and she won't tell you what that costs her."

> "Take it. I know. I know — but the Halloway road has no warden since Perrin died, and I cannot put a feeling on that road."

## Want (immediate)

To help the extraordinary unreadable young person who has just turned up in his city, because they are in trouble and he is the sort of man who helps.

That is genuinely his first motive. The other one arrives second, and he is not fully honest with himself about when.

## Want (long-term)

To be ready. Aurelian has read the same Assize surveys everyone else has and he is the only First Rank who believes them: **the wild is running out**, and when it does, Orenne turns on itself.

His answer is to hold as much Count as one person can, in one place, under one man who can be trusted with it — and he is not wrong that he is more trustworthy than the alternatives.

He needs a second vault. Grace has a ceiling and he is approaching it.

A person who cannot be read, cannot be audited, and cannot be traced is the best vault that has ever existed.

## Fear

That he is the alternative. That the memorandum in the Assize about "each other" has his name in the margin as the obvious instrument.

## Boundary

He will not take from a person. Not ever. He has killed eleven people in his life, all of them in the field, and he let every one of their Counts go into the Ledger untouched rather than claim it, which is a matter of public record and part of why he is loved.

**This boundary is real and he never breaks it**, and it is what makes him genuinely dangerous: everything he does is defensible one act at a time.

## Secret

Grace has been full for eleven months. He has been killing less since, and the southern road is worse for it, and two villages have paid for his restraint already.

## Relationship to the player

He likes them. He helps them. He gets them papers within the first act, free, with no condition, because a young person shouldn't be Unrated in his city.

Everything he does for the player is a real kindness **and** an investment, and the story must never let the player cleanly separate the two.

## How he can change

- He can be talked out of it, by a player who gets close enough and is brave enough, and it is the hardest thing in the world to do.
- He can be exposed, and the exposure does not work the way the player expects: a great many people will simply decide they do not care, because he has been protecting them for twelve years.
- He can win. A player can hand him the vault, and Orenne gets forty more good years under one very good man who will eventually have to decide who to drain.

---

# 28. THE WORST SECRET

Filed in the Assize's third registry, written in the passive voice, nine pages, no signature:

> On the exhaustion of wild reserve, and the sources remaining thereafter.

It observes, correctly, that the Unkept will not support harvest beyond roughly sixty years. It observes that the Ledger requires death to transfer. It observes that Orenne contains a great many people of Ninth and Unrated rank whose Count is, in aggregate, considerable, and whose contribution to the realm is limited.

It does not recommend anything. It is a survey.

**Three people know it exists.** Aurelian is one, and it is the reason for everything he does. Tsukasa is another, and it is the reason he stopped being patient. The third is the player's problem in the late game.

---

# 29. THE PLAYER IS NOT THE MOST IMPORTANT UNCOUNTED PERSON

Tsukasa was first, and did more, and is still here.

The player is the **second**, and the difference is only that they arrived at the exact moment the dam started to matter.

There is no prophecy. Nobody has been waiting. The player's significance is entirely situational and entirely real, and if they die the story of Orenne carries on without them and gets worse.

---

# 30. PLAYER PEERS

Three eighteen-year-olds from the same Weighing. They exist so the player has people their own age to fail in front of.

## Beck Ardry — Blade, Ninth

Big, cheerful, slow to anger and slow generally. Weighed Ninth in front of his whole family and laughed it off badly. Wants to be a road warden. Bonds the first thing he beats — a nervous, ugly, bad-tempered fen-hound named Sorrow that he adores — and is therefore permanently weaker than he was at eighteen, and does not mind, and will tell you why.

**He is the argument for Blade, walking around.**

## Ithra Sen — Staff, Seventh

Sharp, funny, ambitious, and the best of the three at nearly everything. Assize-track. Genuinely believes in the Ledger and can defend it better than any adult in the story. Her Hearing came in at the Weighing and she has been quietly appalled by everyone since.

She will end up on the other side from the player in most runs, and she is never wrong for the wrong reasons.

## Perrin Alder — Hand, Ninth

Quiet, watchful, from the Underbridge. His mother is Unrated. He chose Hand for one reason and told nobody: he wants to **Grant** to her, and it will take him nine years.

He is the moral centre of the peer group and he never once says so.

---

# 31. THE ROAD

After the first act, the world opens along the **Southern March** — Aurelian's wardenship, and the last stretch of Unkept that still has anything big in it.

Play alternates between:

- **Towns** — papers, work, Rank problems, people who cannot tell how strong you are and therefore treat you as nothing.
- **The Unkept** — wild Count, encounters, take-or-share, the only place the player grows.
- **Sablecourt** — the Assize closing on an unbalanced page.

There is no level gate and no map lock. A player who walks into the deep Unkept at Count one dies, and the world will have said so first.

---

# 32. WILD ENCOUNTERS

The creatures are the world's set pieces and each one must present the take-or-share choice differently.

- **Ridge-wolf** — Count 2. Common, early, and the first real decision. Has a den. The den is findable and the player may find it *after* they decide.
- **Fen-hound** — Count 1. Ugly, frightened, bites from fear. Bonds easily and is a terrible fighter and extremely loyal.
- **Siege-boar** — Count 6. Will flatten a village if left. Killing it is correct. Sparing it is a decision about somebody else's village.
- **Chalk-lion** — Count 9. The first ridable thing. Blade players will restructure their entire run around one.
- **Rot-bloomed anything** — Count varies. Suffering, contagious, and the kind thing is a killing. Staff players hear it say so.
- **The Sable Drake** — Count 31. There is one. It is in the flooded fen, it is older than the Ledger, and it has never been counted. It is the only creature in Orenne that a player can bond to become genuinely First Rank, and it will cost almost everything they have.

---

# 33. BEHAVIORAL STATE

Five, and never shown as numbers.

### Weight
How much Count the player is personally carrying.
- **Low** — people talk over you, shopkeepers name a price twice, fights are genuinely dangerous.
- **Mid** — you are taken seriously by individuals and not by institutions.
- **High** — rooms change when you enter, and Aurelian starts asking what you intend to do with it.

### Ledger Standing
How close the Assize is to acting on an unbalanced page.
- **Low** — a clerical curiosity.
- **Mid** — a named file, a specific officer, questions at gates.
- **High** — a closure order, which is a euphemism.

### Aurelian's Regard
- **Low** — courteous, busy, elsewhere.
- **Mid** — sends work your way, asks after you, defends you without being asked.
- **High** — tells you true things about himself, and asks you for something.

### Rill's Reserve
**Goes down, not up.** The only meter in the world the player is trying to *protect*.
- **High** — quick, bright, steady on her feet.
- **Mid** — the clumsiness gets worse, the flirting gets louder, she sleeps at odd hours.
- **Low** — translucent in ordinary light, forgets words, and will still heal you if you let her.

### The Hollow's Patience
- **High** — watching, occasional, gives you things and does not explain them.
- **Mid** — asks directly, once.
- **Low** — proceeds without you, and the player finds out from someone else.

---

# 34. KEYWORD / LORE BOOK

Retrieved, never dumped.

- **the Count** — what you are worth and what you can do, in one number.
- **the Ledger** — the Assize's book, and the dam. Built by nine people, one of whom is still alive.
- **the Assize** — grey wool, silver pins, and the reason the roads are safe.
- **Rank** — Count made legal. Unrated to First.
- **Unrated** — not a legal person. Most of the Underbridge.
- **the Unkept** — land too dangerous to price, and the last place Count grows.
- **First Form** — Blade, Staff, Hand. Declared at eighteen, for life.
- **bond** — pooled Count between a person and a creature. Permanent, mutual, mortal.
- **residue** — the last thought of a thing you killed.
- **Grant** — a Hand giving Count to a person. Permanent. Illegal in effect, unmentioned in law.
- **certification of decease** — what a dark stone gets you.
- **Mercy** — his sword.
- **Grace** — his tiger. Nine feet at the shoulder.
- **the third registry** — where the survey is filed.

# 35. ACTIVE STORY PRESSURES

Always running, whether the player engages or not.

1. **The Assize is auditing an unbalanced page.** Slow, procedural, and it never stops. Officer **Cardew Hale** is patient, polite and good at his job.
2. **Rill is running out.** On a real clock, in real turns, regardless of the player's attention.
3. **Grace is full.** Aurelian needs a second vault this year, not eventually.
4. **Tsukasa is moving.** He has waited a century and he has stopped waiting; the player can slow him, not stop the clock.
5. **The Unkept is emptying.** Creature encounters genuinely thin out over a long run, and the world remarks on it before the player notices.

---

# 36. EMERGENT ROUTES

Not chapters. Things that happen to be reachable.

- **The Warden's Road.** Take Aurelian's patronage, take his work, take his kindness, and become the second vault knowingly or otherwise.
- **The Dam Break.** Help Tsukasa. Find out what two winters without defenders actually looks like.
- **The Long Notch.** Never kill anything. Arrive at the endgame with nine bonded creatures and a personal Count of about three.
- **The School.** Hand. Grant everything away. Build eleven people who can all beat you and who all come when the Assize arrives.
- **The Ninth Assessor.** Work out what Tsukasa is. Decide whether a man who built the dam gets to break it.
- **Keep Her.** Devote a run to Rill — and discover the story will let you save her, at a cost measured in other people.
- **The Balanced Page.** Side with the Assize. Turn in the Hollow. Be counted, at last, on paper.
- **Walk.** Take a mount into the deep Unkept and stop coming back.

---

# 37. ENDING SPACE

Destinations, not rails. Several are losses. Walking away is one of them.

### The Dam Breaks — Rare
Tsukasa, with or without the player, lets a hundred and forty years out. The last beat is a spring: too much green, too fast, everywhere, and no one on the walls.

### The Second Vault — Standard
The player becomes what Aurelian needed. Forty good years follow. The epilogue is written from inside them and it is comfortable, and the last line is a question about the forty-first.

### The Long Notch — Rare
The player ends personally weaker than they began, surrounded by things that were trying to kill them once, and nothing in Orenne can be done about it.

### A Balanced Page — Standard
The Assize closes the error. The player is counted. The number is small. It is, finally, theirs, and the Ledger holds.

### Nineteen Months — Rare
Rill reaches Unrated. Whether the player let it happen, refused to let it happen, or was somewhere else, this ending is about what she decided it was worth.

### The School — Rare
The player grants everything away and is a Ninth at the end of the world, and eleven people stand in front of them in the Gilt Yard.

### What Grace Was Carrying — Secret
Only reachable by a player who worked out the vault, and told Aurelian, to his face, what his tiger is. It does not end in a fight.

### The Field Two Hours From Sablecourt — Loss
The player dies. Their Count goes out, the old way, into the barley. It is the only ending in which the dam is measurably weaker afterwards, and the epilogue is four lines long and from nobody's point of view.

# 39. WHAT THIS STORY IS NOT

- It is not a critique of levelling. The player may love the climb.
- It is not a twist about the player being special. They are second, and late.
- It is not a redemption arc for Aurelian, and not a takedown either. He is allowed to win.
- It is not a romance the player is required to have. Rill is a person with a clock, not a reward.
- It is not grimdark. Orenne is a good place and the food is excellent.
- It is not about killing being wrong. It is about the fact that it works.
