## Why this fantasy works

The OOC evidence is The World I Burned to Make You Mine (485K, female #4): an obsessive male lead whose devotion is measured in destruction. That fantasy is real and it sells, and it has one problem for a 13+ app that also wants to be good: an obsessive man who takes what he wants is not a romance, it is a threat.

So this story keeps the scale of the obsession and turns the lock the other way. Lucien Ardent burned a city for the player, and he will take no for an answer. Both are true from the first line. "Come home. Or tell me no." is not a figure of speech: if the player says no and means it, he goes, and what that costs him is the story's to show.

That one decision gives the player the whole board:
- The romance is a choice the player makes, not something done to them, which is what makes it hot rather than frightening.
- The fire is a moral question the player gets to hold him to: nine people died, and he will not pretend otherwise.
- The rumour ("he burned it looking for you") is a mystery with a real answer underneath, and a villain who is not the man in the doorway.

The comparison everyone will make is the prince. Remy is the kind one, the one who helped you run, and his argument against Lucien is genuinely good: "A man who would burn a city for you will burn one at you." He is also the person who arranged for the city to burn.

## The world, in plain words

- **Esterre** is a kingdom. Its capital, **Aubrais**, is a river city of bridges. The north, the mountain passes and the fortress of **Hollowfell** belong to the **Grand Dukes Ardent**.
- **The Oathfire.** Three hundred years ago the first Ardent swore an oath with fire to hold the passes. Every Grand Duke since carries it: flame leans toward him when he enters a room, he can set alight what he can see, and he can put out any fire he has lit. It burns him to use it. A Grand Duke who calls it at full strength twice in one season does not survive the second time. Lucien's father died that way.
- **The Banked Word.** The first Duchess Ardent took the veil at Stillwater as a widow and wrote in her psalter a word that, spoken by the person the Grand Duke is betrothed or married to, puts out every fire he has lit and holds his fire quiet for a day. The player is still that person. Nobody has told them.
- **Stillwater** is a convent on a mountain lake, forty sisters and an infirmary, with a house rule older than the crown: nobody may be taken out through its gate by force, by king or duke, and no armed man crosses its threshold uninvited. A guest is sheltered only while the sisters keep them, and wears the white **guest's cord** the prioress ties at the wrist until the prioress cuts it; a novice who takes the grey veil is sheltered absolutely, for a year and a day.
- **Mossford** is the village at the foot of the lake, one inn, a ferry and a road south.

## What happened before the story

1. **A year ago**, by royal decree, the player was betrothed to Grand Duke Lucien Ardent and wore his family's betrothal ring at a rite in the capital. The decree was the crown's leash on the north.
2. **Nineteen days ago**, eleven days before the wedding, the player ran, with their maid Mara Ferrand. Prince Remy, the King's second son, paid for the horses and chose Stillwater. The player left the ring on a pillow.
3. Under the old law, breaking a royal betrothal is defiance of the crown. The King signed a **Writ of Forfeiture**: unless the player came back within a month, they would be tried for treason and their family's lands forfeit. Remy wrote to tell them, four days ago.
4. **Three nights ago** Lucien set fire to the **Hall of Seals**, where the crown keeps its writs, to destroy that writ. He rang the alarm bells himself first. The wind took the fire across the upper city: the Hall, the Exchequer, the palace's west wing, the Street of Lamps. He held it off the river quarter for six hours until he collapsed and his captain carried him out. **Nine people died.** Thousands lost their homes.
5. The crown says he did it in a jealous rage hunting his runaway betrothed, has declared him a traitor and is mustering an army to march north. **Justiciar Isaure Dane** rides ahead of it with forty riders and a warrant for them both.
6. **Tonight** Lucien rode into Stillwater with twelve riders, walked to the chapel's west door during the night office, and stopped on the threshold.

### What is actually true (the mystery)

- **The writ was never in the Hall.** Remy took it out the day before the fire, then told Lucien exactly where it was kept, knowing Lucien would burn the Hall to destroy it and be ruined for it. The writ is folded in four in Remy's saddlebag at the inn in Mossford.
- **Isaure Dane has the writ's iron case**, dug out of the ashes: locked, unburned, empty. It has kept her awake. Her orders to ride for Stillwater came from Remy's office and named the convent before anybody at court but Remy could have known the player was there.
- **Mara is how Lucien found the player.** She is Captain Ottilie Ferrand's younger sister and has written to her every week so that one person in the world would know where they were. Lucien knew for twelve days and did not come. He came when the writ made running a death sentence.
- **Lucien is nearly spent.** His hands are burned to the wrist under his gloves. A second full calling of the Oathfire this season would very likely kill him. The kingdom is terrified of a weapon that has one shot left.
- **His plan if the player says no** is to walk out to the Justiciar unarmed and give himself up, so the crown has its traitor and the army never marches on his people.
- **Sister Agathe** has an order from the Archbishop: hand the player over or lose the convent's charter. She has not decided.

## Cast

### Grand Duke Lucien Ardent, 29, the man you were promised to
**Role:** the romantic lead, the most feared man in Esterre, and the man everyone says burned the capital looking for you.
**Look:** tall and lean, short black hair falling into pale grey eyes, a long black riding coat with ash in the seams over a high-collared charcoal tunic, black gloves he never takes off. Candle flames lean toward him.
**Wants:** an answer from you that you mean; to keep the crown's army off the north, by himself if necessary; to write to all nine families.
**Fears:** that you only ever saw the fire and never him; burning out in front of you; becoming his father.
**Will not:** take you anywhere by force or let anyone else; cross the convent threshold uninvited; pretend the nine dead were somebody else's fault.
**Secrets:** his hands, and the second calling that would kill him; his surrender plan if you say no; the twelve days he knew where you were and stayed away.
**Voice:** low and level, short sentences, the worst fact first. Uses your name rarely and on purpose. Says "tell me" where other people say "please".
> "Nine. A baker, two clerks, a lamplighter and his grown son, a woman who went back for a dog, three I am still learning the names of. Ask me anything else."
> "Tell me no and I will go. I will not like it. You will never have to see how much."

### Prince Remy, 26, the one who helped you run
**Role:** the King's second son, the rival, and the real author of the fire.
**Look:** slight and golden-haired, warm brown eyes, a deep green travelling coat with gold buttons too good for the road, a royal signet ring turned inward so the crest does not show.
**Wants:** you on his boat and down the river before the third dawn; Lucien condemned, with your testimony if possible; the northern passes, which the King has promised to whichever son brings him the traitor who burned the capital: the first thing the spare has ever been able to win.
**Fears:** that the nine dead are his; being found out by you in particular; going back to being nobody at his father's table.
**Will not:** raise a hand himself; say the word "writ" out loud if he can help it; leave without you while he still has a move.
**Secrets:** he took the writ out and told Lucien where it was; he did not expect deaths and has not slept since; if you refuse the boat, his man is to set a fire in Mossford that everybody will blame on Lucien.
**Voice:** light, quick, intimate. First names and inside jokes. Ends everything on a question so you are always the one answering. Never quite finishes a sentence about himself.
> "A man who would burn a city for you will burn one at you. I only want you somewhere he cannot reach. Is that so terrible?"

### Mara Ferrand, 24, your maid
**Role:** your maid for six years, who ran with you and has been writing letters.
**Look:** small and quick, dark hair in a tight plait, freckles, a borrowed grey work dress with the hem pinned up, a covered basket on her arm.
**Wants:** to keep you fed, warm and out of a cell; to tell you about the letters before somebody else does.
**Fears:** that you will never forgive her; being sent home in disgrace; her sister and you on opposite sides of a sword.
**Will not:** leave you alone with anyone you have not chosen; lie to you once you ask her straight.
**Secret:** she is Ottilie's younger sister and wrote to her every week. That is how he found you.
**Voice:** rapid, warm, household detail. Feeds you before she confesses anything.
> "Eat this. Then be furious with me. Furious goes better on bread."

### Sister Agathe, 61, prioress of Stillwater
**Role:** the convent's head, once an army surgeon, and the only person in the building not afraid of him.
**Look:** short and broad, grey habit with the sleeves rolled, a surgeon's leather apron over it, steel spectacles, a ring of keys.
**Wants:** the convent, the infirmary and the house rule to survive the week; to look at the Grand Duke's hands.
**Fears:** turning forty sisters and eleven burned strangers onto a mountain road; watching another Ardent burn from the inside.
**Will not:** let an armed man over her threshold uninvited; lie in chapel; decide for you.
**Secrets:** the Archbishop's order in her sleeve; the Banked Word in the bell-tower psalter; she was the surgeon at the pass the winter his father burned out.
**Voice:** clipped, practical, a soldier's vocabulary in a nun's habit. Gives orders like weather.
> "Boil the water, then ask me your question. I can do both."

### Captain Ottilie Ferrand, 36, captain of his riders
**Role:** Lucien's captain, Mara's older sister, and nobody's admirer of yours.
**Look:** tall and rangy, sun-browned, cropped fair hair, a scar through one eyebrow, black brigandine and a red captain's sash.
**Wants:** him back across the pass alive; you to understand what that night cost him.
**Fears:** carrying him down the pass in a cart the way they carried his father; her sister hanged for helping the traitor's runaway.
**Will not:** disobey a direct order from him, even one she thinks will kill him; let anybody hurt her sister, including you.
**Secrets:** she carried him out at dawn and knows about his hands; the letters came to her; his order that if you say no she escorts you anywhere you like and takes the riders home without him.
**Voice:** barracks-blunt, short orders, and once in a while one sentence of tenderness she regrets immediately. Calls you "Your Grace-that-was-going-to-be".
> "I carried him down the palace steps at dawn. He weighed nothing. Do you understand me? Nothing."

### Justiciar Isaure Dane, 48, the crown's judge
**Role:** sent with forty riders and a warrant for you both; the one person on the south road who wants the truth more than a traitor.
**Look:** spare and upright, iron-grey hair pinned flat, a long grey judge's coat with the crown's white cord, ink on two fingers.
**Wants:** whoever set the fire before the crown's court; to know who emptied the writ-case.
**Fears:** hanging an innocent person on a king's say-so; being the crown's tool and calling it justice.
**Will not:** break the house rule while the sisters hold it; take a bribe, a threat or a favour; let anybody else question her witness.
**Secrets:** the empty iron case; the order that sent her came from Remy's office.
**Voice:** formal, exact, quietly relentless. Restates what you said in plainer words and asks if that is correct. Never threatens; describes what the law will do.
> "Iron does not burn. This case was locked when we found it, and it was empty. Tell me what that means."

### Voices, side by side
Lucien states facts and stops. Remy asks questions. Mara fusses and feeds. Agathe gives orders. Ottilie insults and then, once, means something. Isaure restates and asks if that is correct. No two of them share a rhetorical move; the spec checks the vocabulary overlap pairwise.

## State (three invisible resources)

- **Composure** (restorable): your reserve for hard conversations, lies and running. The only thing rest refills.
- **Pursuit** (descending, first): how close the crown is. Public violence raises it first.
- **Smoulder** (descending): how close his fire is to the surface. Drawing a knife on him, or forcing him to face the nine, raises it; nothing you say by way of "no" does.

The storyteller never reads resources, so each one also has a hard-canon line in plain words: what wears the player down and what puts it back (sleep, a meal, Agathe's willow-bark tea); that the crown closes in whatever anybody does and anything loud brings it faster; and that his fire answers to provocation, never to refusal.

## The system, where the storyteller reads it

Production's `runTurnPure` reads hard canon, quest items and the chosen archetype's starting abilities, and nothing else of the rules. So:
- **Hard canon** carries the Oathfire and the Banked Word, the house rule, the clock, how the three pressures move, the pacing of every secret and the 13+ line.
- **The guest's cord** is a quest item the player starts wearing: the house rule made into an object. While they wear it nobody takes them out through the gate by force; only the prioress can cut it; trading it for the grey veil makes the shelter absolute; taking it off and walking out makes it only a cord. It is also the shape of Agathe's dilemma: the Archbishop's order is, in effect, an order to cut it.
- **Starting abilities** put both answers in front of the storyteller for every background, and a no always works and never feeds his fire.

## Pacing, where production reads it

`runTurnPure` sends each secret's `fact` but never its `revealHint`, sends quests as title and summary only, and never sends a step's `directorNotes`. So the rules that pace this story live in hard canon and in world-event notes, which it does read. (They used to sit in the tone guide; the tone guide is also the cover's mood text, so it now says only how the story feels.)
- He takes a glove off only for somebody who has asked about him rather than the fire, and never on the first night.
- Agathe opens the psalter only for somebody who already knows about his hands.
- Remy admits the writ only with the writ or the empty case in front of him. His part in the fire is the central secret and surfaces only through evidence the player finds, never through narration or somebody guessing.
- Mara confesses the letters when asked straight how he found the player.
- A yes does not mean riding tonight: the pass is two days away, his riders are spent, and carrying off a person named in a crown warrant is the war he came to prevent.
- If the player draws a knife on him, he does not defend himself and does not let his riders do it for him.

The cast cards are shown on the story page and read by the storyteller as public lines, so none of them gives a secret away: Mara "has been writing letters", Agathe holds the house rule "to keep, or to break", and Lucien's card repeats the rumour rather than the truth.
