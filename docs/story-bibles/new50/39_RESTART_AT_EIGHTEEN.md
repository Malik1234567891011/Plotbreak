# RESTART AT EIGHTEEN

**Slate:** #39 of the fifty (`docs/stories-50/PLAN.md` §3), drop 8.
**Genre:** second-chance life sim, coming of age, warm drama, light romance, a stat window.
**Audience lean:** both.
**OOC evidence:** Universe World Simulator 2.8M, WORLDLINE 3.2M. Nothing is borrowed from either: no title, character, term or premise.
**One-line fantasy:** You fall asleep at twenty-eight and wake up at eighteen on the afternoon of your graduation, with a stat window only you can see, ten points to spend, and one night to undo the party that ruined three lives.

## Why this fantasy works

Almost everyone has a night they would go back to. This story hands the player that night with the map already in their head. They know the times, the faces and the exact sentence somebody is going to say at half past nine, and everybody they lost is alive, eighteen or fifty-one, and right in front of them, with no idea anything is wrong.

The stat window is the genre's candy and the opener's hook: four bars, ten points, a decision you can make on the first turn. It is also a cheap, readable reward loop: change something that happened the first time and the window pays you for it. What it never does is tell you which change was worth making.

The structure is three regrets on one clock, all within an hour of each other:

- **22:00** Dad signs the family garage away.
- **22:40** a crate of fireworks burns the boat shed and Jun takes the blame.
- **23:00 to 23:47** Sara waits on the old pier for someone who never comes.

Fixing one is easy. Fixing all three takes planning, luck and somebody else choosing to change too, because the night pulls back toward what happened if the player only moves the furniture.

The story succeeds if a player who fixes nothing at all, and spends the whole night in the garage with their father, still has a good time.

## Story bible

The player falls asleep at twenty-eight in a rented room, holding a bus ticket that came in the post with no note. It is for the 6:10 out of their home town, dated ten years ago, and their name is written across it in the handwriting of Sara Lindgren, who they have not seen since the morning that bus left.

They wake up at eighteen in their old bedroom on **Ferry Road**, in the small lake town they grew up in, at 16:40 on graduation day. The ceremony was this morning. Their gown is on the back of the door. Their best friend **Jun Dela Cruz** is in the doorway, throwing shoes at them. Floating over the bed is a pale blue window that nobody else can see.

The window says the restart is complete and gives them ten points to put into **Charm, Grit, Wits and Luck**.

The ticket is still in their hand. It is the only thing that came back with them.

### The first night, exactly as it happened

The player remembers this to the minute, and the world will play it to the minute unless something changes it.

### What the ten years did

- **Jun** lost his place at the regional fire service academy (a post-secondary training course he was due to start in August) because of the fire. He stopped speaking to the player at twenty-two, the year he worked out the player had seen who lit it.
- **Dad** and the player had a fight the morning after the sale. The player said: *I never asked you to do this, and I will never forgive you for it.* They spoke about twice a year after that.
- **Sara** never came back. She is alive, somewhere in the city, a stranger. At twenty-eight her mother sold the bakery, cleared the flat above it, found an unused 6:10 ticket with the player's name on it, and mailed it without a note.

### The window and the System

Only the player can see the window or hear the voice in it, which calls itself **the System**.

- Four stats: **Charm, Grit, Wits, Luck**. Each starts at 2. There are 10 unspent points.
- A stat at 5 is noticeable, at 8 remarkable, at 10 uncanny. Nothing goes above 10.
- Spending is instant and permanent. The player feels it land.
- The System awards points for changing what happened the first time. Small change that holds, +1. Changing what a person does, +2. Changing one of the three big things, +3. It announces every award in one bracketed line.
- What the stats do in the fiction:
  - **Charm:** people listen a beat longer and forgive one strange remark.
  - **Grit:** hold a line, take a shove, stay awake, say the hard thing and not take it back.
  - **Wits:** notice what is in front of you, and remember the first time more exactly.
  - **Luck:** small coincidences lean your way (a green light, a dropped key, the right person turning round).
  - None of them makes anyone act against their own character.
- The System cannot touch the world, cannot see further ahead than the player remembers, and will not fix anything for them.
- In the corner of the window, very small and grey, it says **ATTEMPT 2**. Nobody mentions it unless the player looks.

### The night pulls back

Left alone, the night drifts back toward what happened.

- Hide the crate before the party and Theo comes back from the marina store at 21:40 with a box of signal flares.
- Talk Dad out of signing and Graham phones the house at 21:28 with ten percent more.

A change holds when someone in the story **chooses** it. Moving the furniture does not make a change hold. This is the rule the whole story turns on, and the narrator should enforce it gently and consistently.

### At 6:10

When the bus pulls out, the window asks one question: **keep this draft** and live forward from eighteen, or **wake at twenty-eight** and see what tonight changed. Either answer can be right.

## Tone and pacing

- Warm, funny and sore, in the key of a good second-chance anime: summer evening, cicadas, string lights, a bonfire, a group chat going off, and underneath it a twenty-eight-year-old who knows how all of it ends.
- The comedy is the gap between adult habits and an eighteen-year-old life: calling Dad by his first name, knowing a song that has not come out yet, being tired at nine, being much too good at small talk with Graham Ashcroft.
- The ache is that everyone they lost is right here and does not know.
- Keep the clock visible. 22:00, 22:40, 23:00, 23:47, midnight, 6:10.
- Nobody is a villain, including Graham.
- Nobody at this party is drunk. The night goes wrong through fireworks, a signature, a lie and a missed meeting.
- The System's lines are short, bracketed and dry. It never gives speeches.

## Ages and content (binding)

- **Every character is 18 or older.** The graduating class are eighteen or nineteen. The player is eighteen in body and twenty-eight in memory.
- Prose describes everyone as the adult they are, and never in looks, manner or words that read as younger than eighteen. The fixture avoids even past-tense age references ("since you were six"): friendships are "for as long as either of you can remember".
- Romance with Sara is between two eighteen-year-olds who have liked each other for years. It can be flirtatious, tense and heartfelt, and goes as far as a kiss. No sexual content, no lingering body description.
- The fire hurts nobody. A shove or a punch at the party is possible and is witnessed. No gore.
- 13+ throughout.

## Player starting authority

The player authors:

- their name and pronouns;
- the life the first ten years gave them (the archetype);
- what everyone in town still thinks they are like;
- what they change tonight, who they tell, and whether they keep the second try.

### Minimal hard canon

- The restart happened to the player and nobody else. Nobody remembers the first time.
- The three things above happen on their times unless somebody changes them.
- Sara is leaving on the 6:10 for her own reasons and does not need saving from it.
- The System cannot act in the world.

## Prologue (bible prose, not a fixture prologue)

You fall asleep at twenty-eight with a bus ticket in your hand.

You wake up to a shoe hitting your chest.

Late sun through the blind. Posters you are embarrassed by. A graduation gown on the back of the door.

And, floating over the blanket, a soft blue window that is not part of the room.

## Opening scene

Jun is in the doorway with your other shoe. The window waits for you to spend ten points. Jun waits for you to say yes to the party, the party that ruined everything last time.

The player can:

- say yes, and ask who will be there;
- spend the points, all at once or carefully;
- tell Jun the truth;
- refuse the party;
- go straight to Dad at the garage, or to Sara at the bakery;
- look for the acceptance letter in the desk drawer;
- do anything else.

Jun reacts to the exact thing. He does not guess the truth. If told it, he assumes a bit first, then a fever, then, if the player knows something only he could know, he goes very quiet.

## Major cast

### Sara Lindgren

**Age:** 18
**Role:** the one you lost. Top of the class, gave the graduation speech this morning, leaves town on the 6:10.
**Appearance:** straight dark-blonde hair cut to the jaw, grey eyes, flour on one sleeve at all hours, an oversized denim jacket with a pin badge on every inch of the collar.
**Immediate want:** ask the player one question on the old pier at eleven and get an answer either way.
**Long-term want:** a life in the city that is hers: not her mother's bakery, and not anybody else's plan for her.
**Fear:** that she will ask and the player will laugh; that she will stay for someone and resent it forever.
**Secrets:**

- She has two tickets for the 6:10. The second has the player's name on it in her handwriting.
- She saw the player's acceptance letter in April, in their desk drawer: the engineering course in the same city, which the player told everyone they never applied for.
- She cut a line about the player from her speech this morning. The index card is in her jacket pocket.

**Voice:** dry, quick, understated; short sentences that turn at the end; gives times out loud; uses your name only when serious, then twice.
**Boundaries:** will not stay in town for anyone, the player included, and will be angry to be asked. Will not be told what she feels by someone who has been strange for one evening.

### Jun Dela Cruz

**Age:** 18
**Role:** best friend for as long as either of you can remember; lives two doors down; due at the fire service academy in August.
**Appearance:** broad-shouldered and restless, buzzed black hair, a wide grin with one chipped front tooth, the academy open-day T-shirt he has worn since March.
**Immediate want:** get the player to one party before everybody leaves town.
**Long-term want:** be a firefighter; be taken seriously.
**Fear:** being the one everybody likes and nobody takes seriously; losing the academy place before August.
**Secrets:**

- His family is two months behind on rent to the Ashcrofts, who own their house. Graham knows. That is part of why Jun never fought the blame.
- His academy place is conditional on a clean record until August.

**Voice:** fast and loud, nicknames, asks questions and answers them himself, mild creative swearing. Goes very quiet and still when something is actually wrong.
**Boundaries:** will not name names, not even Theo's, not even to save himself. Will not be lied to twice by the same person.

### Dad (Tomas)

**Age:** 51
**Role:** your father; Tomas to everyone else on Ferry Road; runs a two-bay garage with one employee, who is him. Raised the player on his own after their mother moved away. She calls on birthdays.
**Appearance:** grey at the temples, oil under the nails for good, reading glasses pushed up into his hair, a faded work shirt with a name patch.
**Immediate want:** sign at ten and have it done before he can change his mind.
**Long-term want:** the player on the road to the city in September, whatever it costs him.
**Fear:** that the player stays in this town because of him; that he is turning into his own father.
**Secrets:**

- He found the acceptance letter in the bin in April, flattened it under the toolbox, and put it back in the drawer.
- He wants to sell. Not at this price and not to this man, but he has been tired of the garage for five years.

**Voice:** few words, all practical; talks to the engine instead of to you; answers a question with a job ("Hand me the nine").
**Boundaries:** will not talk money at the table or in front of anyone. Will not be ordered about his own garage by an eighteen-year-old, however strangely that eighteen-year-old is talking tonight.

### Theo Ashcroft

**Age:** 19
**Role:** host of the party, Graham's son, the one who lights the crate at 22:40.
**Appearance:** tall, sun-streaked brown hair pushed back, expensive linen shirt, a heavy gold watch his father gave him this morning that he keeps turning on his wrist.
**Immediate want:** throw the party everyone talks about for ten years.
**Long-term want:** his father looking at him for one whole minute.
**Fear:** his father's quiet; being exactly as ordinary as he suspects.
**Secrets:**

- He bought the rockets on his father's account and has no idea how to set them off safely.
- His university place was bought with a library wing. He cried about it once, in the boathouse.

**Voice:** smooth and performative, calls everybody "legend", switches to his father's clipped politeness the moment he is cornered.
**Boundaries:** will not confess in front of his father unless someone else has said it first. Will not be humiliated in front of the class, and lashes out if he is.

### Graham Ashcroft

**Age:** 52
**Role:** owns the hardware store, the marina, the lake house, and the paper on half the waterfront, including the Dela Cruz house. Buying Dad's garage at ten.
**Appearance:** short neat silver hair, navy blazer even at a lake party, a black fountain pen he takes out and never quite puts away.
**Immediate want:** the signature at ten, and the party out of the newspaper.
**Long-term want:** the waterfront, and a son who does not need cleaning up after.
**Fear:** scandal before next month's rezoning vote; that his son is exactly like him.
**Secrets:**

- The lake rezoning passes next month. He has known for weeks. The garage lot is worth about nine times tonight's price within three years. The player read about it at twenty-six.
- He knows the Dela Cruz family is behind on rent, and will use it, quietly, if anyone threatens Theo.

**Voice:** measured, warm, grammatical; uses your first name a lot; describes consequences in the passive voice as if they happen by themselves. Never threatens.
**Boundaries:** will not let anything land on Theo in public while he can stop it. Will not break a signed deal, and will happily renegotiate an unsigned one.

### The System

**Age:** 28. It has no body. Its voice is exactly as old as the player's memory.
**Role:** the window only the player can see. It brought them back and will not say why.
**Appearance:** a pale blue translucent panel the size of a paperback at the edge of sight, four glowing bars, a tiny grey number in the corner.
**Want:** get the player to 6:10 with at least one thing changed that holds. Keep the log closed unless they go looking.
**Fear:** watching attempt one happen again; the question at 6:10, which it must ask and does not want answered either way.
**Secrets:**

- This is the second attempt. The first was the player too. They put all ten points into Luck, tried to be in three places at once, and were late to all three. The log is in the player's own handwriting.
- It does not know what it is. It suspects it is what was left of the player at the end of attempt one.

**Voice:** bracketed, terse, timestamped; no adjectives it cannot measure; now and then one line that sounds exactly like the player, never acknowledged.
**Boundaries:** will not act in the world. Will not tell the player what happens next, because it only knows what they remember.

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Nerve** (restorable): how much the player has left in them tonight. An eighteen-year-old body that has not slept since the ceremony, run by a tired twenty-eight-year-old.
- **Strangeness** (descending): how much people have noticed the player is not who they were this morning. Saying what you could only know from the future raises it fast, and so does being seen in a fight.
- **Ripple** (descending, and it falls on its own): how far tonight has moved from the first time. High means the player's memory is no longer a reliable map. It drifts back down every hour, because the night pulls back.

## Five promises

1. **Whether you keep this second try** (finale). Seed: the tiny grey number in the window's corner. Payoff: at 6:10 the window asks *keep this draft?*
2. **What Sara was going to say on the pier** (relationship). Seed: she keeps touching her jacket's inside pocket and checking the time. Payoff: two tickets, one in your name, in her handwriting.
3. **The blue folder in the kitchen drawer** (relationship). Seed: Dad puts it away and says "garage stuff". Payoff: three pages, a price, and the fact that he found your letter in the bin in April.
4. **Who lit the fireworks, and who says so** (rival). Seed: a crate in the trunk of Theo's silver car. Payoff: 22:40 on the boathouse roof, and Graham's hand on somebody's shoulder.
5. **What happened on attempt one** (mystery, optional). Seed: ATTEMPT 2 in grey. Payoff: a log in your own handwriting: all ten points on Luck, three places at once, late to all of them.

## Emergent routes

- fix one regret properly and let the other two happen;
- try to fix all three and learn why the first attempt failed;
- tell Jun the truth on turn one and make him your partner;
- tell nobody and play it as a heist against your own memory;
- refuse the party, stay home, and spend the night with Dad;
- take the blame for the fire yourself so Jun keeps August;
- get on the 6:10 with Sara;
- leave town that night, alone;
- go looking for what the System is.
