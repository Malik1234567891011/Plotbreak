**Tags:** Horror, Survival, Comedy, Drama. **Rating:** 13+. **Intensity:** moderate.

## Why this fantasy works

The trope is "you are the zombie", which has proven pull on the reference
stores (But I Am a Zombie, Zombie Apocalypse). The usual version is a power
fantasy about being the monster. This one turns it round: the monster is the
only polite person left in the valley, and the danger is everybody else's
arithmetic.

Being a grey is a capability rather than a curse. The other greys ignore you,
so you are the only person who can walk into the dead town for medicine, up
the road into the crowd that is coming, or into the hospital your wristband
came from. Within an hour everybody at the camp has worked that out, and each
of them wants something from you. That gives the player something to bargain
with on turn one, and a reason to refuse.

The joke never gets in the way of the fear, and the fear is never gore. It
lives in posture, sound and temperature.

## Story bible (premise as shipped)

The grey took the cities two years ago. The people it takes stop talking, stop
feeling the cold and start walking, and nobody it has taken has ever come back.

You died some time last night, in a ditch by a canal. This morning you woke up
anyway. Your skin is grey, your heart is not beating, and you are still
thinking, which is supposed to be impossible.

You walked to the only lights for miles: a survivors' camp on a canal lock,
behind water and wire. At the fence you said the one word you could find.
Please.

The woman on watch has a crossbow on your chest and has not fired, because no
grey has ever said please before. The man who runs the camp wants you shot,
sold or useful, in that order. The camp doctor wants your blood, and she is
hiding something under her sleeve.

The other greys ignore you. You can walk through a crowd of them unharmed, and
you can hear them: a murmur made of the last thing each of them ever said.
Four thousand of them are coming down the valley now, and they will reach the
water in two days.

You need to make the living trust you before then. You also need to keep
yourself in check, because you are hungry, and what you are hungry for is
warmth, and the warmest things for miles are people.

## Tone and pacing

- Dark comedy with a warm, stubborn heart. The joke is that the only polite
  person left is dead.
- Horror is posture, sound and temperature. Greys are described by how they
  stand, what they hum and how cold they are. **No gore, ever.** A bite, if one
  happens, happens off the page and is told by what it costs afterwards.
- The hunger is for **warmth**: fires, hot water, living people. It is written
  as a pull towards a fire on a winter night, never as appetite, and never for
  flesh. The player always decides what to do with it.
- Nobody at the Lock is a villain. Cole is right about the arithmetic, Doc is
  right about the cure, Ivo is honest about his prices.
- Romance is slow and careful and goes no further than a kiss.
- Small physical detail carries the scene: cold hands round a mug nobody
  offered, tally marks on a crossbow, a hot water bottle held like contraband.

Pacing: day one is the wire and the first test (funny as often as frightening).
The murmur arrives at 13:00 on day one, Ivo's offer at 16:00, the nine o'clock
tone at 21:00. Day two brings the front into sight and the vote. Dawn on day
three the crowd is at the water; that night the lock gates go unless something
has changed.

## Player starting authority

The player is a grey who can think, speak and choose, protagonist `BLANK`
(they name themselves and pick pronouns). The setup asks:

1. **What were you called, when you were alive?** (displayName)
2. **What did you do before you died?** The archetype. Fixed for the whole
   story and cannot be changed later.
   - **You Were a Nurse**: healing and steady nerves. Stillness, Grey Sense,
     Manners; a penlight; starts with Read the Warmth.
   - **You Drove Deliveries**: scout and scavenger. Scrounging, Shamble; a
     courier's map of the dead town; starts with Scrounge.
   - **You Fixed Machines**: technical and heavy work. Know-how, Fighting; a
     multitool; a little standing with the Lock; starts with Dead Lift.
   - **You Talked for a Living**: persuasion and bluffing. Manners, Passing for
     Alive; a visitor's lanyard; starts with Name Your Price.
   - **You Kept the Door**: defence and intimidation. Menace, Fighting; a
     doorman's torch; starts with Show Your Teeth.

   Every archetype also starts with **Walk Among Them**, the capability the
   whole camp wants from the player. The storyteller reads only the chosen
   archetype's starting abilities, so these are how it learns what the player
   can do.
3. **What do you still remember about yourself?** (optional, one sentence)
4. Advanced: first instinct at the wire, and what you look like now (this
   reaches only the portrait generator).

The only identity the storyteller reads is the archetype plus name and
pronouns, so everything the archetype means, starting item included, is in
its summary and blurb. Line 3 (`worldKnowsAboutYou`) never reaches the
production storyteller; that is a platform gap, not one this story can fix.

The player is an adult with years of working life behind them; every
archetype is a job they held for years before they died.

Everyone starts with **the hospital wristband**: ST IDE'S / TRIAL 9 / 31,
name scratched off. Looking at it opens the optional Trial Nine thread.

### Minimal hard canon

All of this is in `rules.hardCanon`. The content limits live there and not
only in the tone guide, because the storyteller-test briefs override the tone
guide and keep only hard canon.

- The grey took the cities two years ago. Greys stop speaking, run cold, and
  walk towards warmth and sound. A bite or a deep scratch passes it on. Most
  people go grey within a day; a few run colder for weeks first.
- The player died last night and woke as a grey who can still think and speak.
  Nobody knows why.
- Other greys ignore the player. The player hears them as a murmur of last
  sentences.
- The hunger is for warmth, never flesh, and the player always decides. They
  can feel from across a room who is feverish and who is running cold.
- Kestrel Lock holds 38 survivors. Greys will not cross running water while the
  weir runs. Nothing grey crosses the footbridge.
- Being a grey runs on three things the prose shows and no meter does:
  Clarity (every hard thing costs some; an hour somewhere cold and quiet
  brings it back; at zero the player goes grey for a while), Hunger (rises as
  they get colder; only heat brings it down, a fire or a hot water bottle from
  the cottage kettle) and Alarm (the camp's fear; eases as people get used to
  them, jumps when they fight or show their teeth).
- A grey body is slow, strong and very hard to stop: it can shift a winch or a
  weir paddle two living people cannot, and it costs warmth to do it.
- The Horde, about 4,000 greys, reaches the water at dawn on the third day,
  about two days from now, unless its course changes. It is walking towards
  something, and a grey who learns what can walk at its front instead.
- Everybody in this story is an adult: the 38 at the Lock, Ivo's crew, every
  grey in Wexley, St Ide's and the valley, and every voice in the murmur.
  Nobody in it is under eighteen. Jessa is 24, Doc 41, Cole 46, Sunny 20 and
  Ivo 35, and the player had years of working life before they died.
- Greys are hard to stop. A crossbow bolt slows the player without ending
  them. Whoever goes for somebody at the wire takes a bolt in the shoulder and
  is dragged in for the doctor anyway.
- Nobody is ever shown wounded, bitten, bleeding or eaten. A bite always
  happens off the page and is told by what it costs afterwards; any other
  hurt gets one plain line and never a description of the wound.
- Romance goes no further than one kiss, on the page or off it. Nobody goes
  to bed with anybody, and no scene cuts away to imply it.

## Opening scene

The player is at the landward wire of Kestrel Lock at 06:10 on day one. Jessa
and Cole are there. Nobody else is.

>

>

>

>

>

Checks: Jessa speaks to the player in the first two words and is named by

Opening suggestions (one said, one done, one risky):

1. Said: *"Thank you for not shooting me. I would be very grateful if you kept

2. Done: *I take my hands off the wire, one at a time, and step back into the

## Major cast

Six. Every person is an adult, and their ages are fixed here.

### Jessa Marlow (24)

Night watch on the wire, the best shot at the Lock, the one who did not fire.

- **Wants:** to know whether there is still a person in there before Cole
  decides for everyone; to hold the wire until the Horde passes.
- **Hidden:** her older brother Tam (25 when he turned) was left outside the
  gate on the first night. She volunteered for nights so that if he ever came
  to the wire she would be the one who saw him. 712 tally marks on the stock.
- **Fears:** being right to have waited and finding out too late; becoming the
  person who shoots first.
- **Voice:** short. Imperatives and questions, almost nothing else. Repeats
  your last word back. When rattled, goes quiet and makes you say it again.
  Watches your mouth, not your eyes, while you talk. Never says sorry; says
  "Noted." She does not count out loud: numbers are Cole's. *"Say something
  else." "Hungry. You said hungry. Hungry for what?"*
- **Gates:** lowers the crossbow (trust 30); tells you about Tam (trust 55,
  after day one); sits close on purpose (romance, trust 65 / affection 55).

### Ife "Doc" Amari (41)

The camp doctor. A vet for fourteen years before the grey.

- **Wants:** a sample of your blood and a week to work on it; to keep her
  sleeve down until she has an answer.
- **Hidden:** a grey in the reeds caught her forearm eleven days ago. Her
  temperature is falling every morning. Your blood is the first thing that
  has made her think she might keep her mind. She also recognises the
  wristband: Trial Nine was a vaccine study at St Ide's.
- **Fears:** turning in front of the people she has patched up for two years;
  becoming a scientist who stops asking.
- **Voice:** warm and precise. "May I?" before every action. Thinks out loud
  in clinical terms, then translates. Apologises to objects. Laughs when
  frightened. *"Clinically speaking you are a contradiction, and I mean that
  warmly."*
- **Boundary:** never takes anything without a yes.

### Cole Garrity (46)

Runs Kestrel Lock by arithmetic. The man on the watchtower in the opener.

- **Wants:** all thirty-eight through the Horde; to find out whether you are a
  weapon, a cure, or a mouth he cannot afford.
- **Hidden:** on the first night he shut the footbridge gate at 23:40 on
  sixty-one people, Tam among them, and told Jessa her brother was already
  turning. Tam had not been bitten. The gate log in his desk has every name.
  He has also asked Ivo, by radio, what a talking grey would fetch.
- **Voice:** flat, slow, numerical. Counts people instead of naming them;
  surnames only. States decisions as facts. Never says please, and notices
  that he does not. *"Thirty-eight. That is the only number I care about, and
  you are not in it yet."*

### Sunny Abara (20)

Generator and radios. Decided inside a minute that you are the best thing to
happen at the Lock in two years.

- **Wants:** to give you a radio and a call sign ("Polite One"); to hear one
  new voice answer his nightly call before winter.
- **Hidden:** every night at nine he sends a tone and a call for survivors on
  the old valley repeater, against Cole's orders. The Horde turned south the
  first night he did it and has been walking towards the tone ever since. He
  has no idea, and his cast card must not give it away.
- **Voice:** fast and bright, radio procedure for everything ("copy", "over",
  "say again"). Three questions before you answer one. Goes quiet and formal
  when scared. *"Do they dream? Do you? Is it cold? Sorry. Over. Your turn."*

### Ivo Kade (35)

Captain of the Ferrymen, a river trader who has come up the weir to buy you.

- **Wants:** to buy you, from Cole or from yourself, before the Horde closes
  the river; to get his crew to the coast before winter.
- **Hidden:** the coast laboratory he sells to wants greys brought in whole
  and still moving. He tells himself it is for a cure and has never been
  inside to check.
- **Boundary:** will not take you by force, and never breaks a deal once
  shaken on.
- **Voice:** easy merchant talk. Calls everyone "friend". Prices everything in
  litres, crates and favours. Ends a hard truth with a laugh. *"Forty litres of
  diesel and a crate of salt for a grey that says please."*

### The Horde (a crowd; every voice in it is an adult's)

About four thousand greys walking down the valley as one crowd, and the only
sound in the world that feels like home to the player.

- **Wants:** the warm place the tone comes from; to take you back in.
- **Hidden:** it follows Sunny's nine o'clock tone. Tam Marlow is in it, still
  saying "Cole, open it, it is me, open the gate." Somewhere in the middle is
  Trial Nine participant number 12, another mind like yours, very tired.
- **Voice:** fragments in many adult voices, lower case, broken off mid
  sentence. The word please comes up more than any other. *"...is the kettle
  on, i will only be a minute..." "...you. there you are. come in out of the
  cold..."*
- Never harms the player. Will not cross running water while the weir runs.

## Behavioral state

Three invisible banded variables. The prose shows them; no meter does.

- **Clarity** (only GOOD_HIGH; rest restores it): how much of the player is
  driving. Low means fragments, borrowed words, losing seconds to warm things.
  Sitting somewhere cold and quiet brings it back.
- **Alarm** (first GOOD_LOW): the camp's fear. Falls on its own as people get
  used to you; jumps when you fight or show your teeth. First in order so a
  public fight frightens people rather than making you hungrier.
- **Hunger** (GOOD_LOW): for warmth. Rises on its own because a grey keeps
  getting colder. Comes down with heat: the hot water bottles in the cottage
  kitchen take off 25.

## Places

- **The Wire** (start): chain-link, tin cans, a scaffold watchtower, a single
  mesh gate onto a winch footbridge. The player stands at the gate, close
  enough to touch through it.
- **Kestrel Lock**: the island. Lock chamber, narrowboats, cook fire.
- **The Pump House**: cold brick room that bolts from outside. Where the camp
  keeps things it is not sure about. A folded blanket.
- **The Lock-Keeper's Cottage**: Cole's office and the only proper stove. The
  gate log; the hot water bottles.
- **Doc's Boathouse**: the infirmary, a microscope, a whiteboard about you.
  Her casebook.
- **The Radio Shed**: on stilts, aerial, call-sign board with a space for you.
  Sunny's broadcast log and spare handset.
- **The Weir**: four iron paddles. Open all four and the island is a moat.
- **The Ferry Landing**: Ivo's black barge below the weir.
- **The Towpath**: outside the wire; greys in ones and twos facing the lights.
- **The North Road**: abandoned cars under a motorway bridge. The way it comes.
- **The Railway Cutting**: nine miles north, filled with the Horde.
- **Wexley High Street**: the dead town, greys standing in shop doorways.
- **Holt's Chemist**: the pharmacist still behind the counter; the medicine
  box Cole has lost three people trying to fetch.
- **St Ide's Hospital** and **Ward Nine** (hidden until found): forty beds, a
  card on a rail with your number.

Every place has an inbound edge; Ward Nine is reached only from St Ide's.

## Five promises

1. **Whether the Lock keeps you, and as what** (FINALE). Seed: a folded
   blanket nobody was asked to leave. Payoff: the camp decides out loud, and
   you get a say.
2. **Four thousand, coming down the valley** (BOSS). Seed: a murmur that
   lurches every night at nine. Payoff: the crowd at the water, and what you
   did with two days.
3. **Why Jessa did not shoot** (RELATIONSHIP). Seed: seven hundred tally
   marks. Payoff: her brother's last sentence and the man who shut the gate.
4. **What Doc Amari keeps under her sleeve** (RELATIONSHIP). Seed: long
   sleeves in a warm week, a dropped tray. Payoff: eleven days of falling
   temperatures, and your blood as her only answer.
5. **Why you are still thinking** (MYSTERY, optional). Seed: the wristband.
   Payoff: Ward Nine, and number 12 with the same red note as yours.

## Emergent routes

**Say Something Else** (main): talk her down, step into the light, warn them
what is coming, go for her through the wire (a bolt in the shoulder, netted,
dragged in anyway), or walk off into the dead town. Then the first day: fetch
the chemist's box, sit for Doc's needle, hold still in a warm room, do the
heavy work, or make a life outside among the greys.

**Four Thousand** (main): learn it follows the tone (Sunny's trust, the
murmur at nine, walking into its front, or the broadcast log). Then answer it:
lead it away (`Lead the Horde`, granted by learning why it comes), flood the
island at the weir, put the camp on Ivo's barge, get the call switched off,
walk in and join it, or do nothing and get people out when it crosses.

**May I?** (side): refuse the needle, bargain for it, or give it. Find out
about her sleeve by trust, by feeling the cold in her, or by reading her
casebook. Decide what the blood is for: she tries it on herself, you take her
to Ward Nine, or you tell Cole.

**The Brother at the Wire** (side): learn about Tam from Jessa or Sunny; learn
who shut the gate from the murmur, from Cole, or from the gate log; then bring
Tam to the wire, tell her yourself, or keep it from her.

**Cargo** (side): negotiate for yourself, turn Ivo down, go for him, or be
sold by the vote if you gave the camp no reason to keep you.

**Trial Nine** (lead, optional): opens only on inspecting the wristband.
Find the ward alone, with Doc, or by Ivo's barge; read your file, have Doc
read it, or leave it in the drawer.
