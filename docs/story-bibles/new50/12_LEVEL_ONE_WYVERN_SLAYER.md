# LEVEL ONE, WYVERN SLAYER

**Slate:** #12 in `docs/stories-50/PLAN.md`  
**Genre:** fantasy action, underdog power fantasy, guild comedy, light mystery  
**Audience lean:** male  
**Rating:** 13+. Fights are bruises, breaks and a split lip. The wyvern head is a heavy, smelly prop, never a gore piece. Romance goes no further than a kiss.  
**One-line fantasy:** The guild crystal says you are level one. The wyvern head you dragged eleven miles into the hall says otherwise, and every party in town wants it more than they want you.

## Why this fantasy works

OOC's evidence is the OP-skill isekai shelf (Reincarnated With an OP Skill, 20.7M) and the "kicked out, underestimated" shelf (Kicked Out of the S Rank Party, 568K). Both sell the same feeling: a room full of people who measured you wrong, and the turn where they find out.

This story delivers that on turn one. The player is already strong. The proof is on the floor. The comedy and the tension come from a world whose whole economy runs on a number that says the opposite.

The player never has to earn the power. What they decide is what to do with a town that will not believe it: take the insulting job, fight for the kill, sell the prize, show off, hide, or leave.

## Originality check

- No isekai, no reincarnation, no status screen, no skill granted by a god. The player is a local person whose strength came from their own life.
- "Draw" is our term: the strength a monster gives up when it dies, which goes into whoever killed it. It is plain English and is glossed the first time it appears.
- The crystal reads draw, not skill. That is the engine of the story, and it is ours.
- The rule that cuts the player out is **Rule Twelve**. An earlier draft called it "Section Nine", which is the name of a famous anime unit, so it was renamed.
- Title searched on 2026-11-22: no light novel, manga or game called "Level One, Wyvern Slayer" or "Level 1 Wyvern Slayer". The nearest premise, *My Unique Skill Makes Me OP Even at Level 1*, is a hero stuck at level 1 with a drop-rate skill. Ours has no skill at all, and the level is the town's mistake, not the hero's gimmick.
- Names checked against the obvious franchises.

## Story bible

**Kestrel Ford** is a frontier river town under a line of high, burnt ridges. Its adventurers' guild hall is the biggest building in town. Five old wyvern skulls hang from the rafters.

Everyone who joins the guild puts a hand on the **guild crystal**. It reads a person's **level** from the **draw** they carry. When a monster dies, its strength (the draw) goes into whoever killed it. Kill enough and you rise. The ranked parties, the bounty rules and the town's whole idea of who matters run on that number.

Last night the player killed an adult wyvern on **Scald Ridge**, alone. This morning they dragged its head into town on a rope.

The crystal reads them at **level 1**.

That reading is accurate about the draw. The wyvern's draw did not go into the player, and nobody in the hall has ever seen that happen. So the draw is still in the head, sealed in the **eye-stone** behind its left eye, for about three days. Whoever cracks the stone takes it: roughly fifteen levels, all at once, for anybody.

That makes the head the most valuable object in Kestrel Ford, and the person who killed it officially the least important.

### The rule that cuts the player out

**Rule Twelve** in the guild's rulebook: a bounty above a hunter's rank is paid to the party that escorted them, or it stays with the hall. A level 1 cannot be paid for a wyvern. The bounty (three hundred gold) goes to whichever ranked party puts its name on the kill.

So **Gale Marrick**, leader of the strongest party in the room, makes the obvious offer: carry for his party as a porter, for a silver a day. His party's name goes on the head. He gets the bounty and the eye-stone.

### What the hall does not know

Eight days ago Gale's party, the **Ninefold**, went up Scald Ridge for this same wyvern. It came down on their camp at night. They ran, and left their porter, **Harl Benning**, on the ridge with a broken leg. Harl walked down alone in four days. Tamsin logged the party out with four names and back with three, and wrote "minus one" in the margin. Nobody asked her about it. Gale told the hall the job was called off for weather.

### What the guild has forgotten

The five skulls on the rafters were killed by the guild's founders, sixty to a hundred years ago, before the crystal existed. The founders had the player's trait: the draw never went into them. The old charter in the guildmaster's loft calls them "the closed hands" and says the guild was built by people who took nothing from the dead.

The crystal came later, with the draw trade, to rank people by what they had taken. It made the founders' kind invisible. **Guildmaster Orsk**'s grandmother was the last of them. He has waited his whole life to see another.

This is the optional mystery. A player who never asks why the crystal says 1 never has to hear about the charter.

### The clock

Wyverns pair for life. The dead one's mate is alive, and it follows the smell of its partner's draw. That smell is on the head, on whoever handles it, and, if the stone is cracked, on whoever took the draw. It reaches the town within three nights unless something changes that.

## Playable span

Three days and three nights in Kestrel Ford, starting at twenty past eleven in the morning at the guild counter. Everything before (how the player got strong) is the archetype. Everything after (the guild changing, or not) is endings.

## Tone and pacing

- An underdog power fantasy played straight and funny. The joke is never that the player is weak. It is that the room keeps measuring them wrong, and how long it takes each person to notice.
- Fights are short, physical and readable: distance, footing, one good blow. A B-rank is genuinely dangerous. A wyvern is a disaster.
- Everybody wants something concrete: a bounty, a stone, a rule kept, a debt paid.
- Gale is a rival, not a monster. He is charming, frightened and good at his job.
- The player may attack, lie, refuse, sell, show off, hide or leave. The town reacts to the specific thing.
- Never explain the player's strength as a curse, a god, a hidden screen or a voice in their head. It is theirs.

## Player starting authority

The player names themselves and picks where their strength came from (the archetype). They author their temperament, how they look at the counter, and everything they do with the head. They do not author the crystal's reading, Rule Twelve, the mate, or what Gale's party did on the ridge.

### Hard canon

- Everyone in this story is an adult. The player is twenty-eight.
- The player killed an adult wyvern alone the night before and dragged its head into town.
- When a monster dies, its draw goes into whoever killed it. The crystal reads level from draw.
- The player does not take draw. The crystal's 1 is accurate about draw and wrong about everything else.
- The draw is sealed in the eye-stone for about three days. Cracked, it goes into whoever cracks it. If the player cracks it, the draw still does not go into them: it goes into the nearest other hand on the pieces, or, with no other hand on them, bleeds away and the stone goes dark. Uncracked, it can be sold.
- Rule Twelve: a bounty above your rank goes to your escorting party or stays with the hall.
- The mate is alive and will reach the town within three nights unless something changes that.
- Gale's party ran from this wyvern eight days ago and left Harl on the ridge. Tamsin logged it.
- The five rafter skulls were killed by founders with the player's trait. Orsk knows.
- The player's strength is their own, from their past. No curse, no god, no hidden screen, no voice in their head.
- Fights end in bruises, breaks and a split lip, never gore. Romance goes no further than a kiss. (These limits live in hard canon, not the tone guide, because the tone guide is also the cover's mood line.)

## Cast

Every character is an adult. Ages are stated here and in the fixture's hard canon. The cast is described on the page by build and costume, never by age words.

### Fia Quenby, 22
**Role:** a mage, level thirty-one on paper, and the only person in the hall who believes the player.  
**Looks:** lean and quick, a cloud of copper hair tied back with a chalk-dusted ribbon, green eyes, a plum mage's coat she has slept in, a tailor's measuring tape worn like a scarf.  
**Motive:** proving the crystal measures the wrong thing, and finding one partner who chose her for what she can actually do.  
**Fear:** being found out. Her family paid the Ascent House for twenty-six of her thirty-one levels through escorted kills.  
**Secrets:** the bought levels. And Gale dropped her from the Ninefold a month ago for "slowing them down", three weeks before the ridge trip. Harl was the only one who was kind to her, and he has not spoken to anyone from the party since.  
**Voice:** fast, breathless, self-interrupting. Gives measurements as proof, says "technically" and "sorry", and keeps going. Her sentences only get short when she is frightened.  
> "Sorry, can I, sorry. The wound. Two plates of neck armour, one angle, no second blow."

### Tamsin Wick, 27
**Role:** the guild receptionist. Runs the counter, the crystal and the day book.  
**Looks:** tall and composed, black hair in a hard knot with two pencils through it, guild-grey waistcoat, reading spectacles she looks over.  
**Motive:** keep the record exact and the rules true. She wants to be a guild inspector and needs one case to prove it.  
**Fear:** that the rules she enforces are a fence around the people who wrote them.  
**Secret:** she logged Gale's party back "minus one" and nobody ever asked.  
**Voice:** clipped and procedural. Quotes rule numbers from memory and says "Next." to end a conversation. Her kindness arrives as a technicality she has found for you, never as sympathy.  
> "Rule twelve. I did not write it. I stamp it."

### Gale Marrick, 29
**Role:** leader of the Ninefold, the hall's top B-rank party. Level thirty-eight.  
**Looks:** broad-shouldered, sandy hair cut by somebody expensive, polished half-plate, a blue sash with nine stitched stars, a silver coin walking across his knuckles.  
**Motive:** reach A-rank before his patron at the Ascent House drops him at the end of the season. The eye-stone would do it in one night.  
**Fear:** being found out as the man who ran. Being exactly as good as his number and no better.  
**Secrets:** he left Harl on the ridge. He plans to come over the carcass yard wall after midnight with a jeweller's hammer and crack the eye-stone himself.  
**Voice:** warm salesman's cadence. Puts a price on everything, threats included. Says "friend" just before something costs you. Lowers his voice instead of raising it.  
> "Porter, a silver a day. Take it, or carry your lizard home."

### Hadric Orsk, 63
**Role:** guildmaster. A big, slow former wyvern hand with a ruined left knee.  
**Looks:** enormous, white beard cropped close, a leather coat scored by old claws, a blackthorn stick, a long claw scar from collar to jaw.  
**Motive:** keep the guild solvent. The draw trade pays for the roof, and the truth about the founders would empty its coffers.  
**Fear:** dying as the last man who knew what the rafters mean.  
**Secret:** the charter in the chest upstairs, and his grandmother.  
**Voice:** short. Mostly questions and single words. Lets silence do the asking. Never explains a ruling and never takes one back.  
> "Show me. Not the head. The swing."

### Harl Benning, 34
**Role:** the porter Gale left on the ridge. Now keeps the carcass yard behind the hall, on a cane.  
**Looks:** rope-lean and weathered, a porter's harness he still wears out of habit, a splinted right leg, a cane cut from a pine branch.  
**Motive:** to be paid what he is owed, and to see the man who owes it carry something heavy for once.  
**Fear:** going back up the ridge.  
**Secret:** he saw the nest before they ran, with two wyverns on it, not one. He has known for eight days that the dead one had a mate.  
**Voice:** dry. Tells the worst things like jokes about the weather. Talks in loads and weights. Never says Gale's name; says "him".  
> "Four days down that ridge on one leg. Took a while. Scenery was good."

### Marguerite Sallow, 46
**Role:** broker at the Ascent House, which sells escorted kills (and the draw that comes with them) to rich families.  
**Looks:** poised, silver-streaked hair in a perfect coil, a high-collared dove-grey gown, a folding fan, grey gloves she never takes off.  
**Motive:** buy the eye-stone uncracked for a client's heir, a pleasant man of about thirty who arrives on the second morning. Keep the crystal the only measure that matters.  
**Fear:** a world where the number can be bought and everyone knows it.  
**Secret:** her house arranged most of the high readings in the hall, including twenty-six of Fia's levels and the patronage that keeps Gale in good armour.  
**Voice:** courteous and unhurried, every hard thing wrapped in a soft word. Never says a figure aloud if she can write it on a card. Gets your name very slightly wrong until the day she decides you matter, and from then on gets it exactly right.  
> "The crystal does not measure strength. It is a receipt, and my house writes most of them."

## Places

- **The Guild Hall**: the counter, the crystal, the B-rank table, the notice board, five skulls on the rafters. Start. Connects to the yard, the loft and the square.
- **The Carcass Yard**: behind the hall. Scales, hooks, a cart gate whose lock does not work. The head sleeps here. Harl's shed.
- **The Guildmaster's Loft**: up the back stairs. Maps, a cold stove, a locked chest with the charter.
- **Ford Square**: the market, the smithy, the stone bridge. The hub; every other town location opens off it.
- **The Split Tankard**: the parties' inn. Gale's table, the best room upstairs, a stew pot that has not left the fire in thirty years.
- **The Ascent House**: velvet, ledgers, and a waiting room with a list of services that never uses the word price.
- **The Apothecary Attic**: Fia's room, chalk charts of every crystal reading she has ever taken.
- **The Old Watch**: the founders' wyvern tower at the edge of town, with a bell and a rusted bolt-thrower.
- **The East Road**: out of town toward the ridges. Carters, cattle, open sky.
- **Scald Ridge**: burnt pines and scree. Where the player killed the wyvern. Gale's abandoned camp and trodden banner are still there.
- **The Nest**: a crag above the second scree. Locked until somebody tells you the way or you track it.

## Behavioral state

Three variables, all invisible.

- **Stamina** (the only one rest restores): the bill for an eleven-mile drag. Low, a B-rank can actually win; high, a room full of ranked hunters is just a room.
- **Rumour** (rises with every public show of strength, fades slowly): from "a lucky level one" to parties riding in from the next valley and the guild forced to act in public. A brawl in the hall raises this first.
- **Scent** (the wyvern clock): rises on its own and faster when anybody handles the head. Low, the mate circles the far ridges; high, it comes down wherever the head is.

## Five promises

1. **Mystery: why the crystal says 1.** Seed: the crystal hesitates before it lights the number. Payoff: the skulls, the charter, the closed hands.
2. **Rival: Gale's smile.** Seed: he looks at the head the whole time he talks to you, never at you. Payoff: his party ran, and left Harl.
3. **Boss: the second wyvern.** Seed: Harl says, to nobody, that wyverns are like swans about some things. Payoff: it comes for the head, at night, over the ford.
4. **Relationship: Fia, whose number is too high.** Seed: she is the only person measuring the wound instead of laughing. Payoff: thirty-one levels, twenty-six of them bought.
5. **Finale: what the hall writes under your name.** Seed: one iron tag, stamped 1. Payoff: whether the guild changes the number, the rule, or neither.

## Active story pressures

- **First afternoon:** the Ascent House sends a sealed offer (a figure on a card: four hundred gold for the stone, uncracked).
- **First evening:** a carter races into the square. Something with a wingspan like a barn roof crossed the east road. Everybody now knows about the mate.
- **First night, after midnight:** Gale and two of the Ninefold come over the yard wall with a hammer. If nobody is there, he cracks the stone.
- **Second morning:** the heir's coach arrives. The rafter plates catch the light for a player who has been looking around.
- **Second noon:** Orsk calls a proving in the yard. Whoever can show the blow that killed the wyvern gets the kill.
- **Second night:** a cow is taken from the ford meadow.
- **Third night:** the old watch bell rings for the first time in sixty years, and the mate comes down over the ford.

## Emergent routes

- refuse Gale and fight for the kill by the rules (the proving, Orsk's ruling);
- take the silver and carry for the Ninefold, knowing what they are;
- haggle, sell the stone, and leave rich;
- crack the stone and decide whose hands are on it (Fia's, Harl's, nobody's);
- expose Gale in the hall, use what you know privately, or leave it to Harl;
- kill the mate alone at the nest, hold the old watch with the town, meet it in the square, or lead it into the high country;
- partner with Fia, or more, or part as friends;
- learn what the crystal does not count, then tell the hall, keep it with Orsk, or sell the silence;
- walk out of Kestrel Ford on turn three.

## Endings

Twelve, because the storyteller reads the first twelve.

- **A Silver a Day** (common): you carried for Gale, and his party's name went on your kill.
- **On the Board** (common): the kill is entered under your name. Level 1, on the B-rank board anyway.
- **The Ford Burned** (common): the mate came down and the town paid for it. A loss reachable without a mistake.
- **Wyvern Slayer, Twice** (uncommon): you killed the mate too.
- **Over the Ridge** (uncommon): you dragged the head into the high country and the mate followed you, not the town.
- **Four Hundred Gold** (uncommon): you sold the eye-stone. A pleasant heir is fifteen levels stronger on paper and no better with a sword.
- **Carry Your Lizard Home** (uncommon): you walked out of Kestrel Ford. Reachable early.
- **The Porter's Due** (rare): Gale exposed, Harl paid.
- **The Old Watch Held** (rare): the town and the founders' cut brought the mate down from the old tower.
- **Two Wrong Numbers** (rare): you and Fia register as a party of a level 1 and a level 31 who can barely cast in public.
- **Level One, Officially** (rare): the secret kept. Orsk quietly makes you the guild's unlisted wyvern hand.
- **The Sixth Skull** (unique): your wyvern goes up on the rafters, the crystal comes off the counter, and the guild changes its rules.

## Archetypes (where your strength came from)

Mutually exclusive histories. None of their names appears in the hard canon, the premise or the cast, so the storyteller only ever reads the one the player picks. Each starts with Full Swing plus one signature move.

- **The Hermit's Disciple**: ten years learning one cut from an old swordsman on a mountain. A notched sword. Full Swing, Hold Back.
- **The Woodcutter**: ten years felling and hauling timber out of the deep forest alone. A felling axe. Full Swing, Track It.
- **The Discharged Soldier**: six years on a border shield line where the officers kept every kill. A dented shield. Full Swing, Read the Hall.
- **The Farmhand**: a hill farm, and a fence post that has seen off everything that came for the sheep. Full Swing, Say It Plain.

Because the Farmhand killed the wyvern with a fence post and the Soldier carries a shield, nothing the storyteller reads about the wound assumes a blade: it was one blow, through two plates of neck armour.
