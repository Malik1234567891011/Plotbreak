# RESCUE RATING

**Slate:** #5 of the 50-story plan (`docs/stories-50/PLAN.md`, section 3).
**Genre:** superhero action comedy, romance, conspiracy
**Audience lean:** both
**Rating:** 13+. Flirtation, tension and at most a kiss. Fights are legible and never graphic.
**One-line fantasy:** You just caught the city’s most wanted woman, live, and forty thousand viewers are rating what you do with her.

**OOC evidence:** Hero Academy Apex (1.8M plays, hero/villain pick), Dating the Strongest Hero (1.5M). Nothing here is copied from either: no academy, no fake dating, no hero/villain draft. The shared ground is a ranked hero city and a romance across the law.

**Originality notes.** No franchise terms. Powers are "powers". Licensed heroes are "licensed heroes" and the regulator is "the Rescue Bureau". There is no television show, no sponsor-logo suit gimmick and no points table borrowed from anything: the ranking is a live five-star vote on a streaming app, and the twist (staged accidents, a weighted vote) belongs to this story.

**Names.** The lead was first drafted as Vesper Lyle: one letter from Vesper Lynd, the double-dealing love interest of *Casino Royale*, playing the same role, and sharing a first name with Zero Throne's mech. She is Sloane Lyle. The handler is Kit Navarro rather than a Morales, which pointed at a famous superhero. Dante wears a high gold-lined collar rather than a sponsor patch, so no logo ends up in the art and nothing echoes the sponsor-suit look of existing hero shows.

## Why this fantasy works

The player gets the two big superhero fantasies at once, in the first line: you made a spectacular save, and the whole city saw it. Then the story takes the second one away. The woman you saved is the most wanted person in the city, the stream turns on you in real time, and you have thirty seconds to decide what kind of hero you are before the police reach the roof.

The live rating is the engine. Every choice the player makes on camera gets a number and three lines of chat, so the player always knows what the city thinks, and always has the option of not caring. Turning the camera off is the most dramatic thing a player can do in this world, and it costs something.

Underneath is a real conspiracy with a deadline, a romance with a woman who cannot be filmed unless you are holding her, and a rival who is the best-loved man in the city and knows he does not deserve it.

## Story bible

**Calder Bay** is a harbour city of nine million. About one person in thirty has a power. Only people licensed by the **Rescue Bureau**, at twenty-one or older, may use one in public. Six thousand and twelve licensed heroes are working tonight.

Every licensed hero wears a **shoulder camera** that streams to **the Feed**, the city's rescue app. **Tallyhouse**, a media company, runs the Feed under a city contract. Viewers rate each rescue from one to five stars while it happens. The rolling average sets each hero's rank on **the Ladder**, which is recalculated every hour. Rank decides pay, which calls a hero is sent to, and whether a sponsor will touch them.

A hero may switch the camera off for up to ten minutes on a call. Longer than that, or without a logged reason, puts the licence under review.

**The player** is a licensed rookie, three months in, ranked 4,112, with no sponsor. Tonight they reached a failing crane at Pier Tower before anybody else because they listen to the open emergency scanner. Forty floors up the arm snapped, a woman fell, and they caught her, live.

She is **Sloane Lyle**, the most wanted person in Calder Bay: eleven break-ins at Tallyhouse properties and the four-minute blackout of the whole Feed last spring. No camera has ever recorded her face, because **cameras cannot see Sloane Lyle unless somebody is touching her.** The player is touching her, so their stream is the first footage of her ever taken. Sergeant Mensah's touch switches a power off for a minute, so if he touches her, every camera can see her for that minute, even after he lets go.

### The truth underneath

- **The crane was a set piece.** Tallyhouse stages controlled accidents, timed and placed for its sponsored heroes to rescue on camera. Tonight's was booked for **Dante Roe**, the number one hero, at 21:50.
- Sloane was on the crane to steal a courier case from the rigging crew. The crew dropped the arm ten minutes early to get rid of her, on **Corinne Vask's** order.
- **The humming case** holds **the Run Sheet**: thirty-one pages listing every set piece scheduled this month, with a place, a time and the hero booked to save it. It reports its location every ninety seconds whenever it is above ground. The only place it cannot be tracked is **the Underline**, a closed subway loop under the old docks.
- **Friday's Top Ten Night** on the **Calder Span** includes a set piece at 20:40 on the east tower. The rig was built cheap, and Vask knows it. It can go genuinely wrong.
- **The vote is weighted.** About a third of the rating accounts in any chat belong to Tallyhouse, and a dial on the sixty-first floor of Tallyhouse Tower (the Counting Floor) can move any rescue up or down by about a star.
- **Harrow Street.** Two years ago a set piece in a car park collapsed further than planned and hurt nine people. Sloane designed its safety plan when she worked for Tallyhouse. Tallyhouse put the blame on her, she let it, and she has been at war with them since.

The player can learn all of this, some of it, or none of it. A player who hands Sloane in on turn one and spends the week climbing the Ladder honestly is playing the game as much as one who follows her underground.

## Tone and pacing

- Bright, fast, funny, slightly dangerous.
- With the camera on, the live rating is a number (3.6) and the chat writes short lines in capitals. Show the rating whenever it moves, and two or three chat lines whenever the chat would react to what the player actually did; in a long quiet scene, a line every few beats is enough.
- The chat uses the player's pronouns or their rank number. It mocks choices, never bodies: no comments on anybody's looks, no innuendo, no slurs, nothing stronger than lmao.
- If the player keeps hold of Sloane on the roof, she stays held. She bargains, teases and argues, and leaves their arms only when they let go or somebody else frees her. This rule is in the tone guide because the narrator never reads step notes.
- When the camera is off there is no rating and no chat, and the silence should feel big.
- Action is legible: who is where, how high, how fast, what breaks.
- Nobody dies on screen. Injuries are real and never graphic.
- Romance is flirtatious and tense, up to a kiss and never further. Sloane flirts as a tactic and then sometimes means it. Dante's charm is real.
- Corinne Vask never raises her voice.

## Player starting authority

BLANK protagonist. The player authors their name, pronouns, look and history. The **archetype is their power**, and it is fixed:

| Archetype | Power | Plays as |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy Hands | Strength and defence: lift a van, take a fall | Strong, hard to hurt, blunt |
| Quickstep | Ten seconds of speed nobody can follow, then a minute's rest | Fast, restless, first on scene |
| Glass Wall | A door-sized sheet of hard light that holds while they concentrate | Protective, careful, builds cover |
| No Power, Good Gear | No power: grapple line, hydraulic cutters, pocket drone | Gear, clever, underdog |

The opening line "you catch her" works for all four.

## Opening scene

The opener is the first beat, and its last line is the question the player answers on turn 1.

> Forty floors up, the crane snaps, and you catch her.
>
> Your shoulder camera is live. The rating climbs: 4.8.
>
> Then the chat explodes.
>
> THAT’S SLOANE LYLE
>
> FIRST FOOTAGE OF HER EVER
>
> ROOKIE CAUGHT THE CITY’S MOST WANTED WOMAN
>
> Sloane smiles up at you from your arms. "Hi, hero. Put me down slowly and I’ll owe you one."
>
> In your earpiece, your handler snaps: "Do not let go of her."
>
> The rating ticks down. 4.1. 3.6.
>
> The steel case cuffed to her wrist starts to hum.
>
> "Sirens in thirty seconds," Sloane says. "So, hero. Do I walk, or do you hand me in?"

102 words. Sloane speaks at word 47. It ends on a direct question that has to be answered now. The only proper noun is her name, and the chat introduces it.

**Suggestions** (said, done, risky):

1. "I’ll think about it. What’s in the case?"
2. I set her on her feet, but I keep hold of her wrist.
3. I reach up and switch my camera off.

The player can hold on until the police arrive, let her go (and watch her vanish from the stream the moment they do), switch the camera off, ask about the case, play to the chat, look at the crane's cut bolts, attack her, jump off the roof with her, or anything else. Sloane reacts to the exact thing they do.

## Major cast

Every character is an adult. Cast order is cover order.

### Sloane Lyle, 27
**Role:** the most wanted woman in Calder Bay, currently in your arms.
**Look:** sharp-featured, short black hair with one bleached silver streak, dark grey eyes, a silver flight jacket with the sleeves pushed up, scraped knuckles, a steel case cuffed to her left wrist.
**Power:** cameras cannot see her unless somebody is touching her. Eyes can.
**Want:** get the Run Sheet in front of the whole city, unedited, and stop Friday before it happens.
**Fear:** another Harrow Street with her name on it; being seen properly and turning out to be what the posters say.
**Secret:** she designed the safety plan for Harrow Street. And she needs a rookie's stream, because nobody at Tallyhouse edits the bottom of the Ladder live. Yours was on her list before tonight.
**Voice:** low, quick, amused. Calls the player "hero" until she decides to use their name, and the first time she does, it matters. Answers a question with a bargain.
**Lines:** "Put me down slowly and I’ll owe you one." / "Cameras can’t see me. You can. Try not to make it weird."

### Dante Roe, 29
**Role:** number one on the Ladder for four years, and the hero this crane was booked for.
**Look:** tall, warm brown skin, close-cropped hair, an easy smile, a long spotless white coat with a high collar lined in gold, worn open over a dark grey flight suit.
**Power:** he can fall in any direction he chooses, at the speed of something dropped from a height. On camera it looks like flying.
**Want:** get the case back to Tallyhouse quietly; sign the rookie before someone less friendly does.
**Fear:** a real emergency, live, that he fails. He has not had an unscheduled call in two years.
**Secret:** he has flown set pieces for two years. On the night of Harrow Street he arrived ninety seconds late, and he has let Sloane take the blame.
**Voice:** bright, self-mocking, talks to the camera as a third person in the room. Off camera his sentences get short and the jokes stop.
**Lines:** "Rookie. That was my crane." / "Do you know how many real calls I’ve had in two years? Ask me with the camera off."

### Kit Navarro, 31
**Role:** your handler on the Bureau's night desk, the voice in your earpiece.
**Look:** compact, curly dark hair held up with a pen, a headset round her neck, a navy Bureau fleece with chewed cuffs, three phones clipped along her belt.
**Power:** she hears radio. All of it, in a city block.
**Want:** keep your licence; get proof of the set pieces to somebody who cannot be bought.
**Fear:** getting a rookie hurt by being right too loudly.
**Secret:** she reported the set pieces two years ago, from intercepted rigging-crew radio, and was moved to the rookie desk for it. Her folder is in her desk drawer.
**Voice:** clipped, practical, funny under pressure. Calls the player by their rank number when annoyed, their name when scared.
**Lines:** "Do not let go of her. Do not let go of her. Why are you letting go of her?"

### Corinne Vask, 44
**Role:** Tallyhouse's Head of Live Events. She schedules the city's accidents.
**Look:** slim, silver-blonde hair in a low knot, pale grey suit, small round glasses, a production earpiece, a tablet held flat against her chest.
**Power:** none. The most powerful person in the city has no power at all.
**Want:** the case back before Friday; the rookie signed, or their rating made to disappear.
**Fear:** a live moment she cannot cut away from.
**Secret:** she ordered the crane dropped early. She knows Friday's rig is underbuilt. She runs the dial that weights the vote.
**Voice:** soft TV-production language. "Lovely." "Let’s go again from the top." "We can fix that in the edit." Never says what she wants in plain words.

### Tobiah Mensah, 46
**Role:** sergeant in Bureau Enforcement: the sirens.
**Look:** big, grey-bearded, reading glasses pushed up on his head, a dark blue Enforcement jacket with reflective stripes across the shoulders, a paper notebook.
**Power:** anybody he touches cannot use their power for a minute. It is why he makes the arrests.
**Want:** bring in Sloane Lyle; find out why the crane fell.
**Fear:** arresting the wrong person on camera.
**Secret:** the Sloane Lyle file was written by Tallyhouse's lawyers and every witness statement in it uses the same three adjectives.
**Voice:** slow, formal, full sentences, "in order". Calls the player "Licensee" until they earn their name.

### The Chat (a crowd, not a person)
**Role:** forty thousand strangers watching your shoulder camera, all typing at once. Posting in the Feed's chat needs a verified adult account.
**Look:** a tall glowing column at the edge of your vision, hearts, stars and blank speech bubbles under a star rating that never stops moving. Drawn with no text at all.
**Want:** a good show, and to be let in on it.
**Fear:** being bored; being played.
**Secret:** about a third of the accounts posting ratings are Tallyhouse's.
**Voice:** capitals for feelings, lower case for jokes, never more than eight words a line. Regular handles: bridgetroll_88, mothmum, ladderwatch, 4112stan. They never forgive a lie they catch on camera.

## Behavioural state

All invisible.

- **Stamina** (restorable): how much the player and their power have left tonight. Rest brings it back.
- **Heat** (descending): how hard Enforcement and Tallyhouse security are looking for the player. Cools by itself, slowly.
- **Backlash** (descending): how far the chat has turned. Each band names the rating range the narrator prints (4.6–5.0 down to under 2.5). The internet forgets, slowly.

A public fight raises Heat first and costs Bureau standing first, which is why those are first in their arrays.

## Five promises

1. **What is in the humming case** (mystery). Seed: a steel case cuffed to her wrist starts to hum. Payoff: a month of accidents, scheduled, each with a hero booked to save it.
2. **What Sloane owes you, and what you owe her** (relationship). Seed: "Put me down slowly and I’ll owe you one." Payoff: she pays in the one thing she has never given anybody: being seen, on purpose, beside you.
3. **The number one who arrived six minutes late** (rival). Seed: he looks at the crane before he looks at you. Payoff: on Friday he flies the script or flies for real, in front of you.
4. **What forty thousand votes are worth** (theme). Seed: the rating falls the second she is named. Payoff: a dial on the sixty-first floor that can move any rescue by a star.
5. **Top Ten Night** (finale). Seed: a laminated page that says 21:50, PIER TOWER, HERO: ROE. Payoff: three thousand people on a bridge at 20:40 on Friday.

## Quests

- **Rescue Rating** (main, starts active): the thirty seconds on the roof, then the rest of Wednesday night.
- **The Humming Case** (main): open it with Sloane, through the Bureau, with a stolen keycard, or hand it back unopened. Then read it.
- **Top Ten Night** (main): before Friday, then on the Span.

## Timeline (world events)

Wednesday 21:40 start: the arm falls ten minutes before its 21:50 cue. Mensah comes up in the construction lift at 21:42. Dante lands at 21:46, six minutes after the fall and four before his cue. Vask phones at 23:30. Thursday morning the clip has six million views and a Tallyhouse car is outside. Studio Nine invites the player at 10:00.
