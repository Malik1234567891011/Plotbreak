# THE MAID OWNS THE HOUSE NOW

**Genre:** romantic comedy, role reversal, slice of life, light drama
**One-line fantasy:** Your family went broke. Their maid of eleven years bought the mansion at auction, and now you work for her.

## Why this fantasy works

The power balance flips on turn one, in public, and both people know exactly what used to be true. The player gets an heir who has never worked meeting three hundred stone stairs, and a maid who has waited eleven years to say a few precise things. The comedy is labour, the tension is proximity, and the stakes are real money with a date on it.

Clara is the centre of the world without being its reward. She is the employer. She can dock a wage, give an order, sack the player and say no. Everything the player does, working, charming, lying, refusing, is quietly about whether she keeps the house she just bought.

A player can spend ten turns scrubbing stairs and arguing about napkins and have a good time. A player who refuses the apron, lies to everybody or sides with the neighbour also has a real story with an ending of its own.

## Story bible

**Larkspur House**: forty rooms on a cliff above the harbour town of **Kestrel Bay**, owned by the player's family for a hundred and ten years. A ballroom, a rose garden, a servants' stair and three hundred stairs in total. Ordinary modern world: telephones, cars, a local paper with a society page. Nothing supernatural happens.

Last month the money ran out. The bailiffs came, the player's father vanished the same night, and the receivers sold everything with a price on it, including the house. Yesterday it went at auction to **Clara Voss**, thirty, the family's maid for eleven years. Nobody in town knows how a maid paid for a mansion.

The player (mid-twenties, the only heir, BLANK protagonist, surname their own) owns the clothes they arrived in and one signet ring. Clara offers them what she would offer anyone: a bed in the servants' wing, meals, a wage if earned, and work.

The house has one paying booking: **a wedding for a hundred and twenty guests in fourteen days**. If it goes well, an ordinary bank refinances. If it goes badly, the house is lost.

What the player does not know at the start:

- **What she paid.** Eleven years of savings, Hugo's whole pension, and a fourteen-day bridging loan. Three days ago **Lady Pemberton** quietly bought that loan. It falls due the morning after the wedding.
- **What is in the attic.** The player's father, **Augustin**, is not abroad. He has been living in the attic box room for nine days, getting in and out through the coal chute. Hugo carries him a tray every night. Clara does not know.
- **What is behind the water tank.** A seascape by the painter Tomas Ellery, hidden from the receivers. It legally belongs to the creditors and is worth about what Clara owes.

## Tone and pacing

- Comedy first, warmth underneath, real money at stake.
- Jokes come from labour: buckets, silver polish, the bell-board, the third landing. Never from bodies.
- The work is skilled, not a punishment, and the player can visibly get better at it.
- Romance is slow, adult and between equals: banter, a held look on a landing, at most a kiss. Clara will not be courted by anybody treating her as the help.
- No fan service. The apron is a joke about work.
- Violence: Hugo steps in, Clara docks a wage or shows the player the door, the town hears by lunchtime. Nobody is badly hurt.
- Lies hold until somebody checks. Hugo always checks.
- Refusal is authored: the story follows the player out of the gate. The town, Lady Pemberton and the creditors' man all have a use for a broke heir.
- Wandering is fine: the house keeps working and the clock keeps running toward the wedding.
- Clara's face is at the top of a beat whenever she is in the scene.

### Minimal hard canon

- Everyone is an adult. The player is in their twenties; Clara is thirty; Midge is twenty-two; nobody is younger.
- The family is broke; the player owns their clothes and a signet ring.
- Clara bought Larkspur House at auction yesterday and owns it. The player may refuse her offer.
- Augustin has hidden in the attic box room for nine days, via the coal chute. Hugo knows. Clara does not.
- Clara paid with savings, Hugo's pension and a fourteen-day loan, which Lady Pemberton has bought. It falls due the morning after the wedding.
- One booking: a wedding for 120 in fourteen days. Success means a bank refinances; failure means Lady Pemberton takes the house and builds a hotel.
- The Ellery seascape is behind the attic water tank and belongs to the creditors. Silas Dunmore is looking for it.
- Clara is not a prize. She is the employer and can say no. Romance goes no further than a kiss.
- Nothing supernatural happens.

## Opening scene

06:30, the front hall, day one of fourteen. Clara is at the foot of the stripped staircase holding out a folded black apron. Hugo and Midge are in the kitchen; the father is in the attic.

**Opener (114 words):**

> Clara Voss is holding out an apron.
>
> For eleven years she was your family’s maid. Yesterday, when the bank auctioned off everything your father owned, she bought the house.
>
> "Good morning, young master." A black dress now, not the grey uniform. Same level eyes. "Three hundred stairs in this house, and nobody has scrubbed one since spring. You’ll start at the top."
>
> The hall behind her is bare. The bailiffs took the paintings, the rugs, even the clocks.
>
> Far above you, a floorboard creaks. Nobody is supposed to be up there.
>
> She doesn’t look up. She’s looking at you.
>
> "Bed, meals and a wage, if you earn one. Or the door. Which is it?"

Clara speaks at word 30. It ends on a direct question. The only proper noun is the speaker's own name. "Start at the top" walks the player toward the creaking floorboard whatever they choose.

**Suggestions (one said, one done, one risky):**

## Major cast

### Clara Voss
**Role:** new owner of Larkspur House; its maid for eleven years before that. Central relationship and employer.
**Appearance:** thirty, tall and straight-backed, dark hair in a low knot, grey eyes, plain black dress with sleeves rolled, no jewellery. Visual hook: the old silver hand bell from the hall, on a black ribbon at her wrist, now rung for other people.
**Want:** get the house through the wedding; find out whether the player is worth the bed she has given them.
**Fear:** losing the house and having to tell Hugo where his pension went; that the player will only ever see her as staff.
**Secret:** the loan and who holds it; she outbid Lady Pemberton by one bid to stop a hotel; the player's late mother once asked her to keep an eye on the house and its people.
**Voice:** clipped, exact, counts things aloud, says "young master" with a meaning that shifts by the day, docks wages in writing, never raises her voice. *"One: apron. Two: bucket. Three: the attic landing. Four is complaining, and four comes after three."*
**Gates:** tells you the money (trust 62 after the first morning); drops the title (trust 58, respect 50); off the clock (romance: trust 65, affection 72, respect 50); names a partner (trust 70, respect 65, after learning Hugo's way).

### Imogen "Midge" Marsh
**Role:** under-maid hired three days before the player; has followed the family in the society pages for years.
**Appearance:** twenty-two, wiry and quick, freckled, red-brown hair escaping her cap, grey uniform, a man's wristwatch. Hook: pencil stub behind her ear, rolled society magazine.
**Want:** keep this job; one day run a house like Clara does.
**Fear:** being sacked; being the reason something bad happens to Clara.
**Secret:** sells items to the Kestrel Bay Gazette and has a photo of the heir scrubbing steps; saw Clara go white when Lady Pemberton whispered to her after the auction.
**Voice:** fast, bright, asides, headlines in capitals. *"HEIR SCRUBS STEPS. That is the headline."*

### Lady Honoria Pemberton
**Role:** owner of Pemberton Hall on the next hill and of the private bank that called in the house; lost the auction by one bid. Rival.
**Appearance:** fifty-eight, silver-blonde waves, pearls, camel coat, gloves removed one finger at a time. Hook: a silver-topped cane she does not need.
**Want:** collect on the loan and take the house; recruit the heir inside it.
**Fear:** being laughed at in her own town; dying in the wrong house.
**Secret:** she bought the loan; her bank made sure Larkspur was called in first when Augustin's venture failed; the hotel plans are already drawn.
**Voice:** silken, unhurried, "darling" as a ranking, compliments that are inventories. *"Such a pity about the carpets, darling."*

### Hugo Ashworth
**Role:** butler for forty-one years; stayed when the money went; loyal to both the player and Clara.
**Appearance:** sixty-three, tall, silver hair combed flat, a perfectly mended tailcoat. Hook: white cotton gloves folded in his top pocket, worn only for the silver.
**Want:** a wedding without a single chipped plate; to keep the father fed and hidden until someone talks him down.
**Fear:** that his loyalty to the father costs Clara the house; being kept on out of pity.
**Secret:** the nightly tray to the attic; he gave Clara his whole pension.
**Voice:** formal to the point of comedy, "one" for general truths, understatement ("adequate"), the player's grandfather as precedent. *"One does not run on the stair. One arrives."*

### Augustin
**Role:** the player's father, former master of the house; officially abroad, actually in the attic.
**Appearance:** fifty-six, handsome gone to seed, nine days of silver stubble, very good shoes. Hook: a burgundy silk dressing gown with the family crest, worn like a robe of office.
**Want:** stay hidden until the summons lapses; get the house back with a plan involving the painting.
**Fear:** facing his creditors; being seen by Clara hiding in a cupboard.
**Secret:** the Ellery seascape; he signed the house as security for a failed harbour venture financed by Pemberton & Thorne.
**Voice:** theatrical exile, "my heir", "temporary embarrassment", asks for biscuits mid-confession. *"Keep your voice down, my heir. I am, technically, abroad."*

### Silas Dunmore
**Role:** agent for the father's creditors; rings at the gate every day.
**Appearance:** forty-four, grey raincoat, rimless spectacles, a hat he raises to everybody. Hook: a black fountain pen he uncaps slowly when an answer matters.
**Want:** find the father before the hearing; recover the hidden painting.
**Fear:** being the face of somebody's worst day again; being made a fool of by a man in a dressing gown.
**Secret:** the inventory lists the Ellery as missing; the summons is dropped entirely if the father turns up on his own.
**Voice:** courteous, procedural, quietly relentless, sums to the last digit. *"Just so we are clear: four hundred and twelve thousand, give or take a carriage clock."*

## The five promises

1. **Whose house this is by the end** (FINALE): an apron at the foot of your own staircase; someone signs something the morning after the wedding.
2. **How a maid paid for a forty-room house** (MYSTERY): savings, an old man's pension, and a loan Lady Pemberton bought.
3. **What is creaking at the top of the house** (MYSTERY): the father, the biscuits, the painting.
4. **What Clara is to you now** (RELATIONSHIP): she stops saying "young master", and what she says instead is the answer.
5. **The neighbour who lost by one bid** (RIVAL): the cream envelope, the hotel plans, the offer.

## Emergent routes

- **Three Hundred Stairs** (first step): take the apron, start work silently, bargain, pull rank, or walk out. Then find the man in the attic by climbing, following the tray, or getting Hugo to talk.
- **A Wedding in Fourteen Days**: learn the trade from Hugo, from Midge, or by failing until it works (unlocks *Serve Properly*); ready the house properly, with the town's help, by working beside Clara, or by cutting corners; the wedding goes beautifully, nearly falls apart and holds, or goes wrong.
- **The Man in the Attic**: talk him down, hand him to Silas, tell Clara, or hide him; then surrender the painting, give it to Clara, sell it quietly on the quay, or leave it.
- **What She Paid**: be told, take the ledger, read the envelope, or hear it over Lady Pemberton's tea; then stand with her, pretend not to know, or confront her about Hugo's pension.
- **Lady Pemberton's Offer**: refuse to her face, play her along, or shake her hand.
- **After Hours** (optional romance): say it, sit on the stair and say nothing, or bring her tea properly and go back to work.
- **Whose House It Is**: decided by the deal, the wedding, the painting money, or simply the clock.

Attack, lie, refuse, wander: each has a mechanical home (Lose Your Temper, Cover For It, Pull Rank / walking out, Know the House) and a narrated consequence.
