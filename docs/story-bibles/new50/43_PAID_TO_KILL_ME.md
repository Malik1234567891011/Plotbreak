## Why this fantasy works

The fantasy lands in the first beat. A dangerous, competent man was paid to end the player's life, came into their room to do it, and sat down in the chair instead. Everything he does after that is weighed against what he was hired for. When he tastes the coffee before handing it over, the player can read it as care or as a professional protecting his own job.

The romance rests on Kael being a professional, not a prize. His kindness is logistics: he moves the chair away from the window, checks the lock twice and walks on the street side. He will not explain himself until he trusts the player more than he trusts himself. The player learns what refusing cost him before they learn why he refused, so the payoff arrives in two steps.

The structure under it is two attempts on one life by two people who do not know about each other:

- **The glass** is a short, physical whodunnit inside the house.
- **The contract** is a longer one that leads out into the city.

The player can solve either one without the other. A player who never asks a single question still has a story: a bodyguard, a deadline and a bell.

Every character is an adult. The content ceiling is 13+, and the romance goes as far as a kiss.

## Story bible

**The city.** Saltmere is a cold harbour city where three old shipping families still run the docks, the clubs and the gossip: **Ravelle**, **Vauclair** and **Thorne**.

**The death.** The player's grandmother, **Odile Ravelle**, built the Ravelle Line. She died in her sleep twelve days ago, at eighty-eight. Her heart gave out naturally. Dr. Sato signed the certificate, and it is true.

**The will.** It leaves the controlling share of the Ravelle Line to the player.
- The share vests when the player signs at the board meeting at Ravelle Tower on **Friday at ten**.
- If the player dies before then, it passes to their uncle **Viktor**.
- If they simply do not turn up, the board votes Viktor in as chair.

**The bodyguard.** The week of the funeral, Viktor hired a security agency on Lady Ines's recommendation. Through it, **Kael** was placed in the house as the player's bodyguard. The agency is a front. Kael is a contract operator for **the Bellhouse**, the city's quiet brokerage for killing. He was handed a contract on the player's life, to be done before the signing.

**Last night.** Kael came into the room to do the job and did not. When he sat down he saw that the water glass on the nightstand had gone cloudy. Somebody else had already tried. He stayed in the chair until morning.

**The two culprits.** Neither knows about the other.

1. **Viktor poured the glass.**
   - He crushed his mother's heart tablets into the bedside carafe in the pantry at eleven last night, so the death would look like the family heart.
   - He owes harbour lenders the price of a small ship, borrowed against a share he was sure he would inherit.
   - The day after the funeral he signed a letter promising to sell that share to House Vauclair.
2. **Dr. Mirei Sato paid for the knife.**
   - On Odile's last night, the old woman confessed the truth about **Pier Nine** to her.
   - Twenty-two years ago a fire in the Ravelle warehouse on Pier Nine killed eleven dockworkers. Odile had ordered the rear fire doors chained at night against theft, and had paid an inspector to sign them off. The company blamed a worker's cigarette.
   - Sato's husband, Haruto, a crane foreman, was the sixth name on the plaque.
   - In the worst week of her life, Sato paid the Bellhouse to end the Ravelle line before it could inherit the lie. She paid with the money Odile left her for thirty years of care.
   - She has regretted it every hour since. She cannot make herself walk into the clock shop and cancel, because cancelling means saying it out loud.

**The Bellhouse.** It runs from behind a clock-repair shop in Old Town. Its rules are painted on the back wall:
- An operator rings a bell to close a job.
- Only the client, in person, can cancel a contract.
- **Rule four:** any contract can be bought out at twice its price.
- An operator who breaks a contract is closed along with it.

A bell with no clapper, left for a late operator, means: ring yours, or be rung.

**The clock.** The story opens on **Tuesday at 06:40** (`startWorldMinute` 400). The signing is on **Friday at 10:00** (world minute 4920). The opening obligation is to be in the boardroom with Pell, alive.

## Tone and pacing

- A romantic thriller with a dry sense of humour, set in a cliff-top house and a cold harbour city in the rain.
- Physical detail carries the story: a black-handled knife, a cloudy glass, a chair turned to face the door, a brass bell with no clapper.
- Kael shows care as logistics and never says it out loud. His jokes land flat and late.
- Ordinary life keeps going around the danger. Breakfast is laid whether anybody eats it, and the housekeeper still wants to know about the lilies.
- Romance is flirtatious, tense and heartfelt, and goes as far as a kiss. Never sexual, and never lingering on bodies.
- Violence is quick, legible and never graphic: a wrist caught, a door slammed, a blade that stops short. There is no gore. Poison is a cloudy glass and a bitter smell, never symptoms.
- Nobody is simply a villain. Viktor is frightened, Sato is grieving, and Ines would rather lose than win dirty. The player decides what each of them deserves.
- Kael's face sits at the top of any beat he is in.

## Major cast

Every character is an adult, and each `appearance` opens on their age.

### Kael · 27
**Role:** bodyguard of nine days, and the Bellhouse operator paid to kill you before Friday. No surname: "Kael. Just Kael."
**Appearance:**
- Tall and lean, black hair cut short and badly by himself.
- Grey eyes that rest on doors before people.
- A plain dark suit that fits like it was bought for moving in.
- A thin white scar through his left eyebrow.

**Wants:** keep the player alive until Friday at ten, and find out who placed the contract before the Bellhouse sends somebody better.
**Fears:** that he only stopped because he was tired; and Vesna, who trained him.
**Secrets:**
1. **The cost.** This was meant to be his last job: the fee was the price of his name coming out of the Bellhouse ledger. By refusing it he became a closed contract himself. He tells the player if they ask what it cost him, not why he did it.
2. **The reason.** On his fourth night in the house, the player made two cups of tea at three in the morning and left one outside his door without knocking. Nobody had ever done that.
3. **The card.** It knew the side-gate code and the balcony door, so the client has been inside the house.

**Voice:** short statements, and professional words for personal things: window, exit, sightline, clean. His jokes arrive flat and late.
- "Two people want you dead and only one of them could afford me."
- "Sit there. Not there. That chair has a window behind it."
- "Drink this. I already did. If I fall over, don't."

**Boundaries:**
- Will not lie about what he is.
- Will not hurt anyone in front of the player unless they are about to hurt the player.
- Will never hurt the player.
- Will not be thanked.

**Gates:**
- He tells you the cost: trust 45.
- He tells you why: trust 60, once you know the cost.
- He lets you closer: a romance gate at trust 65 and affection 70.

### Viktor Ravelle · 54
**Role:** your uncle, acting chair of the Ravelle Line for twenty years, and the heir if you die before Friday.
**Appearance:** broad and handsome going soft, silver hair swept back, a navy blazer with a yacht-club pin. He twists his gold signet ring when he lies.
**Motive:** debt to harbour lenders, and a signed promise to sell to the Vauclairs. He poured the glass. The empty bottle is still in his navy coat in the west wing.
**Voice:** big, warm and theatrical. He calls the player "my dear" and talks in sailing metaphors. When he lies he adds detail; when he tells the truth he gets shorter.
- "My dear. You look dreadful. Sit, eat something, let your old uncle worry for once."
- "Sign the proxy and you never set foot in that boardroom. Following wind, all the way home."
- "It was meant to be gentle. Like hers. Asleep, and then not."

**Confesses:** only to kindness, or to evidence he cannot talk past. Never to anyone holding a weapon.

### Lady Ines Vauclair · 26
**Role:** heir of House Vauclair. Your best friend through four years of college, and your rival ever since graduation, when she told her father a secret you had trusted her with.
**Appearance:** sleek black bob, sharp dark eyes, a tailored cream coat, gloves taken off one finger at a time. She wears a single pearl earring, left ear only.
**Motive:** buying the Ravelle Line is the only move big enough to stop her father passing House Vauclair to her younger brother.
**Secrets:**
- She holds Viktor's letter of intent and hands it over if the player asks her straight whether she wanted them dead, and believes her.
- She recommended the security agency without knowing what it was.

**Voice:** bright and faultlessly polite. "Darling" is a weapon. She says cruel things as compliments and kind things as insults.
- "Your bodyguard has looked at every exit in this room and at me twice. I am choosing to be flattered."
- "Ask me. Properly. Ask me if I wanted you dead, and look at me while I answer."

**Boundary:** she will not buy anything over a body. The moment her offer gives anybody a reason to hurt the player, she withdraws it.

### Dr. Mirei Sato · 63 (called "Sato")
**Role:** your grandmother's doctor for thirty years, and the only one with her on her last night.
**Appearance:** small and upright, grey hair in a low knot, half-moon glasses on a chain. She wears her husband's steel wristwatch on the inside of her wrist, stopped at twenty past two.
**Motive:** Pier Nine. She is the client.
**Pacing rule:** she confesses only to someone who already knows about Pier Nine and comes without a threat.
**Will not lie about medicine.** Shown the glass, she names it exactly: her own patient's heart tablets, crushed, a great many. No drug name and no dose ever appear on the page.
**Voice:** soft and exact. Clinical words for frightening things, plain words for kind ones. She always asks how you slept first.
- "Wrist, please. Quick pulse. Did you sleep, or did you lie very still and think?"
- "She asked me to hold her hand and listen. So I listened. I have been listening ever since."
- "You should be frightened of me. Please. It would be easier."

### Vesna · 31
**Role:** the Bellhouse's closer, who trained Kael and is sent when a contract runs late. Vesna is not her real name, and only Kael knows the real one.
**Appearance:** freckled, a cloud of copper curls pinned up, a gap-toothed smile, and a caterer's white jacket or a yellow raincoat. A silent brass bell hangs on a black ribbon at her wrist.
**Motive:** close the contract before Friday, and give Kael one chance to come home by finishing it himself.
**Voice:** sing-song and cheerful, full of nicknames: pet, sunshine, heir apparent. She talks shop like gossip. She drops the cheer for exactly one sentence when she means it.
- "Hello, heir apparent. Lovely house. Terrible locks."
- "Rules are rules, pet. Rule four says twice the price and the bell stays quiet. Nobody ever reads rule four."
- "Midnight is late. Ten on Friday is very late. After that, I ring."

**Boundary:** never hurts bystanders or staff, because it is unprofessional. She will tell the player about rule four for the fun of watching them try.

### Augustin Pell · 68 (called "Pell")
**Role:** executor of the will and keeper of the signing. He is incorruptible.
**Appearance:** tall and thin as a folded umbrella, a three-piece grey suit, and a black lacquered fountain pen kept for one signature only.
**Secret:** he has the key to the library cabinet holding the Pier Nine file. Odile told him to give it only once the player had opened her letter and asked for it.
**Voice:** formal, with exact clause numbers. He puts his jokes in footnotes.
- "Clause nine is perfectly clear. Should you die before ten o'clock on Friday, Mr. Ravelle inherits. I would therefore advise you, in a strictly personal capacity, not to."
- "Forty-one years I drew up her papers. She never once read the footnotes. I put the good jokes in the footnotes."

## Emergent routes

**Change of Plans** (turn one). Keep him on, have him thrown out, take the knife and make him answer, or just hear him out. All four are valid, and Kael hands over the contract card in every version. Then find out what was in the glass, in one of three ways:
- show it to Sato;
- find the gap in Odile's pill row;
- ask the kitchen who touched the carafe.

**Who Poured the Glass.** Prove it was Viktor in one of three ways:
- confront him with the bottle;
- be kind until he confesses;
- let him think the first attempt worked.

Then decide what happens to him: call the police, send him into exile, or keep his secret.

**Who Paid for the Knife.** First find the Bellhouse: Kael takes you, the bell leads you there, or the card does. Finding it unlocks **Buy the Contract**. Then name the client:
- Sato tells you herself;
- you read her name in the Bellhouse ledger;
- you put the daybook and the card side by side.

**The Bell on the Gate.** There are four ways to silence it:
1. The client cancels in person.
2. Buy it out under rule four.
3. Stage your death with Kael's help.
4. Stop Vesna yourselves.

**The Man Who Did Not.** Three steps:
1. **What it cost him:** ask him, see his room, or hear it from Vesna. Learning it unlocks **Stage Your Death**.
2. **Why he did not do it:** he says it, you say it first, or you keep it professional.
3. **After Friday:** hire him for real, take the second ticket, or let him go.

**Read Before You Sign.** Open the Pier Nine file: Pell gives you the key, you break the glass, or you hear it from the families at the pier. Then decide what the company owes: say it in public, pay quietly, or lock it away again.

**Lady Ines's Offer** (side quest). Ask her straight, outplay her, or take her money. Then stand with her on Friday, or beat her.

**Friday at Ten.** The possible outcomes:
- sign;
- sign and tell the board about Pier Nine;
- sign it over to somebody else;
- sign Viktor's proxy beforehand;
- let ten o'clock pass.
