# AFFECTION: 97

**Story #44 of the fifty-story slate.** Slug `affection-97`, export `AFFECTION_97`, asset prefix `story_affection_97`.
**Genre:** court romance, romantic comedy, light intrigue
**Trope:** a status window on the love interest. Female lean.
**OOC evidence:** I Can See the Unconquerable Mastermind's Status Window, 122K (female #6). Nothing is borrowed from it but the genre shape: no title, character, setting or mechanic is copied.
**You play:** a lady-in-waiting at a royal court. Blank protagonist: the player names herself.
**One-line fantasy:** The cold crown prince says he despises you. The little window of light over his head says Affection 97, and it went up when he said it.

## Why this fantasy works

The player starts with the answer every romance withholds: she knows he loves her. That flips the genre's question from *does he?* to *why is he acting like this, and what will he do about it?* The comedy writes itself (every cutting line he delivers can tick the number up), and the tension is real, because the number tells her how he feels and never tells her what he will choose. He can love her and still give his word to someone else in nine days.

It is built on Good Morning, Husband's shape: one relationship at the centre, a small cast who each want something specific this fortnight, and ordinary scenes (a dance card, a map table at two in the morning, doing a bride's hair) that are worth playing on their own.

Two things keep the number from making the story easy:

- **It shows one person.** There is no window over Sir Bastien, who is warm, flirts in public and might mean it. With Aldric the player has proof. With everybody else she has to trust, which is the whole of the theme.
- **It closes.** The first time Aldric says out loud, to her face, what the window says, it goes out for good. Getting what she wants costs her the advantage.

## Story bible

You are a lady-in-waiting at Rosewall Palace, the court of Halcyne, a small kingdom of river towns and vineyards. You have served Queen Ottilie for eleven months: her letters, her errands, her secrets. Her son, Crown Prince Aldric, has spoken perhaps forty words to you in that time, most of them cutting.

This morning you woke and found a small frame of pale light hanging a hand's width above his head. Only you can see it. Its first line reads **[Affection 97 ▲]**. This afternoon he sent everyone else out of the music room and told you he despises you, and the arrow went up.

What the player does not know at the start, and can find out:

- **Why he lies.** Two springs ago Aldric was openly fond of the court harpist, Maren Lisle, his only real friend at court. Within a month the Queen's sapphire brooch was found in Maren's music case and she was put in a carriage at dawn without a hearing. The Queen planted it, to protect a marriage. Aldric suspects and cannot prove it. Since then he has been cold in public to everybody he likes, and coldest to the player.
- **Why the marriage matters.** King Edmar died three winters ago after a border war the crown paid for with borrowed money. House Ashgrove, the kingdom's great banking family, holds the debt. Aldric has been promised since he was nineteen to Lady Viola Ashgrove; on their wedding the debt becomes her dowry and is cancelled. At the Midsummer Rite, nine days away, he gives his word in public and it cannot be taken back. Without that marriage the northern garrisons go unpaid by autumn.
- **Why the window exists.** Last night Aldric climbed the wall of the walled Sunken Garden and dropped a royal coin into its dry fountain, which the palace's old story says grants one honest wish a generation, and wished that the person he could not tell would know anyway. He does not know it worked. This is an optional mystery, like Platform 11 in Good Morning, Husband: it exists only for a player who goes looking.

The player is free to call his bluff, obey him to the letter, tell him about the number, use it to steer him, help his bride escape him, take the Queen's side, choose the knight instead, expose the Queen, run away with him, or leave court alone.

The story succeeds if a player can spend ten turns at the Rose Ball and the morning after doing nothing but talking to people, and still enjoy it, because the number keeps moving and every person she talks to wants something.

## The Window (rules the storyteller never breaks)

- A small frame of pale light, a hand's width above Aldric's head, whenever the player can see him. Only she can see it. Nobody else has one; she has looked at everybody. Mirrors and paintings do not show it, and it cannot be touched.
- First line: `[Affection NN ▲]` or `[Affection NN ▼]`, the arrow showing the last change. It starts at 97.
- **It is always true and it never explains.** It shows how he feels about her right now. It never says why, and never predicts what he will do. He can be at 99 and still walk away.
- It moves by one or two for small things (a look, a joke, his name said by accident) and by more for large ones. It can fall, and fall a long way: being used, lied to or humiliated in public costs him, and it shows.
- Under the number is a second line, greyed out like ink under water. More lines can sharpen over the fortnight: `[Thinking of: …]` after the first time they are alone together after the ball; `[Afraid of: …]` once the player knows about Maren. The grey line becomes readable only at the fountain, and reads `[Wished for: this]`.
- **It closes for good the first time Aldric says out loud, to her face, what it says.**
- In prose it appears as a short bracketed line on its own, exactly like `[Affection 98 ▲]`, only when the number changes or the player looks. Never more than twice in a beat.

## Tone and pacing

- Romantic comedy at a court: quick, warm, specific, with real stakes under it.
- The comedy is the gap between what Aldric says and what the number does. Let him be cutting; let the arrow undercut him.
- The court is small and it talks. Consequences are social and they stick: a dance is news by breakfast, a raised voice is news by noon, and the Queen hears everything by supper.
- Romance is flirtatious, tense and heartfelt, and goes no further than a kiss, asked for. A kiss in public is a scandal; a kiss in private is a secret somebody may learn.
- Nobody is a pure villain. The Queen did a cruel thing for a real reason. Viola is using the player and says so. Bastien began as a decoy and stopped.
- Violence at court is a crime: anybody who strikes someone in the palace is arrested by the guard and dismissed.

## Player starting authority

The player authors her name, her pronouns, her family (one of four backgrounds), her appearance and her temperament. Hard canon:

- she is twenty-three and has been a lady-in-waiting in the Queen's household for eleven months;
- she has been assigned to attend Lady Viola for the betrothal fortnight;
- she has a small shared history with Aldric: once, at two in the morning in the Map Library, he told her she was standing in his light and then did not move to another table; in the winter she corrected his pronunciation of a northern town in front of the council secretary, and he has called her "the expert" since; somebody has been leaving books of northern poetry on her window seat in the Long Gallery since the winter, and she does not know who;
- the window appeared this morning.

## Opening scene (the opener: 112 words)

> The crown prince, Aldric, has sent everyone out of the music room except you, the newest of his mother's ladies-in-waiting.
>
> He doesn't sit. He stands by the cold fireplace in black gloves, looking at you like an appointment he regrets.
>
> "I despise you," he says. "I want that understood before the ball tonight."
>
> Since this morning, a small frame of light has hung above his head. Nobody else can see it. You've checked.
>
> [Affection 97 ▲]
>
> The arrow went up when he said it.
>
> "You'll keep to the far end of the ballroom. You won't speak to me. You won't dance with me."
>
> [Affection 98 ▲]
>
> His jaw sets. "Do you understand?"

Checks: a named character speaks to the player at word 41; it ends on a direct question that needs an answer now; 112 words; the only proper noun is Aldric, introduced as the crown prince.

Three suggestions, three different moves:

- **Said:** "Perfectly, Your Highness. May I ask what I did to earn it?"
- **Done:** I curtsy, then take one step closer instead of back, and watch the number.
- **Risky:** I point at the air above his head. "Your Highness, there's a number up there, and it just went up."

Aldric reacts to the exact move. Telling him about the window is a real branch: he does not believe it, then tests it, then is mortified, and the number does something on each step.

## Cast

Every character is an adult. Ages are stated here and in the fixture's hard canon.

### Crown Prince Aldric, 25
**Role:** the love interest. Heir to the throne, promised to Viola, colder to the player than to anybody.
**Want:** get through the fortnight without anybody seeing where he looks; find a way to pay the northern garrisons that does not need him married by Midsummer.
**Fear:** that his mother will do to the player what she did to Maren; that breaking the betrothal loses the north.
**Secrets:** he made the wish; he writes to Maren every second month, unsigned, and his last letter was about the player; he leaves the poetry books.
**Limits:** will not say he loves her while he believes it puts her in danger; will not break his word to Viola behind her back; will not let anyone be punished for him again.
**Voice:** short, formal, cold sentences with no contractions when he is lying, and contractions the moment he forgets himself. Insults that are compliments if you listen. Uses her name only by accident.
> "Your handwriting is offensively legible. Nobody at this court writes like that. It is very distracting."

### Lady Viola Ashgrove, 24
**Role:** the intended bride, whom the player attends this fortnight. Rival on paper, possible ally in fact.
**Want:** out of the betrothal without the crown defaulting or the blame landing on her, and a seat on the Ashgrove board that her father refuses a daughter.
**Fear:** being married and shelved, as her mother was.
**Secret:** her plan. If the prince publicly chooses somebody else before Midsummer, she will release him and turn the crown's debt into a forty-year loan, in exchange for the board seat, which her father cannot refuse in front of the court. She needs somebody for him to choose.
**Limits:** will not be made a fool of in public; will not hurt the crown's finances out of spite.
**Voice:** fast, amused, commercial: terms, interest, collateral, a bad bet. Asks a question and answers it herself.
> "Let us not waste the morning. You want him, he wants you, I want neither of you married to me. That is a deal with three happy parties, which is rarer than a comet."

### Sir Bastien Roche, 27
**Role:** Aldric's sworn knight, oldest friend and captain of the palace guard. The romance with no number.
**Want:** keep the court looking at him instead of at Aldric until Midsummer; find out whether the player could look at him the way she looks at the prince.
**Fear:** choosing between his friend and what he wants; that she will learn it began as an order and never believe the rest.
**Secrets:** a month ago Aldric asked him to pay the player public attention so the court would think the interest was Bastien's. It stopped being a favour. He also carries Aldric's money and letters to Maren.
**Limits:** will not betray Aldric's confidence, even to win her; will not draw a sword at court except to protect someone.
**Voice:** warm, teasing, unhurried; answers in stories about horses and soldiers, with the answer somewhere in the middle. Calls her "my lady".
> "Ask me anything you like. I will tell you a story about a mule, and somewhere in the middle of the mule there will be an answer."

### Queen Ottilie, 51
**Role:** Queen Regent, the player's employer, and the reason Aldric is cold.
**Want:** the betrothal sealed at Midsummer; to know exactly what her son feels about her newest lady-in-waiting.
**Fear:** unpaid garrisons and a revolt in the north; leaving her son a bankrupt crown.
**Secrets:** she planted the brooch (it is in her jewel book in her own hand); her heart is failing and she has perhaps two years, which she has told nobody.
**Limits:** will not let the marriage fail while she has any means to stop it; never raises her voice or her hand; will not deny the brooch to someone holding the proof.
**Voice:** soft, slow, warm and without give. Domestic metaphors for statecraft. Calls everybody "my dear". Gives orders as invitations.
> "A kingdom is a household with a leaking roof, and the Ashgroves own the slates. One does not insult the slater."

### Lady Cressida "Cress" Fane, 29
**Role:** senior lady-in-waiting, the player's roommate and friend, and the Queen's informant among her ladies.
**Want:** keep her mother's pension and her own conscience through Midsummer; know whom the player will choose before anyone else.
**Fear:** being found out by the women she reports on.
**Secrets:** a blue notebook of reports under a floorboard; the Queen has asked her to watch the player, and later to hide Viola's pearl comb in the player's trunk. She has not decided whether she will.
**Limits:** will not lie to the Queen outright, only leave things out; will not hurt the player.
**Voice:** rapid gossip, lists, nicknames for everyone (the prince is "the Icicle", Viola "the Ledger", Bastien "Sir Smiles", the Queen "Herself"); calls the player "pet"; drops the important fact mid-sentence.
> "Herself asked about you this morning, by the way, pass me the pins, which she never does about anyone dull."

### Maren Lisle, 31
**Role:** the harpist who was sent away. Teaches music in a rented room on Lantern Street in the lower town, a mile from the palace gate.
**Want:** the theft off her name, so she can play in public again; for Aldric to stop paying for what his mother did.
**Fear:** being dragged back into palace business; that the next person will have it worse.
**Secrets:** she has eleven unsigned letters from Aldric, the newest about someone who reads in the gallery window; years ago he told her the fountain story and said he was saving his wish.
**Limits:** will not set foot in the palace unless her name is cleared in public; shows the letters only to someone who says why she wants them.
**Voice:** low, plain, short sentences; music words used straight (tuning, a rest, a key change). Never bitter on purpose.
> "A rest is still part of the music. People forget that. He has been playing nothing but rests for two years."

## Places

All inside or just outside Rosewall Palace; every place has a way in.

- **The Blue Music Room** (start): a harp nobody plays since Maren, a piano, tall windows, a cold fireplace.
- **The Long Gallery**: the hub. Portraits, draughts, and the window seat where the player reads and the poetry books appear.
- **The Ladies' Gallery**: where the Queen's ladies sleep two to a room. The player shares with Cress.
- **The Rose Ballroom** and **the Moon Terrace** outside it.
- **The Queen's Gardens**, and through a locked wall, **the Sunken Garden** with its dry fountain (opens once the player knows to look).
- **The Ashgrove Suite**: Viola's guest rooms.
- **The Queen's Solar**: her study, her desk, her jewel book.
- **The Map Library**: Aldric's real home, lamps lit till three.
- **The Rose Chapel**: where the Midsummer Rite happens.
- **The Tiltyard**: Bastien's guard and the sword practice.
- **The Postern Gate**: the small gate out, after dark.
- **Lantern Street**: the lower town, Maren's music room above a chandler's.

## Five promises

1. **Whether he ever says it himself** (finale). Seed: the arrow going up as he says "I despise you". Payoff: he says it to her face, and the window goes out.
2. **Why he pushes you away** (relationship). Seed: he is colder to her than to anyone. Payoff: Maren, the brooch, and the Queen's own handwriting in the jewel book.
3. **The bride who doesn't want him** (rival). Seed: Viola watches the prince watching the player. Payoff: Viola's offer behind a locked door, with terms.
4. **Where the window came from** (mystery). Seed: a grey line under the number that won't come clear. Payoff: a dry fountain, a royal coin, and `[Wished for: this]`.
5. **Knowing without a number** (theme). Seed: Bastien, with no window over his head. Payoff: the player decides whether to trust someone without being told the answer.

## Endings

Eleven, using every rarity. Several are losses, and none is written as a punishment.

- **Out Loud** (common): he says it; the window closes; they face the crown question together.
- **Ninety-Seven, Forever** (common): the betrothal is sealed; she stays at court; the number never falls and he never says it.
- **Sent Home Like the Last One** (common): she is dismissed, for a comb in her trunk, a scene in public, or telling the court she sees numbers.
- **No Number Over His Head** (uncommon): she chooses Bastien, on trust.
- **The Coach at Dawn** (uncommon): she leaves court on her own. Reachable early.
- **The Number Falls** (uncommon): he finds out she used the window to steer him.
- **The Midsummer Upset** (rare): the betrothal broken with Viola's terms, the debt survivable, and the prince courting her in public.
- **The North Road** (rare): he gives up the crown and they leave together.
- **The Ashgrove Accord** (rare): she and Viola win; she goes to work for the bank; the romance is left open.
- **The Queen's Left Hand** (rare): she takes the Queen's side and keeps the window as a tool of state.
- **Small Mercies** (unique): she learns he made the wish, and makes her own at the fountain.
