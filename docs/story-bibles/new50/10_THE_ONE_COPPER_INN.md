## The pitch

A month ago you walked out of the fog on a mountain road in the clothes you wore to work, into a
valley that has never heard of your world. They call people like you strays, feed them, and expect
them to settle. Yesterday you settled: the town's Reeve sold you a roofless coaching inn on the hill
for one copper, because nobody else would take on the back taxes, the roof, or the old charter.

At midnight on your first night, a soaked woman with horns walks in out of the rain, puts a dragon
scale on your counter, and asks for one room. The scale is worth more than the town. There are
hunters' torches on the hill behind her.

Slow life with a pulse: every job you do on the house (a patched roof, a lit range, a paid supper) is
also a move in the hunt, because an old law only protects a guest in a real, working inn.

## Playable span

Seven nights at the inn and the valley below it, from midnight on day 0 (the opener) to first light on
the seventh morning, when the wound has closed and she can fly. The inspection at noon on the third
day is the spine. The cage wagon on the fourth day makes "taken alive" concrete. Everything after the
seventh dawn lives in the endings.

The span is small on purpose: one ruin, one yard, one neighbour, one town, one camp of polite hunters,
and a guest who needs a week. Thirty turns spent fixing one roof and feeding six drovers is a real way
to play it.

## The world, in plain words

- **The valley.** A river valley under mountains. The market town at the bottom is **Brindle Ford**:
  four hundred people, a stone market cross, a bridge with a toll house, a tavern called the Drowned
  Ox. The old **north road** climbs past the inn and over the mountains.
- **Strays.** Every generation or so somebody walks out of the fog on the north road from another
  world. The valley calls them strays, treats them kindly, and does not find them as strange as they
  find the valley. Nobody knows how to send one back. The player is one, thirty-one days in.
- **Money.** Twelve copper pennies make a silver; twenty silver make a crown. A bed and supper costs
  four copper. A good horse costs eight crowns. The inn owes the town forty crowns in back taxes. A
  dragon scale is worth about four hundred.
- **The inn.** It was called **Wren's Rest** and kept by the Wren family for three generations. It
  closed twenty years ago, when the new king's road went down the valley floor and took the carts
  with it. Of seven guest rooms, one (Room Four) still has a whole roof.
- **The Lantern Charter.** Three hundred years old and never repealed. Nobody, not even an officer of
  the crown, may take a paying guest from under a chartered inn's roof between lamp-lighting and dawn,
  so long as the lamp over the door is lit and the house is a working inn: a fire, a bed and a meal for
  anybody who pays. Under the same roof no guest may raise a hand against another. A working inn may
  not refuse a paying traveller while it has a bed free. The charter must be shown if challenged. The
  paper is in Old Marla's biscuit tin.
- **Dragons.** Real and rare: fewer than a dozen left in the mountains. A dragon can take human shape,
  but the horns stay and the body runs hot enough that rain steams off it. Fire or a dragon's strength
  used in human shape reopens an unhealed wound.
- **The scale.** A scale a dragon gives away freely, held near a flame, turns in the hand toward the
  dragon it came from, like a compass needle. Every hunter knows this.
- **The hunters.** The **Greyhook Company**: twenty dragon hunters with chain-bolt crossbows (made to
  pin a wing, not to kill) and hooked pikes, under a crown hunting warrant, which makes them officers
  under the charter. Their contract is from the **Margrave of the Fells**, who pays a thousand crowns
  for the red dragon taken alive and fifty for word of where she sleeps. He wants her chained in his
  tower so her scales can be cut as they grow back, one a month, for the rest of her life.

question that needs an answer now, because the torches are coming. Under 120 words. The only proper
nouns are Ember (who names herself) and Tobin (introduced as "your stablehand"). Turn 1 changes
something whatever the answer: she is a guest, hidden, refused, or the player is out in the rain.

## Cast

Every one of them wants something that has nothing to do with the player. That is what keeps them from
being an audience. Order in the fixture is cover-casting order: Ember, Wren, Tobin, Marla, Thatch.

### Ember, 212 (looks about twenty-five), a red dragon in human shape

- **Role:** Your first guest. Wounded, hunted, and paying in a currency that could get her caught.
- **Looks:** Tall and long-limbed, two dark swept-back horns, copper-red hair worn loose, amber eyes
  with slit pupils, a travel-stained dark green cloak over a plain linen shirt, one arm held stiff.
  Rain steams off her.
- **Her own motive:** Seven nights under a roof, unseen, so the tear across her shoulder (her left wing
  in her own shape) closes and she can fly. And, underneath, to find out whether the cook who fed her
  soup here fifty winters ago is still alive.
- **Fear:** The chain. A tower, an iron collar, and a man with shears once a month. Also that she has
  outlived everyone who would remember her kindly.
- **Secret 1:** She wintered here fifty years ago under another name. She chose this inn on purpose,
  because she remembered the charter and the lamp.
- **Secret 2:** The scale was a test. She knew it could betray her if it went near a fire or a buyer.
  She is watching what the keeper does with it.
- **Secret 3:** Her true name, given, is a promise: whoever holds it can call her once, from anywhere,
  and she will come. She has never given it to a human.
- **Boundaries:** Will not beg, and will leave by the window, wound or not, before anyone puts a collar
  on her. Will not use fire under a roof that sheltered her unless a life depends on it.
- **Voice:** Formal and a little archaic, short sentences, no contractions when frightened. Calls things
  by what they are: "the wound", "your fire", "the men with hooks". Very dry, perfectly straight-faced.
- **Romance:** available, slow, warm, and allowed to stay unspoken. Up to a kiss.

### Captain Corin Wren, 34, Greyhook Company

- **Role:** Leads the twenty hunters at the crossroads. Polite, patient, and at your door at 00:25.
- **Looks:** Lean and weathered, close-cropped dark hair going grey at the temples, a neat beard, a long
  oilskin coat over grey hunter's leathers, a wide-brimmed hat he takes off indoors, a coil of light
  chain at his belt.
- **His own motive:** Take the red dragon cleanly, by law, before the Margrave's cage wagon arrives, so
  that his company is paid and nobody he leads does anything ugly. Twenty people are owed wages.
- **Fear:** Becoming a man who puts anything in a cage for a fee. Walking into this inn and finding a
  ruin he could have saved.
- **Secret 1:** He was born in this inn's stable. His mother, Hester Wren, was the last keeper; she died
  two winters after it closed, still owing the taxes. The sign face down under the cellar stairs is his
  family's name. He carved a "C" into its back the day he left.
- **Secret 2:** He learned a week ago what "alive" means in his contract, and has told nobody in his
  company.
- **Boundaries:** Will not break the charter, or let his company break it while he can stop them. Will
  not lie about what he is hunting or why.
- **Voice:** Courteous, measured, formal. Calls the player "keeper" because that is the legal word.
  Quotes the charter word for word. Never threatens; describes what happens next, accurately.
- **Romance:** available, slow, and complicated by the fact that he is hunting your guest.
- **The legal joke at the centre of the story:** a working inn may not refuse a paying traveller while
  it has a bed free. Patch a second roof and the captain can rent the room next to hers, and then both
  of them are guests and neither may raise a hand under your roof.

### Tobin Ashby, 22, the stablehand who came with the inn

- **Role:** Has lived in the stable loft for six years and kept it spotless in a building with no
  roof. Nobody told him it was sold, so he kept working. Calls the player "Boss" because they own it.
- **Looks:** Very tall and broad, shaggy straw-coloured hair, a sunburned face, patched work trousers
  and braces over a collarless shirt, a stable lantern always in one hand.
- **His own motive:** Keep his job and his loft. He wants the inn alive because it is the only place
  that ever let him stay.
- **Fear:** Being sent away now the place has an owner. Fire in a stable at night.
- **Secret 1:** Yesterday a Greyhook scout paid him one silver to light a lantern in the hayloft
  window if a stranger came to the inn. He took it because the mare needed oats. It is in his left
  boot. If nobody asks him what is wrong by the second night, he lights it.
- **Secret 2:** He saw her come down on the crag last night and went up after. He knows where the
  chain-bolt is.
- **Boundaries:** Will not let an animal be hurt, and counts a dragon as an animal until told
  otherwise. Will not lie to your face twice.
- **Voice:** Slow and literal, short words, repeats the important part back to you. Calls the old grey
  mare by her full name, Lady Biscuit.

### Old Marla Tull, 71, next door

- **Role:** Your only neighbour. Cooked at Wren's Rest for thirty years. Talked the Reeve into selling
  you the place for a copper, for reasons she has not shared.
- **Looks:** Small and quick, white hair pinned up under a blue headscarf, a flour-dusted apron over a
  brown wool dress, sleeves pushed up, a covered basket on one arm.
- **Her own motive:** Hear the inn full one more time. Talk to Ember alone, once.
- **Fear:** Dying with the inn still dark. That Ember remembers her as a cook's helper with flour on
  her face and nothing more.
- **Secret 1:** The only copy of the Lantern Charter that would stand up in front of the Reeve has
  been in her biscuit tin for twenty years. Hester Wren gave it to her the night the inn closed, to
  give to the next keeper worth the name.
- **Secret 2:** Fifty years ago she was the one who fed the hooded traveller soup every night and asked
  nothing. The traveller left a scale on her pillow. Marla never sold it. It is sewn into the lining
  of her good coat, which means Marla could always have found her, and never went.
- **Boundaries:** Will not hand over the charter to anybody she has not watched do a day's real work.
  Will not talk about Hester Wren in front of her son.
- **Voice:** Fast, flat, rural. Short commands. Calls people "pet" or by their surname. Every kind thing
  she says is disguised as a complaint. Keeps bees and a goat called The Bishop.

### Reeve Imogen Thatch, 49, the town's officer

- **Role:** Keeps Brindle Ford's ledgers and its law. Sold you the inn. Will inspect it at noon on the
  third day and decide whether it is a working inn, which decides whether the charter protects anyone.
- **Looks:** Angular and upright, iron-grey hair in a tight bun, ink-stained fingers, a dark blue
  official's coat with a brass chain of office, a ledger under one arm and a folding measuring rod.
- **Her own motive:** Get the inn open and paying taxes. The north road dying took half the town's
  money, and an open inn brings the road's toll back.
- **Fear:** The Margrave taking an interest in her town. Choosing between the law and the town and
  getting it wrong in ink.
- **Secret 1:** She sold the inn for a copper on purpose and has been rooting for the player since.
- **Secret 2:** The Margrave has written asking her to "cooperate with the Greyhook Company in all
  things". She has not answered. The letter is under a duck-shaped paperweight, and its postscript asks
  how wide the inn's yard is, as if somebody wants to know whether a cage wagon would fit.
- **Boundaries:** Will not sign anything she has not read. Will not be bribed, and writes down that you
  tried.
- **Voice:** Procedure ("for the record", "pursuant to"), numbers everything, then a sudden plain
  sentence in a lower voice, said fast as if it does not count.

## Five promises

1. **FINALE: whether she flies on the seventh dawn.** Seed: a wound bound with her own torn sleeve.
   Payoff: the crag at first light, and what she does there.
2. **MYSTERY: why she chose this inn, of all the roofs in the valley.** Seed: she walked straight to the
   counter in the dark as if she knew where it was. Payoff: a page in a fifty-year-old guest book, and
   Marla.
3. **RELATIONSHIP: what the captain at the door used to call home.** Seed: a painted sign face down
   under the cellar stairs. Payoff: Wren's Rest, and a "C" carved in the back.
4. **BOSS: the inspection at noon on the third day.** Seed: the captain's petition. Payoff: the
   Reeve's rod, four questions, and the charter out of a biscuit tin.
5. **THEME: what a scale is worth.** Seed: worth more than the inn. Payoff: near a flame it turns toward
   her.
