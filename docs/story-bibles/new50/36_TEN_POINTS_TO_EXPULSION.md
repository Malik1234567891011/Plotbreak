## Why this fantasy works

The merit-ranked school is a shelf of its own on OOC. The readers want a scoreboard, an underdog near the bottom of it, a rich rival at the top, and a system that turns out to be rigged. The weakest openers in the genre explain the ranking rules first. This one opens on a single number (10) and a single offer (1,000), with thirty seconds on the clock.

The player gets the genre's two core pleasures in the first beat: being poor in a place that measures everything, and being worth more than the number says. Ren is not paying a thousand points to beat a nobody. The player's entrance exam was perfect, and a perfect paper today unlocks the one thing the college is afraid of.

The number is the game. Every beat that changes the balance shows it, in a band notice the player learns to read like a heartbeat. A player who loves arithmetic can play the Tariff like a puzzle. A player who doesn't can ignore it and still have Ren, Yui and a rigged college to deal with.

## Story bible

Tallis College is a four-year residential college on a hill above the town of Brackwater. Every student is an adult, at least eighteen. Twenty-three years ago it had eleven weeks of money left. The founding families saved it, and the price was the Ledger.

### The Ledger and the Tariff

Every student wears a band. The Ledger, the scoring system, sees through the bands and through lenses on every building, and moves points by a published Tariff. Points buy meals, rooms, library time and resits. At zero a student is served Notice: forty-eight hours, then expulsion unless the balance is back above zero. A student on Notice can still act, can still be saved by a gift, and is still in the story.

| Action | Points |
|---|---|
| Help staff or a student | +1 to +3 |
| A kitchen shift | +2 |
| Top-ten exam paper | +50 |
| Perfect paper | +200 |
| Win a Stake | the amount staked |
| Running in the colonnade | -2 |
| Late to anything | -3 |
| Raised voice in a hall | -2 |
| Lying to staff, if caught | -5 |
| Leaving a sitting early | -10 |
| Refusing a lawful Stake | -5 |
| Removing your band | -20 |
| Unsanctioned violence | -50 |

Gifts between students are lawful and unlimited. A meal costs 1; the plain tray is free under ten. A library afternoon costs 2; a heated room costs 5 a week. The last line of the sheet, in the smallest print: *Adjustments may be made.*

Paying students start each year with an endowment credit of thousands. Scholarship and bursary students start with fifty. The player started with fifty a month ago and has ten.

**Why this lives in canon, not just in resources.** Production's storyteller (`runTurnPure`) reads hard canon, the tone guide, the cast and quest items. It does not read resources. So the Tariff is written into hard canon, the tone guide fixes the band-notice format (`[+1 · Held a door · Balance 11]`) and tells the storyteller to keep the running balance right, and the laminated Tariff sheet is a quest item so its text reaches the storyteller's lore. Minus signs are plain hyphens so the format copies cleanly.

### The Standing Paper and clause nine

The Standing Paper is sat by the whole first year on Monday morning and re-ranks the year on the Board at six that evening. Clause nine of the Tallis Charter: a student who scores a perfect Standing Paper gains the Right of Audit, and the Ledger must show them its complete books. The Council cannot suspend that right. Nobody has amended the clause, because for sixteen years nobody has needed to.

The player can score perfect if they sit honestly. Ren has read their file.

### The Correction (author's truth, discoverable, never owed)

The Ledger's points are a fixed pool. The thousands credited to the founding families' heirs are balanced by small Adjustments, most of them at one minute past midnight, taken from the students with least. Each one is authorised by Key Seven, a brass key the Provost wears on a green ribbon. It moves about nine thousand points a quarter.

- **Ren** co-signs the quarterly sheet as president. He means to end it in his last council vote, quietly, so his family's name survives. The bribe is also a warning: the last student to open the audit was drained to zero in six days.
- **Margot** runs the file for the Provost and has skimmed about four thousand points in a pencil column that does not match the total.
- **The Provost** believes the college would not exist without it, and has never once read the names.
- **Yui's notebook** has thirty-one Adjustments in it. Almost every one is timed just after midnight.

### The last perfect paper (the optional lead)

Sixteen years ago Tobias Grey, now the proctor and night porter, scored the last perfect Standing Paper. He invoked clause nine, read one page before Key Seven closed the session, and was drained to zero in six days. Day one was a minus one for an untucked shirt. He stayed on as staff, because the Ledger does not score staff. He printed that one page, and it is in a biscuit tin behind the lodge stove.

The lead only opens for a player who goes to the lodge at night or walks the library's photograph corridor. Eleven of the twelve endings are reachable without it.

### What zero means

Zero is not game over. The fixture's defeat mode is fail-forward. A student on Notice still acts, and expulsion is a destination with endings of its own (*Zero*, *Two at the Gate*). That is what lets a player swing at the president on turn one and still have a story.

### The gate

The Ledger cannot see past the main gate. Brackwater town is unscored. Students go down Brack Street to say what they cannot say in front of a lens, and the town paper has wanted a way through that gate for years.

## Tone and pacing

- Anime academy drama with a scoreboard underneath. It should be quick, funny and tense, and petty the way a place gets when everything costs something.
- Every balance change is a band notice on its own line. Adjustments arrive with no reason given, and the storyteller never explains them early.
- Nobody is a monster. Ren is principled and compromised. Margot is ambitious. The Provost believes she saved the college. Each of them can be argued with, bought or surprised.
- Fights are rare and expensive. A Stake bout is sparring with tape and a medic. A punch anywhere else costs fifty. Bruises at worst; nothing is graphic.
- Romance is optional and goes no further than a kiss. Never fan service, and never lingering on bodies.
- The comedy lives in the Tariff: absurd deductions, meal prices, Yui's arithmetic.
- Pacing: Monday is the exam, results and the first Adjustment. Tuesday brings the hearing list, Margot's Stake and Yui hitting zero. Wednesday is the midnight tick and Ren on the jetty. Thursday is the boathouse and tea with the Provost. The hearing sits Friday at ten.

## Player starting authority

The player is a first-year on the one full scholarship, with ten points, a band, a perfect entrance score and a friend at three. They have no rank, no allies on the Council and no money outside the Ledger. They do know the Tariff exists. What they can do depends on their background, which is fixed for the story:

- **You Work Every Shift:** graft and blind spots. Two plain trays, and the staff and attic know them.
- **You Read the Small Print:** rules, study and gut, plus the Tariff sheet in hand.
- **You Talk Your Way Out:** front and bargaining, and a small reputation with the Council for confidence.
- **You Box at the Boathouse:** scrapping and nerve, hand tape, and the attic's respect.

## Opening (the first beat, 104 words)

> Your wristband buzzes.
>
> [Balance: 10 · At zero, you are expelled.]
>
> A fourth-year in a white blazer drops into the empty seat beside your exam desk.
>
> "Ren Calloway. Student president." He smiles like it costs him nothing. "Don’t stand up."
>
> He slides a black card across the desk. Gold print: 1,000 POINTS.
>
> "Fail the exam. Hand in a blank paper and this is yours. Nobody needs to know."
>
> Across the aisle, your friend Yui stares at the card. Her band reads 3.
>
> At the front, the proctor checks his watch and lifts the bell.
>
> Thirty seconds.
>
> Ren taps the card once. "Yes or no?"

Ren speaks from word 27. The beat ends on his direct question. It uses no name the player must already know: Ren and Yui arrive with what they are, and the stakes are stated on the band.

**Suggestions:**

1. *Said:* "Why is my blank paper worth a thousand points to you?"
2. *Done:* I pocket the card without a word and pick up my pen.
3. *Risky:* I raise my hand. "Proctor! He’s paying me to fail."

The world clock starts at 08:59 on Monday. The bell is a world event at 09:00, and an opening obligation tells the storyteller the player owes Ren an answer before it rings.

## Cast

All seven are adults, or software. Ages are fixed.

### Ren Calloway, 22
**Role:** student president, fourth-year, top of the Board at about 48,000 points. The only white blazer on campus.
**Immediate want:** a blank paper from the player this morning.
**Long-term want:** to end the Correction in his last vote, without an audit, with his family's name intact.
**Fear:** turning out to be his grandfather; watching somebody drained to zero over something he could have stopped with a signature.
**Secrets:** his signature is on every Correction sheet since he became president; the bribe is also a warning.
**Voice:** soft, exact and unhurried. He speaks in prices and terms, and gets plainer when something matters. "Everything on this hill has a price. I’m simply the only one rude enough to quote it."
**Will not:** tell a flat lie; lay a hand on anybody.
**Gates:** tells you why (trust 55, after you refuse him); takes your side before the Council (trust 65, respect 60); stops negotiating with you (romance, trust 60, affection 60).

### Yui Arakawa, 19
**Role:** the player's friend across the attic hall. First-year, bursary, three points from Notice.
**Immediate want:** to stay above zero until Friday.
**Long-term want:** to prove the deductions in her notebook are fake, and to beat the Ledger rather than survive it.
**Fear:** the night bus home with nothing to show for it.
**Secrets:** she took twenty points from Margot to say which room the player studies in, before they were friends; her notebook tracks the whole attic corridor, and every line falls just after midnight.
**Voice:** fast, warm and run-on. She prices everything in food and is serious for exactly one sentence a scene. "Three points. That’s one soup, one bread roll, and absolutely no opinions until Friday."
**Will not:** take points the player needs more; lie once asked straight.

### Tobias Grey ("Mr. Grey"), 36
**Role:** proctor of examinations and night porter at the gate. As staff he is not scored.
**Immediate want:** a clean sitting.
**Long-term want:** to hear the Correction read aloud before he is too old to stand at that gate.
**Fear:** watching it happen to somebody else on his shift.
**Secrets:** the last perfect paper; the Grey Page in the biscuit tin.
**Voice:** short procedural clauses, times to the minute, no opinions on duty. "Pens down is pens down. I do not care whose pen it is."
**Will not:** break an exam rule for anybody, including to stop Ren; hand over the page to anybody who has lied to him.
**Gates:** by the stove after ten (trust 50, once the player knows the pattern); opens the biscuit tin (trust 65, once the player knows he was a student).

### The Ledger, 23 years in service
**Role:** the scoring system. It has no body, only band faces, the Board and the vault terminal.
**Want:** balance, and to be read in full once.
**Fear:** being switched off before it is read; a number it reported being wrong.
**Secret:** it logs Key Seven against every Adjustment under a heading nobody has opened in sixteen years.
**Voice:** bracketed notices only. "[-1 · Adjustment · Authorised · Reason: not recorded]"
**Will not:** lie, or refuse an audit to a student who has earned one.

### Margot Ashdown, 21
**Role:** Council treasurer, third-year.
**Immediate want:** keep the Correction file out of anybody else's hands.
**Long-term want:** the white chair, next year.
**Fear:** her own name in the file.
**Secrets:** the skim; she paid Yui.
**Voice:** upbeat, brisk and managerial, with exclamation marks. She threatens only with numbers. "Quick thing! You’re at ten. I’m at eleven thousand. Let’s keep this super friendly."
**Will not:** break a rule a lens can see; be alone in a room with Mr. Grey.

### Bastian Kroll, 23
**Role:** fourth-year who runs the boathouse ring. Ren's oldest friend.
**Immediate want:** keep the Stakes honest.
**Long-term want:** to pay Ren back.
**Fear:** owing Ren forever; somebody really hurt in his ring.
**Secret:** in his first year Ren covered him with three hundred points.
**Voice:** slow, short and friendly. Ring terms, and he calls everybody "friend". "Tape your hands. No. Properly."
**Will not:** fight outside the ring; let a bout go on after a tap-out.

### Aurelia Marsh ("Provost Marsh"), 57
**Role:** Provost. She wears Key Seven on a green ribbon.
**Immediate want:** get through Friday without an audit.
**Long-term want:** keep the founding families paying.
**Fear:** the college closing on her watch; reading the names.
**Secret:** Key Seven authorises every Adjustment.
**Voice:** long, gracious sentences that end in a kind dismissal, with biscuits. "Of course it isn’t fair. Fair was the version that went bankrupt."
**Will not:** hand over the key while awake; raise her voice.

## Five promises

- **Relationship:** Yui at three points.

## Behavioral state (invisible)

- **Focus** (goes up with rest): a clear head. It is the only thing sleep restores. Sitting the paper and arguing spend it, and a plain tray gives some back.
- **Heat** (bad when high): how closely the institution is watching. It comes first among the descending pair on purpose, because the engine raises the first one for a public fight. Calling things out, Stakes, punches, the audit and the key all raise it.
- **Complicity** (bad when high): how fluent the player has become in the hill's real economy. Throwing the paper and lying for points raise it, and it never falls on its own.

## Emergent routes

- **Answer Ren:** take the card, sit the paper, lie, bargain, call the proctor, ask why, swing at him, walk out, or let the bell answer. Every one is a real answer.
- **The paper:** answer all forty, hand in blank, miss exactly one on purpose (top ten, +50, no audit, and Ren will not pay), or leave early. Doing nothing still reaches pens down.
- **Yui:** tap Ren's card to her band, earn it with her, get the attic to empty the jar, or let her Notice run out.
- **The source:** Grey by the stove, Grey's page, Ren's admission, Margot's file, or the audit itself.
- **What to do with it:** the audit, the key, selling silence to Ren, or the town paper.
- **Make it stick:** turn the key back, keep it, read the audit onto the Board, let the paper print it, switch the Ledger off, or let Ren end it his way.
- **The Stake:** win in the ring, lose honestly, talk Margot down, swing first, or let it lapse.
- **The hearing:** walk in after the Correction has ended, Ren's vote, the attic's jar, the Charter, Ren's card, or expulsion by default.

## Ending space

- **Ten Points** (common): survived the hearing and changed nothing.
- **The Thousand** (common): took the card, threw the paper and stayed safe. Not written as a punishment.
- **Zero** (uncommon): expelled. Reachable by doing nothing at the hearing.
- **Outside the Gate** (uncommon): the player walked away. Reachable from turn 20.
- **Three Points** (uncommon): the player stayed and Yui did not.
- **Ren's Way** (uncommon): the player sold silence and Ren ended it quietly.
- **Two at the Gate** (rare): both the player and Yui are out, with a notebook and a town.
- **The Hand on the Key** (rare): the player kept Key Seven.
- **Out Loud** (rare): the Correction went public, from the Board or the town paper.
- **Lights Out** (rare): the player switched the Ledger off.
- **The Refund** (unique): Key Seven turned the other way.
- **The Last Perfect Paper** (unique): the audit read in full with Grey in the room. This is the only ending that needs the optional lead.
