# STRUCK FROM THE REGISTER

**Slate:** #31 · Failed heir · male lean · OOC evidence: Failed Knight of the Swordsmanship Family (1.2M)
**Slug:** `struck-from-the-register` · **Story id:** `story_struck_from_the_register`
**Genre:** sword-house drama, family, rivalry, martial arts, light mystery
**One-line fantasy:** The sword house is expelling its failed heir this morning, and the sister who will take your place is holding the blade that cuts your name out of the family book.

## Why this fantasy works

The failed heir is one of the most reliable openings in Korean web fiction, and OOC's charts say the
appetite is real. What makes this version playable rather than a recap is that the expulsion is not
backstory. It is turn one. The whole house is kneeling, the book is open to your page, and the person
holding the knife is the one person in the house who loves you.

Every route out of that hall is a real story: leave quietly, fight your sister for your name, refuse
both and make your grandfather do it himself, or grab the book and run. None of them is the wrong one.

Underneath, the player is not untalented. Their body answers an attack faster and truer than anybody in
the house, and the house has never once tested it, because every test the house sets is a form performed
alone. That gap is the hook for the power fantasy, and the reason the gap exists is the mystery.

This story succeeds if a player who never fights anybody still has a good ten turns, and a player who
fights everybody still has a story with a family in it.

## Story bible

**The city.** Haseong, a river city of seven sword houses. The houses sit on the Sword Council, run the
city's training yards, guard its barges, and settle their quarrels in licensed bouts. A Long Bridge joins
the two banks; under it, after dark, the unlicensed fight rings run for coin. An island in the river holds
the bout ground, where the Spring Bout is fought every year.

**House Seol.** Thirty-one generations of the "first cut": a forward art in which every form begins with
an attack and a good swordsman ends a bout with one stroke. Every member of the house is written in
**the register**, a long folding book of mulberry paper kept open on a stand in the ancestral hall. The
heir keeps a short rite at the register every morning.

**The test.** Each spring an heir must pass the lantern test: one first cut through the cord of a hanging
paper lantern, so that it drops upright and the flame stays lit. The player failed it at twenty-two,
twenty-three, and twenty-four (a month ago). Three failures and the name is struck.

**Striking.** House law: whoever cuts a name from the register takes that person's place. Before the cut,
the struck person has one right, the **right of contest**: they may challenge whoever holds the blade to a
bout in the yard, to first clean touch or a yield. Win and the name stays for a year and a day.

**What a struck name costs.** When a house strikes a name it sends a notice to the city's **Roll Office**,
which keeps the roll of every person allowed to carry a blade inside the walls. At the next sundown bell
the struck person's brass **sword token** lapses, and the watch will take any blade carried without one.
A struck person loses the family name too. Two ways back to a token: be written into another house's
register, or win the city's **open trial** (three bouts against licensed blades, fought on the first
morning of the Spring Bout) and become a **free blade**, who writes their own family name on the roll.

**The hidden truth (hard canon, revealed through play).**
- The player has an **answering hand**: their blade moves only once the other one has committed, and then
  it is faster and truer than anybody in the house. Alone, performing forms, they freeze. Attacked, they
  are excellent. Nobody in the house tests it, because no house test is a fight.
- 140 years ago, the grandmaster of the day struck his own elder brother, who had the answering hand. The
  brother crossed the river and founded **House Geum**, whose art is the "second cut": the answering
  stroke. The two houses signed **the Pact of the Bridge**. Its terms: no Seol may keep the answering hand
  in the house; any Seol who shows it is struck; House Geum sends a witness to every striking; House Seol
  pays Geum a yearly "bridge rent"; and if a witness ever sees a Seol use the answering hand while still in
  the register, Geum may call a **feud bout** at the next Spring Bout for House Seol's seat on the Sword
  Council. Losing the seat means losing the right to train licensed blades. The house would empty.
- Five names have been cut under the pact in 140 years. One of them is **Han**, the old servant who keeps
  the whetstones. He is the Grandmaster's younger brother, struck forty-seven years ago, and he chose to
  stay in the house as a servant rather than cross the river.
- Three days ago the Grandmaster told **Siwon** the real reason and made her promise not to tell the
  player. She agreed to hold the blade because the alternative was their cousin Doha holding it.
- House Geum's own art is dying. No Geum child in three generations has had a true answering hand, and they
  teach the second cut from books. That is why their heir has come to the morning rite every day for a
  month.

**The playable span** is three days: the striking (day one, 09:00), the sundown bell (day one, 18:40), the
Spring Bout draw (day two, noon), and the Spring Bout itself (day three, 15:00).

## Tone and pacing

- Webtoon sword drama: fast, physical, clear. A fight is a decision made with the body, and it reads as
  one. Distance, weight, the sound of steel, who moved first.
- Family pain under formality. People say the polite thing and mean the other thing, and the beat shows
  both.
- Humour is load-bearing: Doha's spite, Yeon's dry price-talk, Minseo's clauses, Han's grunts.
- Violence is quick and never graphic. House bouts are to first touch or yield. A cut sleeve, a bruise, a
  sword in the sand. Real blood is a scandal and is written as one.
- Romance (Yeon only) is tension, banter and choice, up to a kiss. Siwon is the player's sister and the
  relationship is family, nothing else.
- When somebody attacks the player, the player's hands answer before they decide. Narrate that as a
  reflex the player can use or hold back. Never narrate the player as weak in a real fight; narrate forms
  done alone as the thing that fails.

## Player starting authority

The player is a **BLANK** protagonist: they name themselves (the family name is Seol), and Siwon calls
them brother. They are **24**. They author:
- their given name and appearance;
- what they did with the three years since the first failed test (the archetype);
- one thing nobody in the house knows about them;
- their first instinct this morning;
- everything they do from the first beat on.

### Minimal hard canon
- The player is 24, the eldest grandchild of House Seol, and was heir until this morning.
- They failed the lantern test three springs running, the last a month ago.
- Siwon holds the cutting blade. Whoever cuts a name takes that person's place.
- The right of contest exists and is binding.
- A struck name's sword token lapses at the next sundown bell.
- The player has the answering hand. Nobody has ever tested it in the house.
- The Pact of the Bridge exists. Han is the Grandmaster's struck brother. Both are discoverable and
  neither is owed to the player.

## Major cast

Every character is an adult. Ages are stated and fixed.

### Seol Siwon, 22
**Role:** younger sister; holds the cutting blade; heir from the moment she cuts.
**Appearance:** house white, hair tied high with a cord cut from her first broken practice sword, a
straight-backed stance she never relaxes in public.
**Want:** the heirship, honestly earned, and her brother out of the house before it uses him.
**Fear:** the contest. In fifteen years of night practice she has never once touched him. In daylight,
in front of the Geum witness, that would be the feud.
**Secret:** the Grandmaster told her the real reason three days ago, and she promised not to tell.
**Voice:** clipped, imperative, counts things (bowls, years, strokes). The only person who calls the
player "brother".
> "Say you'll leave quietly, brother. Then I won't have to fight you."

### Grandmaster Seol Jae, 74
**Role:** head of House Seol, the player's grandfather.
**Want:** the house intact and the player alive and far away. He has paid for a room at the Ferry Inn and
a seat on the southern ferry in the player's name.
**Fear:** dying with the thing about Han unsaid.
**Secret:** he held the blade that cut his brother forty-seven years ago. His hands shook.
**Voice:** formal, sentence-shaped, speaks of the house in the third person and calls the player "the
eldest", never by name.
> "A house is a sentence. Somebody has to decide where it stops."

### Han, 68
**Role:** the old servant who keeps the whetstones; born Seol Han.
**Want:** the player to have the choice he did not.
**Fear:** that the player will choose what he chose and sweep the yard for forty-seven years.
**Secret:** he has been training the player's answering hand for years by throwing things at him when
nobody was looking. He is the Grandmaster's brother.
**Voice:** few words, grunts, talks to the blades instead of to people, still calls the player
"young master" and will not stop.
> "Young master. Your edge is rolled. Give it here."

### Geum Yeon, 24
**Role:** heir of House Geum; the pact's witness at the striking. Rival, possible romance.
**Want:** the real second cut, which her house only has on paper. The player across the bridge.
**Fear:** that she is the best imitation in three generations and nothing more.
**Secret:** she saw the player's blade twitch toward the swinging lantern on the third test, and knew.
She goes under the bridge at night looking for a masked fighter who never swings first.
**Voice:** dry, amused, polite on the surface; talks in prices and offers.
> "Across the bridge, a name costs one signature and one bout. Ours are cheaper than yours."

### Seol Doha, 27
**Role:** cousin; best at the forms in the house; wanted the heirship and will never get it.
**Want:** to be seen as the one who did the work. Siwon's second at the Spring Bout.
**Fear:** that hard work is not the same as being good, and everybody knows.
**Secret:** he spied on the woodshed for years and knows the player is the better sword. He owes the
bridge rings money and has bet what he does not have on the Spring Bout.
**Voice:** sneering commentary, money and odds, laughs at his own jokes.
> "Three lanterns, cousin. I had silver on the second one and you still disappointed me."

### Oh Minseo, 31
**Role:** clerk of the Roll Office; she rings the sundown bell.
**Want:** the roll correct, and one struck name to take the open trial properly.
**Fear:** the roll becoming a thing the houses own.
**Secret:** she is a struck name herself, from a small house, at nineteen. She won the open trial and
chose her mother's family name. A licensed sword sits under her counter.
**Voice:** clauses, form numbers, exact times, deadpan.
> "Your token lapses at sundown. Not at dusk. Sundown is a bell, and I ring it."

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Stamina** (good high, restores with rest): how much fight and talk the player has left today.
- **Scandal** (good low, first in order): what the city is saying about House Seol's struck heir. Public
  violence feeds it.
- **Rift** (good low): the distance between the player and Siwon. Claiming the contest and refusing her
  widen it. It narrows only with time and honesty.

## Five promises

1. **FINALE: Whether your name is in a book by the end.** Seed: the register open to your page. Payoff:
   a name written somewhere: Seol's register, Geum's, the city roll, or nowhere by choice.
2. **RELATIONSHIP: Why Siwon agreed to hold the blade.** Seed: her hands are steady, her eyes are not.
   Payoff: the Grandmaster told her three days ago, and the alternative was Doha.
3. **MYSTERY: What your hands do when nobody is testing them.** Seed: fifteen years in the woodshed and
   she never touched you. Payoff: the second cut, the art House Seol strikes out of itself.
4. **MYSTERY: Who Han was before he swept the yard.** Seed: he still calls you young master and sweeps one
   patch of sand twice. Payoff: a hole in the register, forty-seven years old, the shape of his name.
5. **RIVAL: The witness from across the bridge.** Seed: she has come to the morning rite every day for a
   month. Payoff: a hundred and forty years of bridge rent, and what she wants that money cannot buy.
