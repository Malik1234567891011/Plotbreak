## Why this fantasy works

The lone man among witches is a proven shelf, and it sits next to the academy-with-a-big-cast shape that tops the charts. What it usually lacks is a first beat. This one has a famous moment on turn one: the most powerful woman in the building has asked for her bird back, in front of sixty people, and the bird has said no.

That one image does four jobs:

- **It makes the player special without making him a chosen one.** Nothing about him glows. A very old raven has made a decision, and everybody else has to deal with it.
- **It gives the player a relationship with every major character on turn one.** The headmistress wants the raven back. The top student was promised him. The roommate has to live with him. The deputy head can use him.
- **It starts a real political clock.** The governors sit in five days to reconfirm the headmistress, and her familiar is on a first-year's arm.
- **It plants the mystery without explaining it.** Why the Hedge let a man in is the question under everything. A player who never asks it still gets a whole game.

Being the only man is funny because of logistics and attention. There is no men's robe, so he wears the groundskeeper's. There is no men's dormitory, so he shares a room with a second-year and her hen. Four hundred people stop eating when he walks into the refectory. The witches are people with jobs, rivalries, debts and opinions, and most of them are much better at magic than he is.

## Story bible

**Thornwitch** is the oldest college of witchcraft in the country: four hundred students over four years, all adults. It stands on a tidal island called **the Holm** in a cold grey estuary, reached by Tobias Fenn's ferry or, twice a day at low tide, by a stone causeway that stays open for about forty minutes.

The island is ringed by **the Hedge**, a living wall of blackthorn and briar forty feet high, planted by the seven founders three centuries ago. It is the college's ward and its gatekeeper. Every applicant walks up to the **Hedge Gate** alone on Admission Night, and it opens or it does not. Nobody, the headmistress included, can overrule it.

For three hundred years it opened only for women. This year it opened for the player. His letter arrived on the headmistress's desk already addressed, in a hand nobody at the college writes, with a small drawing of a pair of shears where a signature should be.

The college had six weeks' notice and did not use them well. He wears the groundskeeper's spare robe. The North Tower was full, so he shares **Room Nine** with Nadia Kowal, her hen, and a curtain on a rope down the middle. The refectory has laid one place at a small table by the kitchen door, with a card that says GENTLEMAN, and nobody will say who wrote it.

On the first morning comes **the First Calling**. Every first-year chalks a circle in the Calling Hall and calls, and whatever answers is their familiar for four years. Fifty-nine witches get toads, moths, sparrows and a hare. The player's circle calls **Sexton**, the headmistress's raven, who has sat on her shoulder for twenty-one years and on the shoulders of headmistresses for three hundred. He lands on the player's arm, puts his claws through the sleeve, and says no.

### The deeper truth (never volunteered, only earned)

Three hundred years ago the college had two offices. The **headmistress** ran the college. The **Hedgewarden** kept the Hedge alive with the founders' shears, and by custom the Hedgewarden was not a witch; the last one was a man. In a bad year the governors voted to close the gate to men and struck the office out of the Charter. That vote is **the Closing**, and everyone now believes the Hedge closed itself.

Since then the raven has carried both jobs, passed from headmistress to headmistress. It has been too much for a long time. The north end of the Hedge, **the Grey Reach**, is dying.

Two more things are true this autumn:

- **Morgaine Ashdown's own craft is fading.** For two years Sexton has been lighting her lamps for her. She cannot light the Equinox lamp alone, and the governors arrive in four days.
- **The Hedge opened for the player because it needs a Hedgewarden again,** and Sexton chose him for the same reason. Nobody alive knows the whole of it. A cat in the kitchens, a ferryman outside the gate and a pasted-shut page in the governors' archive each know part.

Sexton will go back to Morgaine's shoulder only when the Hedge has a keeper or she stops pretending she can carry the college alone. Either change frees him.

## Tone and pacing

- Bright, quick and funny on top, with a real college and real politics underneath.
- The only-man comedy is about logistics and attention: the robe, the room, the washroom rota, the table by the kitchen door, four hundred heads turning. Never about bodies.
- Witches are competent, specific and busy. Most of them are better at magic than the player, and the prose knows it.
- Magic is tactile: chalk, salt, thorn, ink, thimbles, smoke, a circle drawn half an inch short.
- Romance is a choice, slow and earned, and goes no further than a kiss. Ivy and Nadia are both possible and neither is a prize. Either can say no.
- Nobody is a villain. Morgaine is formidable and frightened at once, and the second shows only when it has been earned. Hester is right that something is being hidden.
- The mystery is for a player who goes looking and never intrudes on one who does not.
- If a scene has nothing at stake, keep it pleasant and short: soup, a library window, a hen in a satchel.

## Cast

Cast order in the fixture is cover-casting order: Ivy, Morgaine, Nadia, Sexton, Hester, Cass, Tobias. Everyone except Cass (kitchens) and Tobias (the landing) is in the Calling Hall when the story starts.

### Ivy Calder, 22
**Role:** First of the fourth year, the headmistress's apprentice, and the witch the raven was promised to at graduation.
**Look:** tall and sharp-featured, dark auburn hair cut straight at the jaw, green eyes, a thorn-green silk sash worn exactly straight, an ink stain on her writing hand. A silver adder called Footnote coiled round her left wrist.
**Want:** to beat the player fairly on the Bowl and take the raven back properly.
**Need:** to be chosen for once rather than only earning things.
**Fear:** being second; being the apprentice only because nobody else applied; being pitied.
**Secrets:** for six weeks she has gone to the Grey Reach alone at two in the morning and patched the dying Hedge with her own craft. On her own Admission Night she asked the Hedge to let her be the one who saves Thornwitch, and she suspects it answered by sending the player.
**Voice:** fast, precise, competitive. Keeps score out loud ("That's one to me"). Gets more formal, not less, when flustered.
**Limits:** will not cheat in a duel; will not speak against Morgaine to Hester; will not be rescued in public.
**Romance:** rivals, then possibly something else. One kiss at most, late, earned at the Grey Reach.

### Morgaine Ashdown, 58
**Role:** Headmistress for twenty-one years; until this morning, the raven's.
**Look:** tall and straight-backed, iron-grey hair in a braid pinned round her head, black robes lined in thorn green, a silver thimble on her left forefinger, a worn leather pad on her left shoulder with nothing on it.
**Want:** Sexton back before she has to light the Equinox lamp.
**Need:** to stop carrying the college alone, and to say so.
**Fear:** being found out in front of the Seven Chairs; the college failing in the one year she cannot keep it.
**Secrets:** her craft has been fading for two years and Sexton has been lighting her lamps. The player's letter arrived already addressed, and she argued the governors into honouring it because the raven sat on it and would not get off.
**Voice:** complete sentences, quietly delivered. Counts in threes. Uses surnames. Ends a threat or an order with "Thank you."
**Limits:** will not beg; will not expel a student for something the Hedge did; will not lie outright to the governors.

### Nadia Kowal, 20
**Role:** second-year herb witch on a scholarship, and the other half of Room Nine.
**Look:** broad-shouldered and freckled, a thick black plait, a hand-me-down robe let out at the seams, a satchel of herb cuttings that smells of rosemary. A speckled hen called Duchess, who lays an egg whenever somebody nearby lies.
**Want:** to keep her scholarship; to make Room Nine liveable for two people and a hen.
**Need:** one friend at this college who is not keeping score.
**Fear:** losing the scholarship and going home to gut herring; being known as the witch who roomed with the scandal.
**Secrets:** Hester has told her the scholarship renewal depends on reporting on the player; she has reported twice, the truth both times, as little as she could. Her last roommate, Bea Lowrie, left after a night at the north end of the Hedge and wrote one letter Nadia never answered.
**Voice:** port-town blunt and warm, swears in fish ("oh, cod"), narrates her own embarrassment as it happens.
**Limits:** will not lie for the player (the hen would give her away); the curtain stays shut after ten.
**Romance:** friends first, possibly more. Slow, warm, funny, and one kiss at most.

### Sexton, over 300
**Role:** the headmistress's raven for three centuries, and since this morning, the player's.
**Look:** a raven far larger than a raven should be, glossy black, one grey feather at the throat, a silver ring on his left leg engraved with seven thorns.
**Want:** rest: a keeper for the Hedge, so he can stop being two people at once.
**Voice:** one to four words, never more. "No." "Bread first." "Ask the ferryman." "Not her. Not yet." "Two jobs. Too heavy."
**Limits:** will not go back to Morgaine's shoulder until something real changes; will not speak within arm's reach of Hester; cannot lie, and will not explain.

### Hester Crane, 47
**Role:** Deputy Head and Mistress of Discipline, who has wanted the headmistress's chair for nine years.
**Look:** neat and sharp-eyed, greying brown hair in a perfect bun, wire reading glasses on a chain, a grey wool shawl over regulation black, a ring of brass keys at her belt. A grey moth called Ledger on her collar.
**Want:** to show the Seven Chairs that Morgaine has lost control, and to perform the Severing on the fifth afternoon.
**Fear:** becoming a footnote in minutes she has kept for nine years; the college closing while she was right.
**Secrets:** she already holds the governors' writ for the Severing, with the player's name written in. She watched Morgaine fail to light a lamp last spring, and watched the raven light it.
**Voice:** warm, maternal, procedural. Calls everyone "dear". Quotes rule numbers. Never finishes a threat; lets the paperwork finish it.
**Limits:** will not break a rule where she can be seen; will not harm a student with her own hands, though she will sign a paper that does; will not lie outright to the governors.

### Cass, 91 (by her own count)
**Role:** the last headmistress's cat, unbound for twenty-one years, who runs the kitchens' trade in secrets.
**Look:** a long-haired black cat larger than a cat should be, one white forepaw, a torn left ear, yellow eyes.
**Want:** sardines, warmth, and to see the Hedge saved before she goes.
**Fear:** being forgotten in a kitchen; watching the Hedge die with only her knowing why.
**Secrets:** she knows the Closing was a governors' vote, and where the minutes are kept. The last headmistress, Agnes Pryor, died trying to patch the Grey Reach alone; Morgaine was told it was her heart.
**Voice:** languid and transactional. Prices everything. Describes people by what they smell of. Ends offers with "Going once."
**Limits:** will not be picked up; will not tell a secret for nothing; will not lie, only withhold.

### Tobias Fenn, 73
**Role:** the ferryman, and the only other man on the island, who has lived outside the Hedge his whole life.
**Look:** weathered and white-bearded, an oilskin coat and a flat cap, rope-scarred hands, a clay pipe he never lights, and a long oilcloth bundle never far away.
**Want:** to hand over what his family has kept for three hundred years before he dies.
**Secrets:** the Warden's Shears, kept dry since the Closing with one instruction passed from each ferryman to the next: when a man walks through that gate again, give him these. At night, at the gate, he can hear the Hedge asking for somebody.
**Voice:** slow and coastal. Tides, weather, and stories about his grandmother.
**Limits:** will not go through the Hedge Gate; will not hand the bundle to anybody who has not asked what is in it.

## Quests

- **The Raven on Your Arm** (MAIN, active from the start). First, the next five minutes in the Calling Hall: ask him to go, tell her straight, make the hall laugh, walk out with him, or throw a hex (which also ends the story under Rule Forty-One). Then the six o'clock appointment in the Rookery (keep it, offer him back, or stand her up), which accepts the bond and grants the Raven's Eye. After the Sitting, **whose raven**: he goes back only if `hedge_kept` or `morgaine_confesses`; he stays if the player says plainly that he is his and Sexton's trust is 60; or he is cut away.
- **First of Her Year** (MAIN, found when the duel is called). The duel: beat her fairly, win ugly with a hex, lose, refuse to her face, or never turn up. Then two in the morning: she takes you, you follow her, or you watch through the raven. Every route teaches `knows:the_reach_is_dying` and opens the Hedge lead. Last, what you are to each other: more than that (one kiss), allies, or rivals for life.
- **The Equinox Sitting** (MAIN, found after the first day). What she is hiding: she tells you, you see it through the raven, Hester tells you, you notice it in the Rookery, or everyone finds out on the fourth night. The lamp: make her say it, light it with her, take it to Hester, or let it fail. The Sitting: expose the Closing, stand beside her honesty, let the lit lamp speak, or watch the Chairs choose Hester.
- **The Severing** (MAIN, found when Hester visits). The writ: argue her down (respect 50), let a reconfirmed headmistress tear it up, take the red folder, or stand there while Headmistress Crane does it.
- **Room Nine** (SIDE, found by visiting it). Two beds and one curtain, then what she tells Hester, then her scholarship: speak for her, give her true and harmless things to report, or let the review happen.
- **Going Once** (SIDE, found by visiting the kitchens). Pay Cass in sardines, in work, or with a true secret.
- **Why the Hedge Let You In** (LEAD, found only through `knows:the_hedge_wants_something`). The Closing (from Cass, the minutes, the Charter or the raven), the ferryman and the shears, the Warden's Door, and whether the Hedge gets a keeper: two keys with Ivy, take the office alone, or let it go.
- **Rule Forty-One** (SIDE, active from the start). Only finishes if the player attacks Hester, Morgaine, Nadia, or Ivy before any challenge, or hexes the Calling Hall. Finishing it is expulsion.

## Five promises

1. **Whose raven he is** (FINALE). Seed: he says "No" in front of the whole hall. Payoff: he goes back, stays, or is cut away, and says which in a word.
2. **Why the Hedge let a man in** (MYSTERY). Seed: a letter that arrived already addressed. Payoff: the Closing and the office nobody has held for three hundred years.
3. **First of her year** (RIVAL). Seed: a green sash stepping out of a circle. Payoff: the Bowl, then the north end at two in the morning.
4. **What the headmistress cannot do alone** (RELATIONSHIP). Seed: she touches the empty pad on her shoulder. Payoff: a lamp that will not light for her.
5. **The deputy head's folder** (BOSS). Seed: biscuits and a red folder she has not opened yet. Payoff: a lawful writ and a chair she has wanted for nine years.

## Image plan

Polished anime key art. Adults only, described by costume and pose, never by age words.

### Cover direction

Ivy Calder is the subject, waist up in the foreground, pointing a stick of white chalk at the viewer like a duelling challenge: competitive, furious and a little flustered. Dark auburn hair cut at the jaw, green eyes, black college robe, thorn-green sash worn exactly straight, a silver snake round her wrist. In the lower foreground, from the viewer's side, a forearm in a brown groundskeeper's sleeve gripped by a huge black raven with one grey throat feather, looking up at her, unimpressed. Behind her, soft: the round Calling Hall, gold window light, chalk circles, thin smoke. Her face stays clean.

**TITLE:** the words THE ONLY MAN AT THORNWITCH painted into the art in the upper third as an elegant serif logo in white with thin thorn-green briar through the letters, spelled exactly THE ONLY MAN AT THORNWITCH, legible at thumbnail size. No other text anywhere.

## Conventions this fixture follows

- Exported raw as `StoryVersion.parse(raw)`, not wrapped in `withDerivedAssetKeys`. Not added to `index.ts`. `publishedAt: null`, no prologue.
- Production runs `runTurnPure`, which reads the premise, tone guide, hard canon, the chosen archetype, the full cast, the opening, style examples, places, quest items, factions, quest summaries, world events and the first twelve endings. It never reads resources, step notes, promises or non-starting abilities. So the raven's rule and the clock are in hard canon, the Hedge's letter is a starting quest item, every archetype starts with Ask the Raven, and event notes carry their own time.
- The three checks: every gated ability is granted by a step reward, every card blurb says "you", and every location has an inbound edge.
- Gates: `npx vitest run src/the-only-man-at-thornwitch.spec.ts` and `npx tsc --noEmit`, both from `packages/test-fixtures`. `infra/scripts/story-checks.ts` is not in the sparse worktree; run from the main checkout it passes, with warnings only for the `attacked:` flags, which the engine sets itself. Still to run before shipping: register the story in `index.ts`, `npm run migrate`, and `npm run smoke -- --only=the-only-man-at-thornwitch`.
