## Why this fantasy works

The kicked-out-of-the-party fantasy usually starts after the betrayal, with the hero already somewhere safe and already powerful. This one starts during the fall, and it hands the player power over the people who betrayed them on turn one.

The player is a **support mage**, a binder. A binder ties thin threads of mana to the people beside them, so those people take less hurt, keep their footing and feel a fall coming before it happens. Highwater called it "shields" and thought it was the cheap part of the party. It was most of the reason they were alive.

When Lucan cuts the rope, **the threads do not break.** The player lands in black water at the bottom of the world and can still feel all four of them: Lucan, Elsbeth, Kes and Bran, climbing away. So the first question is not "how do I survive". It is the one the Voice asks: *do they get to reach the top?*

That gives every kind of player something to do at once:

- the revenge player can let go, or pull, and watch the Long Stair do the rest;
- the decent player can keep holding them up and let them find out, one floor at a time, what they threw away;
- the curious player can ask the blue light who she is;
- the power-fantasy player gets a new kind of binding from a mentor with her own agenda;
- the political player has a guild founder, a registry of the dead and a rival crew to play against each other on the surface.

Revenge is available and satisfying, and it is one road among several. None of them is the correct one.

## Story bible

### The world

**Lowmarch** is a city built in rings around the mouth of **the Deepway**, a dungeon of ninety-nine charted floors. Delving is the whole economy: guilds sponsor crews, crews bring up deepglass and salvage, and the **Tally House** at the lift-head writes every delver in on the way down and out on the way up. Anyone who does not come up goes into the **Book of the Lost**.

The Tally House enforces the **Delvers' Peace**: no delver raises a blade against another below the Rim. It has no soldiers. It has the book, and every guild needs its name in the book. A frayed rope is the Deepway. A clean cut is a person. A cut proven in front of the Tallykeeper strips the cutter of their share, their sponsor and their right to go down again.

Floors 1 to 90 have guild lifts and camps (the wayline). Below **Waycamp Ninety** it is stairs, ropes and nerve. Nobody had cleared **Floor 99** until today.

Below Floor 99 there is a floor that is on no chart: **the Sump**, where everything that falls anywhere in the Deepway ends up. Lamps, packs, boots, sixty years of cut ropes. And one woman.

### What happened today

Highwater (five delvers, sponsored by the **Gilt Rope Guild**) broke the stone warden on Floor 99 and opened the vault behind the **Last Door**. Inside was **the Heartglass**: a fist-sized core of black deepglass, warm, beating very slowly. The Gilt Rope pays 200,000 crowns for it. Five ways is 40,000 each. Four ways is 50,000.

Lucan Harrow owes the Gilt Rope 45,000 crowns, borrowed to outfit the descent and due the day after the clear.

On the way back over the pit, with the player last on the rope and anchoring everyone's crossing, Lucan knelt at the edge and cut it at ten past six in the evening.

- **Kes** had agreed the night before. She did the arithmetic.
- **Elsbeth** saw the knife and looked at the wall.
- **Bran** was two flights up the stair with the lamp. Lucan told him the player slipped.

The player fell past the bottom of every map into the Sump.

### The truth underneath

1. **Why Lucan really did it.** The money is real and it is not the whole of it. On Floor 70, Elsbeth found a letter from the Gilt Rope offering *the player* a crew of their own. She told Lucan, thinking he would be glad. He took the letter, never passed it on, and three floors later started talking about shares. "Nothing personal" is the most personal thing he has ever said.
2. **Why the Gilt Rope wanted the player.** Only a binder can hold the Heartglass without the deep floors waking around it. Odran Sallow sponsored Highwater *for its support mage* and wrote that letter. Lucan has just thrown away the only thing Sallow wanted from the expedition, and does not know it.
3. **Who the Voice is.** Sixty-one years ago, on Floor 61, Odran Sallow cut the rope of his own party's binder, **Agathe Lorne**, so that the founding prize would split four ways. The Gilt Rope Guild was built on that prize. Its gold rope emblem is that rope. Agathe fell to the Sump and survived by tying her own thread to the floor. Her body sleeps, sitting upright on a stone bench, in a hollow behind the Rope Field. What speaks is her, in the lichen-light, and she can reach anywhere on the bottom floor and, faintly, along any thread.
4. **Why she says "Finally."** Sound runs down the Deepway. She has heard every name spoken in it for sixty-one years, and she heard the player tying threads and humming on the forty-first floor. A binder fell. She has waited for one since the day she landed.
5. **What the Heartglass does.** In its vault it keeps the Deepway asleep. Out of the vault and not held by a binder, the deep floors wake, slowly: first the walls tick, then the stairs bite, then things from below Floor 90 come up. Whoever holds it, and can bind, can put the deep back to sleep, or wake it on purpose.

### The player's threads

At story start four threads are still tied: Lucan, Elsbeth, Kes, Bran. The player can:

- **hold them** (they climb safely and never know why; it costs the player's strength);
- **cut one**, or **let all four go** (the Long Stair stops being kind);
- **listen down one** (hear what is said at the other end, including things nobody would say to the player's face);
- **tug one** (a warning, a signal, or a fright; Bran, who was told a lie, can be told the truth this way).

A thread is felt, not hauled like a rope. A hard tug lands as a jolt, a cold shock at the wrist or a stumble, never enough by itself to throw anybody off a ledge or kill them. Cutting a thread or letting go only takes the ward away; after that the danger comes from the Long Stair and the waking deep, over hours, not in the same instant. This is in hard canon so turn one cannot drop Lucan and the Heartglass into the pit.

Elsbeth can feel her thread too. If she can still feel it, she knows the player is alive, and on the second day she turns round and comes back down alone.

### What everybody wants

- **The Voice** wants a binder to carry her thread out of the Deepway, and wants to stand in front of Odran Sallow. With the Heartglass she could leave on her own. She has not decided whether she would wake the deep under his hall if she did.
- **Lucan** wants to reach the Gilt Rope Hall, sell the Heartglass, clear his debt, and never have to say the real reason out loud.
- **Sallow** wants the Heartglass and a binder to hold it. When he learns the player lived, he will be the most generous man in the city.
- **Taro Kade** wants the Rust Company's name on the Tally House wall for once, and wants to be standing there when somebody shows Lucan a clean-cut rope.
- **Hesper Moss** wants the Book of the Lost to be true, and has suspected for thirty years that it is not.

## Tone and pacing

- Webnovel rhythm: short paragraphs, one hard image each, dialogue that cuts.
- The dungeon is physical: rope, lamp oil, wet stone, distance in floors and hours. Threads are felt in the wrists and fingers.
- The support mage's power is shown by what the threads do, never by numbers on a screen.
- Fights are quick and legible. Who has the higher ground, the light, the rope. Nothing graphic.
- The Voice is warm, curious and slightly wrong. Never a jump scare.
- Every betrayer is a different kind of guilty, and none of them is a cartoon.
- Revenge is allowed to feel good. It is never free.
- Romance is possible (Elsbeth, or Taro) and stays at the level of tension, honesty and at most a kiss.
- 13+: no sexual content, no lingering bodies, no graphic injury, no self-harm detail.

- Lucan cut the rope at 18:10. Kes agreed; Elsbeth looked away; Bran was told the player slipped.
- The Voice is real, cannot leave on her own, and does not lie outright. She withholds.

- The Tally House records the dead; a proven cut rope is the gravest crime in Lowmarch.
- Climbing out takes days. There is no wayline below Waycamp Ninety.

## The opener (turn 1)

114 words. A named character (Lucan) speaks at word 41. It ends on a direct question that has to be answered now. Every proper noun is introduced in the beat itself.

> You hang over the pit on the ninety-ninth floor, and the rope is all that holds you.
>
> Lucan kneels at the edge, his knife on the rope. Your best friend for eight years. Behind him, Elsbeth, your healer, looks away.
>
> "Nothing personal," Lucan says. "Four shares beat five."
>
> The rope parts.
>
> You fall a long way. Black water. Stone.
>
> In the dark, a woman’s voice, pleased: "Finally."
>
> Blue light spreads along the wall in the shape of her. She says your name like she has practised it.
>
> "Your threads are still tied to them, my dear. All four, climbing away, and you’re still holding them up.
>
> "So. Do they get to reach the top?"

**Suggestions (said / done / risky):**

1. *Said:* "Who are you? And how do you know my name?"
2. *Done:* I find the four threads in the dark and hold on. Nobody else falls today.
3. *Risky:* I take Lucan’s thread in my fist and pull, hard.

The Voice reacts to the exact move. If the player pulls, Lucan stumbles on a ledge two floors up and Kes catches his collar, and the Voice laughs like somebody who has not laughed in a long time.

## Major cast

Every character is an adult. Ages are as of the day of the fall.

### The player, 26

Highwater's binder. Met Lucan at eighteen, sharing a bunk at the delvers' hostel; they founded Highwater together that year. Name and pronouns are the player's; what they did with their threads is the archetype.

### Lucan Harrow, 26

**Role:** Highwater's leader and swordsman, the player's best friend since they shared a bunk at the delvers' hostel at eighteen, and the man who cut the rope.
**Wants:** sell the Heartglass at the Gilt Rope Hall, clear his 45,000-crown debt, keep the other three believing it was the only way.
**Hides:** the Gilt Rope letter offering the player a crew, still in his coat; the debt note, which Sallow holds personally.
**Fears:** being ordinary; that the player is alive, and that he is relieved.
**Voice:** easy and warm, a leader's "we" even now. Jokes to lower the temperature. Cornered, he goes formal and uses the player's full name, which he has done three times in eight years, every time while lying.
> "Nothing personal. Four shares beat five."
> "We were going to be the first. First. Not good for a support crew. First."

### The Voice (Agathe Lorne), 87 by the calendar

**Role:** what speaks on the bottom floor. The binder of the party that founded the Gilt Rope Guild, cut loose on Floor 61 sixty-one years ago. The shape she makes in the lichen-light is the woman she was at twenty-six.
**Wants:** a binder to carry her thread out; to stand in front of Odran Sallow.
**Hides:** her name, until it is earned; that her body sleeps, sitting up, in the Hollow; that the Heartglass would let her leave alone.
**Fears:** another sixty years; that there is not enough of her left to walk out with.
**Boundary:** she will not force the player to do anything, because a forced binding does not hold. She gives her name when it is earned: when the player asks what happened to her rather than what she is, or says the name on her badge out loud. That rule is in her boundaries in the fixture, because the storyteller never reads reveal hints.
**Voice:** old-fashioned and precise, sixty years out of date ("shall", "rather", "my dear"). Asks questions she already knows the answers to. Laughs at things that are not funny, because nothing has been funny for a very long time.
> "Finally."
> "I shall not make you do anything. A thread that is forced does not hold. Ask any of the four you are still holding up."

### Elsbeth Marr, 24

**Role:** Highwater's healer, who joined three years ago at twenty-one, saw Lucan's knife and looked at the wall.
**Wants:** to go back down and find out whether the player is dead; to say what she did without asking to be forgiven for it.
**Hides:** she is the one who told Lucan about the letter, on Floor 70; she has been in love with the player for two years and decided on Floor 99 that she had lost the right to say so.
**Fears:** that she is a coward and always has been.
**Voice:** quick, practical, medical. Counts pulses and breaths out loud when frightened. Says sorry about small things constantly and not yet about the big one. Swears by the Saint of the Stair.
> "I looked at the wall. That's the whole of what I did. I looked at the wall, and I counted to eleven, and you were gone."

### Taro Kade, 27

**Role:** captain of the Rust Company, eleven salvagers who have come second to Highwater on every floor for three years. Camped on Floor 98, one floor above the pit.
**Wants:** the Heartglass, or the credit, or a cut of either; all eleven of his people home.
**Hides:** Sallow's people paid him a week ago to slow Highwater down, and he took the money and did not do it; his father was cut loose on Floor 52 and written down as misadventure.
**Fears:** being the crew that only ever picks up after.
**Boundary:** he will never cut a rope, for anything.
**Voice:** loud market patter. Everything has a price and he says the number. Calls everyone "friend" until they aren't. A spoken deal is a deal; he does not sign things.
> "Well, look what climbed out of the drain. Friend, you are worth a fortune right now and you are dripping on my maps."

### Kestrel "Kes" Ivo, 22

**Role:** Highwater's scout and archer, who joined two years ago at twenty. Agreed to the four shares because she did the arithmetic.
**Wants:** her share, to clear the mortgage on her mother's house in the Rim; to work out why the climb up is so much harder than the descent was.
**Hides:** she has seen Lucan's debt note and knows the number.
**Fears:** owing the player her life, and knowing it.
**Voice:** clipped, numerical, dry. Prices, distances, odds. Calls the player "support", never their name, until the day she stops.
> "Five ways is forty thousand each. Four ways is fifty. I can count, support. So can you."

### Bran Tull, 31

**Role:** Highwater's shieldbearer, who joined five years ago at twenty-six. Two flights up with the lamp when the rope went.
**Wants:** to go home to his wife, Odette (30), with his share; to find out why Lucan keeps checking the box instead of the stair.
**Hides:** he saw the cut end when Lucan threw it into the pit, and has been not-thinking about it ever since.
**Fears:** finding out he carried the lamp for a murder.
**Voice:** very few words, low and slow. Uses names. Asks one question and waits for the whole answer. Never swears.
> "You slipped. That's what he said. You slipped."

### Odran Sallow, 88

**Role:** Guildmaster and founder of the Gilt Rope Guild. Lives above the guild vault.
**Wants:** the Heartglass and a binder to hold it, before he dies.
**Hides:** Floor 61; that he has heard a woman's voice in the hall's courtyard well every night for sixty-one years.
**Fears:** dying with the Heartglass out of his hand; her.
**Voice:** formal, slow, courteous. Offers tea before anything. Uses the passive voice for everything he has done: "a rope was lost".
> "Ropes are lost every season. The Deepway takes a share. It always has."

### Hesper Moss, 56

**Role:** Tallykeeper of the Tally House. Wrote the player's name into the Book of the Lost on Lucan's word.
**Wants:** the book to be true; one misadventure she can prove was not.
**Hides:** a second list in the margin, forty-one names she never believed, Agathe Lorne the first.
**Fears:** that the book has been lying for sixty years with her hand on it.
**Voice:** procedural and dry. Speaks in the book's order: name, floor, cause, witness. Asks "and the witness?" about everything.
> "Name. Floor. Cause. Witness. In that order, please, and slowly. I'm writing."

## Places

**The bottom (the Sump region)**

- **The Sump** (start). The floor under the last floor. A black pool, a shore of sixty years of fallen things, cold blue lichen that brightens when somebody speaks.
- **The Rope Field.** A cavern where every cut rope in the Deepway hangs from the ceiling like roots. Guild badges in the silt. A camp ring and tally marks from long ago.
- **The Echo Shaft.** A dry chimney where sound from every floor comes down. What you say into it goes up.
- **The Hollow** (hidden until her name is known). Agathe's body, asleep and sitting upright on a stone bench, her coat green with lichen, breathing once a minute, a thread of light running from her wrist down into the floor.
- **The Spine.** A drain shaft climbing from the Sump to the underside of Floor 99. Things that eat lamplight live in its cracks.

**The charted deep**

- **The Last Door** (Floor 99). The ledge where the rope was cut, the broken warden, the empty vault.
- **Rust Company Camp** (Floor 98). Eleven tents, a cookfire, a salvage pile, Taro.
- **The Long Stair** (98 to 90). Eight floors of railless switchback stair. It never bit on the way down.
- **Waycamp Ninety.** The deepest guild camp: the lift-cage, bunks, the chalk board.

**The surface**

- **The Rim.** The city in rings around the mouth.
- **The Tally House.** The Book of the Lost under a lamp that has not gone out in sixty years.
- **The Gilt Rope Hall.** Marble, gold rope on every doorframe, a courtyard well that goes down too far.

Every place has a way in. The Hollow is the only one that is hidden, and it opens from the Rope Field once the player knows her name.

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Mana** (the only thing rest restores). Holding four threads drains it. If it runs out, the threads slip one by one, furthest first.
- **Tremor** (descending is good). How awake the deep floors are. It rises on its own while the Heartglass is out of the vault and unheld, and fighting makes it worse.
- **Undertow** (descending is good). How much the bottom floor has its hooks in the player. Deep binding, cutting threads and carrying the Voice all raise it.

## Five promises

1. **What you do to the man who cut your rope** (finale). Seed: a rope end cut too clean to be anything but a knife. Payoff: Lucan face to face, with the Heartglass, the letter he never passed on, and nobody left on his side.
2. **Who the voice in the dark used to be** (mystery). Seed: she says your name like she has practised it, and will not say her own. Payoff: a founder's badge in the Rope Field, and a woman asleep on a stone bench in the hollow behind it.
3. **The one who looked at the wall** (relationship). Seed: one thread keeps tugging gently, as if somebody is checking it is still there. Payoff: Elsbeth coming back down alone, and what she told Lucan on Floor 70.
4. **The man who did it first** (boss). Seed: a gold rope stamped on a sixty-one-year-old badge, the same as on the sponsor's contract. Payoff: Odran Sallow at the head of his table, and the rope he cut on Floor 61.
5. **What a support mage was actually worth** (theme). Seed: the Long Stair bites on the way up; it never once did on the way down. Payoff: four people learning, one floor at a time, what the player was holding.
