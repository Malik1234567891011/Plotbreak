# NOVA HOUSE

**Slate:** #3 of the 50 (drop 3). Psychic academy, slice of life, with a murder plot underneath.  
**Audience lean:** both.  
**OOC evidence:** Psychic Academy NOVA, 7.8M plays. Nothing is borrowed from it but the shelf it sits on: our college, house, cast, powers and plot are our own.  
**Title check (PLAN §7.3):** "Nova" is the one distinctive word in OOC's Psychic Academy NOVA, on the same shelf. The title is not copied word for word, so it passes §7, but the owner should know. Editor search (2026-11-22) for "Nova House" with anime, webtoon and manhwa found no existing work of that name; the nearest is *Himote House* (2018 anime), a comedy with a different premise. If more distance is wanted, change only the display `title` and the cover TITLE line; keep storyId, slug and asset prefix.  
**Genre:** slice of life, mystery, supernatural, light romance, drama.  
**One-line fantasy:** You read minds. Your three housemates know it, and at your first dinner one of them thinks about killing you too.

## Why this works

Two fantasies in one house. The first is the telepath's: you hear what people actually think, and everybody around you knows you can. The second is the house: four strangers, a kitchen, a rota, a roof you are not supposed to go on after midnight, and six days of dinners together.

The murder plot is what makes the slice of life matter. Every ordinary scene (washing up with Haru, Lena's drawings, the roof at night with Sable) is also a scene with a suspect in it. A player can solve it, ignore it, make it worse, or end up holding the glass.

The power has a real price. Reading someone works, and it costs you their trust. The best answers in this story come from people who choose to open up, not from minds you break into.

## Story bible

**Halloran College** is a residential college, up a hill above the river town of **Lowbridge**, for adults whose minds do things they should not. Every student is 18 or older. Most are 19 to 24. It has big, cheerful houses for each kind of ability, and one small one.

**Nova House** is the small one: a narrow old house at the bottom of the grounds, by the orchard wall, with four student rooms, a house mother, and an observatory dome on the roof. It is named for a new star seen from that dome in the house's first winter. The college puts the students the bigger houses cannot hold here.

The player arrives this afternoon. Their telepathy surfaced late, after they turned nineteen, and the college assigned them **Room Four**. Until April, Room Four belonged to a telepath called **Corin Abbott**. Officially, Corin transferred.

The housemates know what the player is. The first house rule, chalked by the coat hooks in four handwritings: *nobody reads anybody at the table.*

At the first dinner one thought comes through anyway:

> "If the new one hears this, I'll have to kill them too."

Nobody's lips moved.

### The truth (hard canon, never volunteered)

- **The thought was Sable Quint's.** Sable's mind is silent because she holds a wall nobody can read through, and within arm's reach of her every ability stops working. At dinner Lena mentioned the Founders' Night tradition (the newest resident pours the toast), Sable thought of the Provost and the new telepath's hands on the decanter, and for one second her wall slipped. "Them too" meant the player as well as the Provost. It was fear talking, and she is ashamed of it.
- **Sable plans to kill Provost Nathaniel Sorrell on Founders' Night,** six days from now, by putting a stolen vial of Stillwater into his glass at the Nova House toast. She intends to walk to the wardens herself afterwards.
- **Stillwater** is the college's suppressant. A small dose dulls an ability for a day. A course of it, with the Provost's voice, removes an ability for good, along with the memories tied to it. The college calls that **settling**. A whole vial in a drink would stop a heart. Ruth keeps two emergency vials in a locked tin in the kitchen. One is missing.
- **Sorrell has settled eleven students in twenty years** and filed every one as a voluntary withdrawal. Corin was the eleventh, in April, after he read the list in Sorrell's mind. Sable was ordered to sit with Corin "to keep him calm"; her null is what held his ability still while the Stillwater worked. She has not forgiven herself.
- **Sorrell can steer.** Within earshot, his voice makes agreement feel like your own idea. A strong wall resists it.
- **Sorrell put the player in Nova House on purpose.** The welcome packet holds a Wellbeing Form: a page a week on how the housemates are sleeping, handed to him in person. He wants eyes on Haru, who he is sure will burn somebody, and on Sable, who he suspects is shielding the house.
- **Haru Ezaki has two fires on his record.** A third means settling. He has a one-way bus ticket for Founders' Night and has told nobody.
- **Lena Morrow sees the future as still frames:** always right about what, never about who. She has drawn Founders' Night: the Provost on the parlour floor, a spilled glass, a hand with a silver ring on the thumb, and the player in the doorway. She has never once broken a frame.
- **Corin is not empty.** The settling missed one locked room in his mind: the list he read. A telepath who goes carefully can open it.

## Tone and pacing

- Slice of life first, thriller underneath. Dinners, classes, laundry, the roof at night, a trip into town.
- Thoughts arrive as overheard fragments, in plain quotes with a clear cue that nobody spoke.
- Every mind has a texture: Haru's is heat and kitchen noise; Lena's runs slightly ahead of the room; Sable's is silence; Ruth's is a radio turned low; Sorrell's is warm and agreeable like a thick carpet; Corin's is a house with the furniture taken out.
- Funny, often: house rules, Haru's jokes, Lena's tense slips, Ruth's soup.
- The player does not know whose thought it was. All three housemates stay plausible until the player earns the answer.
- Violence is rare and has weight. No gore. Death happens offstage or in a cut. Romance can go as far as a kiss.
- Reading somebody without asking is a real act with a real cost, and people can tell.
- Reading never solves the mystery on its own. Haru's surface is loud and sounds guilty out of context (Saturday, a bag, getting out, smoke); Lena's runs ahead to a frame she will not describe; Sable's is silence, which reads as innocence. Nobody confesses at the first dinner. A secret comes out when it is earned: asking before reading, eating what Haru cooked, sitting with somebody without fixing them, or bringing evidence to one person alone. Reading without asking gets fragments, never a whole secret.
- A thought whose owner the player does not know is narration, never a line with a speaker, and never sets that character's face.
- Sable's slip at dinner was loud enough that every kind of telepath heard it as exact words.
- This guidance lives in the fixture's `rules.toneGuide`, because production (`runTurnPure`) never reads quest-step notes or secret reveal hints.

## Opening (the first beat)

> The woman ladling soup slides a bowl across to you. "I'm Lena. House rule: nobody reads anybody at dinner. Deal?"
>
> You nod. You try.
>
> The table's thoughts leak in anyway, like a radio in the next room. Too much salt. Don't stare at the new one. Heat ticking off the man across the table.
>
> Then one thought cuts clean through the rest.
>
> "If the new one hears this, I'll have to kill them too."
>
> Your spoon stops halfway. Nobody's lips moved.
>
> The man is tearing bread. The pale woman at the end turns her glass, perfectly silent inside. Lena tilts her head.
>
> "Something wrong with the soup?"

Checks against the quality bar: Lena speaks to the player in the first 15 words. The beat ends on her direct question. 106 words. The only proper noun is Lena's, and she says it herself.

**Suggestions** (said, done, risky):
1. "It's great. Just a long day," I lie, and pick my spoon back up.
2. I break the house rule and listen harder, one mind at a time.
3. "I heard that. Someone at this table just threatened me. Which one of you?"

## Major cast

Every character is an adult. Ages are stated and fixed.

### Haru Ezaki (21) — pyrokinetic, third-year
**Role:** the loud one. The first suspect, because his mind is full of fire.  
**Look:** tall and restless, bleached hair with dark roots, a burn-scarred left hand, sleeves always shoved up, a brass lighter he does not need.  
**Want:** get through his third-year review without a third fire.  
**Private:** he wants to be somewhere nobody has heard of him. A bag is packed under his bed and a bus ticket for Founders' Night is in his jacket.  
**Fear:** hurting somebody; becoming the person the college thinks he is.  
**Secret:** his first fire, at eighteen, during a Friday service, took his father's restaurant. Nobody died. His father has not spoken to him since.  
**Voice:** fast, joking, kitchen slang ("behind", "service", "plate it"). Jokes about heat before anybody else can.  
**Bond:** Sable is the only person he can stand next to without checking his hands.

### Lena Morrow (20) — precog, second-year
**Role:** the friendly one who already knew you were coming. Asks the opening question.  
**Look:** quick and wiry, dark curls under a knitted band, charcoal on her fingers, a sketchbook everywhere.  
**Want:** break one frame, once, to prove the future can be moved.  
**Private:** she drew the player's face at the table three weeks ago, spoon halfway up. Her question in the opener was a test.  
**Fear:** that every frame is fixed and she is only a witness.  
**Secrets:** she has drawn Founders' Night (the Provost on the parlour floor, a spilled glass, a ringed thumb, the player in the doorway). She also has a frame of Haru alone on a night bus that she has never shown him.  
**Voice:** warm, chatty, slips between tenses ("you took the chair... sorry, you're about to"). Never says a frame out loud, because saying it makes it heavier.

### Sable Quint (23) — null, fourth-year
**Role:** the silent one. Her mind is a wall; within arm's reach of her, abilities stop. The one the player rules out first. The planner.  
**Look:** pale, very still, black hair cut blunt at the jaw, a grey wool coat, a silver ring on her left thumb that used to be Corin's.  
**Want:** make the Provost stop, permanently, on Founders' Night.  
**Private:** after the toast she means to hand herself in. She is not planning an escape.  
**Fear:** being furniture: the thing the college puts in a room to make people calm, and the thing that held Corin still.  
**Secrets:** the plan and the vial; her part in Corin's settling; the Provost has scheduled her for "study" after Founders' Night.  
**Voice:** short and exact. Answers questions with questions. Measures everything in arm's lengths.  
**Romance hook:** inside her reach, a telepath's head goes quiet for the first time. She is the only silence in the world.

### Ruth Calder (58) — House Mother
**Role:** runs the house. Cooks, keeps the keys, walks the landing at curfew.  
**Look:** broad and grey-haired, reading glasses on her head, apron over a cardigan, a big ring of keys.  
**Past:** twenty years as a police empath, feeling what people felt across interview tables.  
**Want:** every one of her four alive and still in the house after Founders' Night.  
**Fear:** that she is complicit, and that kindness was her excuse.  
**Secrets:** she signed Corin's transfer in April because the Provost called it kindness, and has kept a file ever since. She knows a vial is missing, suspects Sable, and has not reported it.  
**Voice:** house-rule firmness, numbered rules, ends hard conversations with an order about food.

### Nathaniel Sorrell (61) — Provost of Halloran College
**Role:** the kind man in the tower, and the reason for the plot.  
**Look:** silver hair, a soft cardigan under a good jacket, rimless glasses, a cup of tea he never finishes.  
**Ability:** steering. His voice makes agreement feel like your own idea.  
**Want:** settle Haru before the fire he is sure is coming; study Sable; make the new telepath his eyes.  
**Why:** his sister died at nineteen in a fire set by a student who could not hold it. He has spent forty years making sure nobody else gets that telephone call, and he believes it.  
**Secret:** eleven settlements in twenty years, every one filed as voluntary.  
**Voice:** gentle, generous, ends on questions you find yourself answering yes to.

### Corin Abbott (22) — gardener at the glasshouse, formerly a telepath
**Role:** the eleventh. The evidence nobody thinks of as evidence.  
**Look:** lanky, soft-spoken, soil on his knees, a cardigan with the elbows gone, a pencil stub behind his ear.  
**State:** settled in April. No ability, no memory of Nova House, and a gap where last spring was.  
**Private:** he keeps stopping at the Nova House gate without knowing why, and hums a song Sable used to play on the roof.  
**Secret:** one locked room in his mind survived: the list he read in Sorrell's head, ten names and his own added at the bottom. (His page behind the wardrobe says "Eleven. Haru is next.")  
**Voice:** gentle, pleasant, loses the thread, says "I think" a lot, talks about plants.

## Five promises

1. **The toast (finale):** whether anybody drinks from that glass on Founders' Night.
2. **Whose thought (mystery):** a mind the player cannot hear at all, turning a glass at the end of the table.
3. **The kind man (boss):** what the Provost actually does with the students he worries about.
4. **The house (relationship):** whether four strangers become a household.
5. **What a mind owes (theme):** what it costs to read people who did not ask.

## Emergent routes

Solve it by reading; solve it by asking; solve it by searching rooms. Accuse the wrong person. Help Sable. Stop Sable. Talk her down. Take the vial. Pour it yourself. Warn the Provost. Work for the Provost. Expose him with the files, or with Corin's locked room. Get Haru on the bus, or off it. Leave the college. Fall for a housemate.
