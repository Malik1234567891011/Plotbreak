# EVERY PLATFORM A WORLD

**Slate:** #38 · **Slug:** `every-platform-a-world` · **Export:** `EVERY_PLATFORM_A_WORLD` · **Asset prefix:** `story_every_platform_a_world`
**Genre:** choose-your-world sandbox, fantasy, light mystery, ensemble comedy, optional romance
**Audience lean:** both
**One-line fantasy:** You are the night stationmaster at a station where every platform leads to a different world, and on your first shift three stranded travellers put their hands on the only golden ticket.
**Hook (Discover card):** Three stranded travellers, one golden ticket, and only your punch can send one of them home. Your first night as stationmaster of a station between worlds.

## Why this fantasy works

The hook is a choice the player can make in one tap: three people, one ticket, only you can punch it. That is the whole first beat, and it needs no lore to land. A samurai, an astronaut and a vampire queen standing at the same railway counter is funny before it is anything else, and each of them has a deadline that is real.

Underneath the choice is a sandbox. Six platforms are lit and lead to six other worlds. Lost Property holds everything anybody has ever left on a train. The Conductor knows every rule and answers only the exact question asked. The old stationmaster is forty minutes away by train, eating fish on a harbour. A player who wants to choose on turn one can, and a player who wants to find a way to save all three can spend the whole night looking for it.

The second layer is the player's own ticket. It came in an envelope with their name on it. Every stationmaster is given one, once, to anywhere. "Choose your world" is literal: the best ending is the one where the player gets everybody home and still has a ticket of their own in their pocket, and then has to decide whether to use it.

## Story bible

Lantern Cross is a railway station between worlds. It opens at dusk and closes at dawn. It has nine platforms, and while a platform's lamp burns, its train runs to one world.

The player took the job of night stationmaster. Tonight is their first shift. At sunset Ida Fennimore, stationmaster for forty-one years, handed them her brass ticket punch, wished them luck and caught the 21:10 from Platform 1 to retire on the Salt Coast.

At midnight the lamps of Platforms 4, 6 and 8 went out at once. Ida had been keeping those three weak lamps lit by hand for years, and when she left they failed. The station clock stopped at the same moment. Three travellers were stranded on the concourse, one from each dark line.

There is one golden ticket. It came in an envelope addressed to the player, because every stationmaster is given one, once, to anywhere. Punched for a dark platform it relights that lamp long enough for one train, one passenger, one trip. Only the stationmaster's punch validates a ticket.

The story opens five minutes after midnight with the three travellers' hands on that ticket.

### The station's rules (hard canon)

1. Lantern Cross opens at dusk and closes at dawn. Nine platforms; a lit lamp means a running line to one world.
2. Platforms 4, 6 and 8 are dark. Platforms 1, 2, 3, 5, 7 and 9 are lit: 1 to the Salt Coast, 2 to a desert of glass, 3 to a city built on bridges, 5 to the Owl Market, 7 to a snowfield of sleeping giants, 9 to a forest that is all one tree.
3. A golden ticket punched for a dark platform relights it for one train, one passenger, one trip.
4. Only the stationmaster's punch validates a ticket. In anyone else's hand it is cold brass.
5. A dark lamp can also be relit by a light carried from its own world, given up freely by the person who carried it. A stolen light will not catch. Each of the three travellers is carrying one without knowing it matters.
6. At 06:00 somebody must stand at the master lamp in the Lamp Room. Whoever is there becomes stationmaster. If nobody is, every lamp in the station goes out.
7. The stationmaster may step off at any lit platform, but one who is in another world at dawn cannot come back to the job.
8. The Conductor runs every train, answers exactly the question asked, cannot climb the Lamp Room stair, and can stop any fight on the concourse with a word. He cannot command the stationmaster. His silver pocket watch is the only clock in the station still running, and he gives the time to the minute whenever he speaks.
9. Trimming by hand only keeps a weak lamp from going out. A lamp that has gone dark will not take a match, a wick or a hand: only a golden ticket punched for it, or a light from its own world given freely, relights it. Nothing crosses a dark line, not a train, not a tender, not a traveller on foot; past a dark platform the tunnel ends in cold brick.

### The best night, and why nobody hands it over

Fully investigated, the night can save everybody, and every part of it costs somebody something:

- **The ticket is worth most to Ren.** With it he goes home and keeps his clan's flame. Without it, he can only relight Platform 4 by giving the flame to the lamp, which ends his lord's last order.
- **Okafor can relight Platform 6 with her survey beacon.** That burns the logged route with it, so her Directorate can never find the station. She has to disobey her orders to do it.
- **Isolde can relight Platform 8 with her moonstone crown-ring.** Nobody can ever be crowned with it again. She wants to put the crown down and is ashamed of wanting it.
- **Ida's torn ticket** is a second golden ticket, if the player finds the half in Lost Property, takes the train to the Salt Coast, and tells Ida honestly who it is for.
- **Somebody still has to hold the lamp at six.** If the player wants to use their own ticket, somebody else has to take the job. Isolde has wanted it for years.

Nobody volunteers the long way. The Conductor answers only the right question, Ida's ledger has to be read, Lost Property has to be searched, and the lamp-wright at the Owl Market charges an hour of the night for one answer.

## Tone and pacing

- A night shift at a railway station that happens to connect worlds. Warm, quick, a little uncanny.
- Small physical detail carries it: the stopped clock, the samovar, frost on one platform and cedar smoke on another, three hands on one ticket.
- Every beat is somebody wanting something from the stationmaster, out loud.
- The three travellers come from genres that do not belong together. The comedy of a samurai, an astronaut and a vampire queen sharing one waiting room is allowed to be funny while the clock runs.
- Nobody at the counter is a villain. Everyone has a reasonable claim.
- Deadlines fire on time whether or not the player is looking.
- Violence on the concourse is short, legible and costly. The Conductor ends it.
- Romance with any of the three adults is allowed: flirting, tension, at most a kiss.

## Opening

The opener is the first beat. 113 words. A named character (Ren) speaks at word 13 and is named at word 22. It ends on a direct question. The only proper noun is Ren, who introduces himself.

> A golden ticket lies on your counter. Three hands are on it.
>
> "Stationmaster." The samurai doesn't raise his voice. "I am Ren. My lord's castle burns at dawn."
>
> "Item one: my crew has four hours of air," says the astronaut beside him, helmet under her arm.
>
> The third is a queen in black lace, pale as the clock face above her. "They crown my cousin at moonset. I intend to be there to object."
>
> Behind them, three platforms stand dark. Each led to a different world until midnight. One ticket can wake one of them, and only your punch makes it valid.
>
> Nobody lets go.
>
> "One ticket," Ren says. "Choose, stationmaster. Who rides?"

Suggestions, three different moves (said holds off, done chooses, risky claims):

- **Said:** "Hands off, all of you. I'm hearing each of you out first."
- **Done:** I take the ticket out from under their hands and hold it out to the astronaut.
- **Risky:** I put my hand flat on top of all three. "Actually, it's mine."

The player can also punch it for anyone at once, refuse, walk off to look at the dark platforms, go for tea, attack someone, flirt, lie, or leave the station. The travellers react to the exact thing done.

## Major cast

All five are adults. The player is an adult.

### Ren Shiomi — 27

**Role:** samurai of House Uraga, stranded behind Platform 4.
**Appearance:** tall and narrow, hair tied back and singed at the ends, a scorched travelling coat over plain dark armour, two swords, soot on one cheek. A black lacquered box tied at his hip with red cord.
**Says he wants:** to get home before his lord's castle burns at dawn.
**Actually wants:** his lord means to burn Castle Akebono at dawn with the besieging army inside it. Three hundred townspeople are asleep in the lower bailey and have not been told. Ren intends to open the river gate and get them out. That is treason, and House Uraga executes traitors.
**Second secret:** the lacquered box holds his clan's ancestral flame. His lord's last order was to carry it out and keep the house alive. It is the light that could relight Platform 4, at the cost of the flame.
**Fear:** arriving after the fire; choosing the flame over the people.
**Voice:** formal, spare, unhurried. Calls the player "stationmaster". States a fact and stops. Jokes so dry that people miss them.
**Lines:**
- "Choose, stationmaster. One ticket does not divide."
- "A blade drawn indoors is rude. I am trying very hard tonight to be polite."
- "Three hundred people are asleep under my lord's walls. None of them knows I promised them anything."

### Commander Adaeze Okafor — 38

**Role:** commander of the survey ship *Long Patience*, stranded behind Platform 6.
**Appearance:** close-cropped hair going grey at one temple, dark skin, a fresh burn across the knuckles, an orange pressure suit with the collar ring open and sleeves pushed up, a grey thermal underneath, helmet under one arm. A survey beacon on her chest blinking white every four seconds.
**Says she wants:** to get back to her crew, six people on reserve air in a dead orbit, with the reactor part she came through to find.
**True:** all of that is true. Her tender set down on Platform 6 at half past nine, after Ida had gone, and she spent two hours finding a reactor coupling on Ida's shelf of spares in Lost Property. At midnight her line went dark behind her.
**Secret:** she did not find the station by accident. Her Outer Survey Directorate found Platform 6's lamp in old telemetry. Her sealed orders: locate the station, log a repeatable route, recover the device that controls its tickets if possible. Her beacon has logged the route since she arrived. Giving the beacon to the lamp would relight Platform 6 and burn the route with it.
**Fear:** reaching her crew an hour late; handing this place to people who would fence it.
**Voice:** brisk and warm. Counts things out, "Item one. Item two." Says "copy" for yes. Gives the real number, then jokes so nobody has to react.
**Lines:**
- "Item one, my crew has four hours of reserve air. Item two, I have the part. Item three is you."
- "Copy. Bad news first, always. Good news is cheaper and keeps longer."
- "Copy. I'll be scared at ten past four. It's on the list."

### Queen Isolde of Ostravel — 312 (looks about thirty)

**Role:** vampire queen of Ostravel, a kingdom where it is always night, stranded behind Platform 8.
**Appearance:** black hair pinned with silver, pale, a high-collared gown of black lace and velvet, long gloves, a moonstone ring worn over the glove.
**Says she wants:** to get home before moonset and stop her cousin Casimir's coronation.
**The lie:** she does not want her crown back. She ran from it. If Casimir is crowned she is free, and she is ashamed of how much she wants that, because Casimir means to shut Ostravel's human quarter behind its gates at night.
**Second secret:** for forty years she came to Lantern Cross on this night to play cards with Ida in the Lamp Room. They joked she should be night stationmaster after Ida. Isolde stopped joking a long time ago.
**Third secret:** the moonstone ring is Ostravel's crown-ring and holds moonlight from home. Given to Platform 8's lamp it would relight her kingdom's line for good, and nobody could be crowned with it again. It brightens very slightly whenever she says "home": the tell a sharp player can catch.
**Rule she keeps:** she does not feed on anyone at Lantern Cross. Nothing about feeding is ever shown.
**Fear:** being seen to abandon Ostravel; wanting something for herself and being refused.
**Voice:** arch, amused, unhurried. "Darling" when dismissive, the player's name when not. Long elegant sentences that end on a short sharp one.
**Lines:**
- "They crown my cousin at moonset. I intend to be there to object."
- "Darling, I have outlived four dynasties and a great many people who told me to calm down. Punch the ticket."
- "Ida cheated at cards for forty years. Badly. I let her. Do you play?"
- "I have sat through eleven coronations, darling, and I can tell you they are mostly hats."

Voice samples show the voice only. None of them may hint at a secret: the writer model copies samples word for word.

### The Conductor — centuries old (looks about 60)

**Role:** runs every train through Lantern Cross.
**Appearance:** tall, lean, a weathered face with deep lines and grey stubble, a long black coat with brass buttons, white gloves, a peaked cap. A silver pocket watch that keeps running when every station clock has stopped. His face is always plainly visible.
**Wants:** somebody at the lamp at 06:00. He does not care who.
**Secret:** he was the first stationmaster, Caspar Ashdown. Centuries ago he followed someone through a platform and was not back at dawn, and every lamp went out for a hundred years. When it relit he was on the trains, and he has never been able to climb the stair since.
**Also knows:** the home-light rule, and Ida's 05:00 message ("Ask him what he was").
**Voice:** clipped and exact. Times to the minute. "Correct" and "Incorrect" instead of yes and no. Never asks a question back.
**Lines:**
- "Platform 1, ten past every hour, forty minutes. Last return from the Salt Coast, 05:15."
- "You asked whether a ticket can light a lamp. Correct. You did not ask whether a ticket is the only thing that can."
- "At 06:00 someone will be standing at the lamp, or no one will. I have seen both."

### Ida Fennimore — 73

**Role:** stationmaster for forty-one years, retired at sunset tonight to a room over a fish restaurant on the Salt Coast.
**Appearance:** short and broad, white hair cut short, a swimming towel round her neck, a cardigan with brass station buttons, a leather purse held shut with a rubber band, always on her knee.
**Wants:** the first night of her retirement in peace, and to know, without asking, whether the new one is any good.
**Secret:** forty-one years ago she arrived as a stranded traveller and was given a golden ticket. She tore it in half so she could not change her mind. One half is in a Lost Property drawer marked TICKETS, ASSORTED. The other is in her purse. Mended and freely given, it works again.
**Also:** she knew the three weak lamps would go out when she left, and went anyway.
**Will not:** come back to Lantern Cross; give her half to someone who wants it for themselves.
**Voice:** warm, plain, quick. Calls everybody "love". Feeds you before she answers.
**Lines:**
- "Sit down, love, eat something, you look like a timetable."
- "Those three lamps were always going to go when I did. I kept them going by hand and bad temper."
- "Tell Isolde she can have my chair. Don't tell her I said so."

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Stamina** (ascending, restored by rest and tea): how much night the stationmaster has left in them.
- **Dimming** (descending, first in order): how troubled the station's lamps are. Violence, broken rules and force raise it; it settles on its own when the concourse is calm. High bands make the lit lines falter.
- **Unrest** (descending): how close the three travellers are to taking matters into their own hands. Promises, force and laying down the law raise it. High bands mean alliances, grabs for the punch and the standoff.

## Five promises

1. **Who rides, and who holds the lamp (finale).** Seed: three hands on one ticket. Payoff: a punch, a train, and the person standing at the lamp at six.
2. **Which of them is lying (mystery).** Seed: Isolde reaches for the ticket a little slower than the others, and her ring brightens when she says "home". Payoff: Isolde does not want her crown; Ren's need is true and costs him his head; Okafor's is true and comes with orders.
3. **The long way round (theme).** Seed: Ida's note says "Ask the old man the right question". Payoff: lights carried from home, given up freely, and a ticket torn in half forty-one years ago.
4. **What the Conductor was (mystery).** Seed: he goes everywhere in the station except up the Lamp Room stair. Payoff: the first stationmaster, who once was not at the lamp at dawn.
5. **A ticket with your name on it (theme).** Seed: Ida's envelope. Payoff: every stationmaster gets one, once, to anywhere, and the last question of the night is whether you use it.

## Threads (quests)

- **One Ticket** (main, active): the counter, then who rides.
- **Three Stories, One Lie** (main): what each of them is really going home to.
- **The Long Way Round** (lead): how else a dark lamp can be lit. Teaches *Relight a Lamp*.
- **A Light for Platform 4 / 6 / 8** (three leads, open once the home-light rule is known): one quest per dark lamp, so all three can be relit in one night. The engine closes a step on its first matching route, so a shared step could only ever light one.
- **The Other Half** (lead): the torn ticket, Ida, the mend, then a second rider on the mended ticket.
- **The River Gate** (Ren), **Reserve Air** (Okafor), **A Crown at Moonset** (Isolde): one side thread each.
- **Step Through** (side): ride a relit line into a traveller's world, and be back by six or not.
- **Someone at the Lamp** (main): six o'clock.
- **What the Conductor Was** (lead): his name, and whether he can be let off the trains.

## Content and originality

- Rated 13+. All characters are adults and every age is stated above, including the named characters who never reach the counter. Nobody is described as childlike, and no crowd scene (the river gate, the Regulars) adds a minor.
- Romance stays at flirting, tension and a kiss. No fan service.
- Violence is short and never graphic. Isolde's feeding is never shown; she keeps a no-feeding rule at the station.
- The burning castle is a threat; no one is shown burning. Okafor's crew go into cold sleep, alive.
- Original names and places. No franchise terms. The Conductor has a plainly visible, weathered face and is not modelled on any existing train conductor character. No platform has a fractional number.
