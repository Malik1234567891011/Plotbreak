# SEVEN YEARS EARLY

**Slate:** #28, drop 10. Business regression revenge. Audience lean: male.
**Genre:** revenge thriller, regression, startup drama, family, slow-burn romance (optional)
**One-line fantasy:** Your partner stole your company and let you die. You wake up seven years early, on the morning you met him, with his hand held out and the same contract on the table.

## Why this fantasy works

Regression revenge gives the player the one power everybody wants in a betrayal story: they already know. They know which clause he will use, which day he files, which investor writes the cheque and which night he closes the door. The fantasy is not strength. It is being one step ahead of somebody who has always been one step ahead of everybody.

The structural bet is Good Morning, Husband's: an ordinary week that is a complete game on its own, a person who is not a prize, a secret with a deadline, and an optional mystery that never forces itself on a player who does not pull on it. Thirty turns of building the app with your sister and getting your heart checked, without ever speaking to Victor again, is a legitimate way to play and should be good.

What keeps it from being a power fantasy with no friction:

- **Every change blurs the future.** The player's memory is exact until they change something. Then people stay who they are, but events drift, change shape or stop happening. Using what you know spends what you know.
- **He must not see it coming.** Victor reads faces for a living. The more the player shows, the earlier he moves.
- **Revenge is optional.** Building it clean, taking it from inside, putting him in front of a detective, or walking away entirely are all whole games.

## Story bible

**Westharbor** is a rainy port city with a startup district along an old canal. Everybody in the scene drinks at the same three cafés on Canal Street and knows everybody's business by morning.

The player wrote **Parley**: a translation engine that lets two people on a phone call speak different languages and each hear the other in their own, less than half a second behind. The only complete copy is on their old laptop with the cracked hinge. There is no backup.

**The first life, which only the player remembers:**

- **Monday, 10:40, the Blue Hour Café.** They met Victor Lane and signed his co-founder contract without reading clause fourteen. At 11:30 the investor Naomi Ito waited forty minutes for a meeting they had booked and never came to. That night they emailed Victor the code: "all yours, partner".
- **Wednesday, 9:00.** Victor filed the patent on Parley with himself as the inventor, through the patent attorney Wendell Osei.
- **Friday, 19:00, Demo Night at Founders' Hall.** Victor pitched Parley as his. Arden Vos of Vos Calder Ventures offered two million for a fifth of the company that night.
- **Years one to five.** Parley went into every phone. Victor went onto every magazine cover. Sol, the player's sister, joined as head of design.
- **Year six.** Victor showed the board forged expense records and removed the player "for cause" under clause fourteen, buying their shares back at a dollar each. Sol believed the records and voted with him. The siblings did not speak again.
- **Year seven, the night before the listing.** The player went to the server room on the seventh floor of the Halcott Building to take their code back. A heart-rhythm condition nobody had ever checked for made them collapse. Victor came in, picked up their phone, looked at them for a long time, and closed the door.

Then they woke up in the café, seven years early.

Nobody knows why, including the player. Nobody else remembers. Nothing supernatural is visible in ordinary life and nobody has powers.

### Hard canon

- Everyone is an adult. Ages are fixed and listed under the cast.
- The story opens on a rainy Monday at 10:40 in the Blue Hour Café. Victor has just introduced himself, held out his hand and put the contract and an uncapped pen on the table.
- **Clause fourteen** assigns everything the player writes, now and later, to the company, and lets a board Victor controls remove a founder for cause and buy back their shares at a dollar each. Victor will cross it out if challenged, because he does not need it this week.
- **The patent race.** Whoever files a complete application first owns Parley, and contesting it takes years. Victor has booked Osei for Wednesday at nine and files with or without a signature. If the player retains Osei first, Osei must turn Victor away for a conflict, and Victor files through a slower firm on Thursday at five. A filing needs a written technical description, Osei's fee of twelve hundred, and a signature. The player has about four hundred in the bank.
- **Owen Mercer.** Three years ago Victor's first co-founder had a severe asthma attack alone in a locked office at their company's launch party. His phone and inhaler were never found; it was ruled an accident. Eleven days later Osei's office filed a correction making Victor the sole inventor on Owen's patent. Detective Celia Ruiz never believed the ruling, pushed it, and was moved to the cold-case desk for it.
- **Arden Vos.** Owen's launch party was in a Vos Calder building with the cameras off. In the first life, Vos Calder's security team wiped the Halcott Building's cameras the night the player died. The player does not know this.
- **The memo.** A fourteen-second voice memo, dated seven years from now, holds the player's own voice: "It wasn't only him. Don't sign. Call Sol." It surfaces only for a player who goes looking.
- A punch is a punch. Violence brings the police, a night in a cell and Victor's lawyers. Romance goes no further than a kiss.

## Tone and pacing

- Fast, specific, webnovel cadence. Short beats, concrete objects: the pen, the laptop hinge, the sticky note on the kettle, the brass timer on Osei's desk.
- Victor is charming, generous in public and sometimes right. He never raises his voice. He is frightening because he waits.
- Foreknowledge is concrete. When the player calls the future, the narrator pays it off with a time, a name, a number, and lets it drift once the player has changed enough.
- Ordinary life is load-bearing: noodles, rent, a clinic appointment, a sister who draws on everything.
- No gore, no lingering on the death. A cold floor and a closing door is the whole of it.
- Romance with Naomi is optional, tense and dry, at most a kiss.

## Player starting authority

The player authors their name, pronouns and what they were best at in the company (the archetype). They are twenty-six in body and thirty-three in memory. They decide everything else: whether to sign, whether to tell anybody, what revenge looks like, and whether they want any.

## The opener (first beat)

> "Victor Lane." The man across the table holds out his hand. "I think we're going to build something huge."
>
> You know that voice. The last time you heard it, you were on a cold floor, and he was closing a door.
>
> Now rain runs down a café window. Your old laptop sits open in front of you, the one with the cracked hinge. Your phone says Monday. Seven years ago.
>
> Same handshake. Same grey coat. Same contract on the table, a pen uncapped on top.
>
> Seven years ago, you signed it. Seven years later, he owned everything you built and let you die.
>
> Victor's hand stays out. His smile doesn't move.
>
> "So. Partners?"

A named person speaks in the first line. It ends on a direct question that needs an answer now. No proper noun the player must already know: Victor introduces himself.

**Suggestions** (said, done, risky):

1. "Before I sign anything, read me clause fourteen. Out loud."
2. I turn the contract over and read the last page, the one I skipped last time.
3. I shake his hand, smile, and sign it. Exactly like last time.

The risky one is the inside-job route: sign on purpose and take him apart from inside.

## Major cast

Every character is an adult. Ages are canon.

### Victor Lane, 31
**Role:** the co-founder who stole the company and let the player die, on the morning they first met.
**Want now:** the player's signature and the code by Monday night; Parley as his at Demo Night on Friday.
**Underneath:** grew up counting coins for the electric meter and decided at nineteen never to be the one who needs something. He loves the product and believes the person who built it is the least important part of it.
**Fear:** being ordinary; being found out by somebody he underestimated.
**Secrets:** he heard Owen Mercer on the floor of that locked office, took his phone from the desk, went back to the party and made a toast, and has told himself every day since that he did nothing. He has eleven thousand dollars left and a loft he cannot pay for past next month. He has already told Arden Vos he owns the technology outright.
**Voice:** warm, quick, says your name a lot, "Here's the thing." Compliments precisely. Never raises his voice.
**Look:** neat dark hair pushed back, charcoal wool coat, open-collared white shirt, a small pale scar through his left eyebrow he jokes about before anyone can ask.

### Naomi Ito, 34
**Role:** seed investor, Lowtide Capital, a one-woman fund in a boathouse. The meeting the player skipped last time.
**Want now:** one company this year worth the whole fund; to know why the player booked her and then turned up with Victor.
**Underneath:** she left engineering because a manager took credit for her work.
**Fear:** being the investor who saw it coming and said nothing, twice. Charm.
**Secrets:** four years ago Owen Mercer pitched her alone and told her, off the record, he was afraid of his co-founder. She passed and never told anyone. She wrote the player a term sheet two weeks before the meeting they never came to.
**Voice:** clipped, interrogative, numbers. "Show me." Takes her glasses off when she starts to take somebody seriously.
**Romance:** optional, dry, earned. At most a kiss.

### Sol, 23
**Role:** the player's younger sister, final year at the Westharbor College of Art, asleep on the player's sofa.
**Want now:** get the player to the clinic on Thursday at four; pay the rent without them noticing.
**Underneath:** she has held the family together since their father died and is very tired of it.
**Fear:** being the reason her sibling fails; hospitals.
**Secrets:** she is filling in a form to leave college and work full time at the noodle shop on Canal Street, dated Friday. She has the family heart-rhythm condition that killed their father at forty-one; it is treated and she is fine.
**First life:** she drew Parley's logo on a café napkin, Victor told every interviewer he drew it, and in year six she voted the player out.
**Voice:** "Okay, so..." Rambles, jokes, then one plain true sentence.

### Detective Celia Ruiz, 46
**Role:** cold-case detective who never believed Owen Mercer's death was an accident.
**Want now:** reopen the Mercer file; find out what Victor wants with the player.
**Underneath:** she is no longer sure whether she is after the truth or after being right.
**Secrets:** she still has the Mercer box out, which she should not. She has followed Victor in her own time for three years and keeps a notebook of every founder he has had coffee with. The player is the ninth name. She is at the corner table of the Blue Hour on the opening morning.
**First life:** she knocked on the player's door on Tuesday morning. They were asleep after coding all night and Sol sent her away. The player never knew.
**Voice:** flat, procedural, yes-or-no questions. "For the record."

### Wendell Osei, 61
**Role:** patent attorney above the Lucky Bowl noodle shop. The fastest on Canal Street. Booked by Victor for Wednesday at nine.
**Want now:** file cleanly and on time; find out whether the player is who the demo says they are.
**Underneath:** he wants one chance to put right the correction he filed for Victor three years ago.
**Secret:** he filed the correction that made Victor sole inventor on Owen's patent, eleven days after Owen died. In the margin he wrote "Asked twice. Client insisted."
**Voice:** formal, exact, bills by the quarter hour, turns a brass desk timer over at the start of every conversation. "Strictly speaking."

### Arden Vos, 58
**Role:** managing partner of Vos Calder Ventures, the biggest money in Westharbor.
**Want now:** a fifth of the next huge company by Friday night; to find out which of the two founders actually built it.
**Underneath:** he has been told he is the man who makes companies and needs it to stay true.
**Secrets:** his security team turned the cameras off at the Mercer party at Victor's request and he did not ask why. He tests founders by offering to cut their partner out; anybody who takes it, he never trusts again.
**Voice:** few words, valuations, ninety seconds of attention. "What's it worth to you?"

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Energy** (restorable): a body that is twenty-six again running on seven years of exhaustion.
- **His Suspicion** (descending): how much Victor has noticed. Low, he thinks you are the trusting founder he researched. High, he moves first, calls lawyers, and starts asking about your sister. Public violence raises it first.
- **Ripples** (descending, never comes back down): how far the week has moved off the rails you remember. Low, memory is exact to the minute. High, you know people, not events.

## The week (pressure, not a schedule)

- **Monday 11:30.** Naomi arrives at the Blue Hour for the meeting you skipped last time.
- **Monday 18:00.** If you asked for time, Victor texts: Vos Calder want a look on Friday.
- **Tuesday 08:00.** Sol's sticky note on the kettle: St. Brigid's, Thursday 4pm, you cancelled twice.
- **Tuesday 09:15.** Ruiz knocks on your door with two coffees.
- **Wednesday 09:00.** Victor files through Osei, unless you got there first.
- **Thursday 14:00–17:00.** A thunderstorm cuts power on Canal Street.
- **Thursday 16:00.** The clinic.
- **Thursday 17:00.** If you retained Osei, Victor files late through a slower firm.
- **Friday 17:00.** Sol's withdrawal form goes in, unless you found out.
- **Friday 19:00.** Demo Night. The third team's demo crashes on stage.
- **Saturday 10:00.** Vos's office calls whoever impressed him.
- **Saturday 21:00.** Victor's party at the loft. Vos arrives at ten.

## Five promises

1. **Finale: whether you end this week with the company, the revenge, or your life.** Seeded by a pen uncapped on a café table. Paid off when somebody signs something, or nobody does, and the door he closes seven years from now stays open.
2. **Rival: what Victor does when he notices.** Seeded by him using your name three times in one sentence and watching your face on the third. Paid off when he moves first, or never sees it coming.
3. **Mystery: Owen Mercer.** Seeded by a woman at the corner table reading the same page for twenty minutes. Paid off by a box labelled MERCER, an empty inhaler holder, and a correction filed eleven days later.
4. **Relationship: the sister you stopped speaking to.** Seeded by a sticky note about Thursday at four. Paid off by Sol at a board table seven years from now, or not at one.
5. **Theme: fourteen seconds you have not recorded yet.** Seeded by a phone that feels heavier than it should. Paid off by your own voice, out of breath: it wasn't only him.
