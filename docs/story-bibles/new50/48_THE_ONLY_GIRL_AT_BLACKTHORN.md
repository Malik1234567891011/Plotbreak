# THE ONLY GIRL AT BLACKTHORN

**Slate #48.** Reverse-harem academy, female lean. OOC evidence: Was School Easy 904.6K (female #1);
Only Male Student at the Witch Academy 1.5M, flipped.
**Genre:** academy romance, rivals and roommates, comedy, a quiet mystery underneath
**One-line fantasy:** You are the first woman ever to pass the entrance trial of the kingdom's knight
academy, and you passed it first. Your four roommates vote on whether to keep you in seven days, and
last night none of them said yes.

## Why this fantasy works

It gives the player a clear and fair win condition on turn one: three votes out of four, seven days.
Everything the player does in the tower, the yard, the hall and the stables moves one of four specific
men toward yes or no, so ten turns of ordinary academy life (a stair run, a bout, a shared supper, a
library at midnight) all count.

It flips the most-played OOC shape, the lone outsider in a single-sex academy, so the cast is four
distinct romance options in one room, each with his own reason to say no that has nothing to do with
the player's worth. The obstacle is a custom, and custom is made of people, so the player can beat it
with a sword, a law book, a loaf of bread, a long walk on the sea wall, or by changing the rule.

Losing is a real ending and it is not a punishment. A lance that does not take you is the custom
working as designed, and the story should be willing to deliver that.

## Story bible

**Blackthorn** is the Crown's knight academy: a four-hundred-year-old fortress-college on a headland
above a cold grey sea, in the kingdom of **Corveth**. Three years long, sixty cadets an intake, and by
its own Charter nobody under eighteen may sit its entrance trial. It has never admitted a woman.

**The Trial** is judged blind. Every candidate fights, rides and sits a written paper in a closed helm
and a numbered tabard, and the masters score the number. The rule dates from a scandal eighty years
ago, when places were sold to noble sons. The player wore **41**, the last number issued, and ranked
first overall.

When she took the helm off in the chapel to swear the First Seat's oath, the chapel went silent.
**Lord Provost Anselm Greaves** tried to void the result. The Crown's answer came back by rider:
the **Charter** says "any subject of the Crown, of eighteen years and sound body, who passes the Trial
shall be admitted, ranked and housed", and it does not say man. So she is in.

**The First Lance.** The top five of every intake share the **Rook**, the room at the top of the north
tower, built for five. By custom (not Charter) a lance "takes" its own: on the seventh morning, in the
chapel, in front of the whole intake, each of the other four stands and says yes or no out loud. Three
yes votes are needed. A cadet nobody takes is **lanceless**: sleeps in the common barracks, cannot ride
in the **Founding Tourney** on the tenth day, and is sent home at the end of term "with thanks". It has
happened twelve times in four hundred years, always quietly.

**Last night**, before the player arrived, the four held a practice vote. Nobody said yes. Rowan said
no. Aren and Felix abstained. Gideon was asleep.

**This morning** porters carried the fifth bed out of the Rook and left it in the stable yard. The
Senior Cadet **Leopold Marr** gave the order, after the Provost remarked in passing that it was a fire
risk. Rowan saw them do it and held the door.

**The buried thing.** Thirty-one years ago a woman passed the Trial in a helm. She ranked ninth and wore
seventeen. Her lance did not take her on the seventh day, under pressure from the Provost of the day,
and her name was cut out of the register by the then Master of the Rolls, Anselm Greaves. The rolls say
only: *Seventeen, sent home with thanks.* She was **Margery Holt**. She married the village armorer, and
when he died she took over the forge inside the walls that threw her out. She is **Margery Dunmore**,
the armorer, and her unmarked cadet sword hangs on the armory's back wall. **Sir Matthias Crane**, now
Master-at-Arms, was in her lance and stood up and said no. Greaves kept the page.

Nobody alive says any of this aloud. The player does not need to find it. If she does, it changes what
the Taking is.

**No magic.** Steel, horses, law and people.

## Tone and pacing

- Comedy first, tension second, romance grown from both. Four men and one woman in a tower room is
  funny because of boots, snoring, the window that never shuts and whose turn it is to fetch water.
- **No fan service.** No undressing, no bath or changing-room scenes, no lingering on anybody's figure.
  The canvas partition is about dignity. Romance is flirtatious, tense and earned, and goes no further
  than a kiss.
- **Everyone is an adult** and is described by build and costume, never with a word that reads younger.
- The sword is a language. Bouts are legible: distance, footwork, who is tired, what Crane writes in
  his ledger. Bruises and a split lip at worst. Steel drawn in anger outside the yard is a scandal.
- Sexism is the obstacle, written as people doing things rather than speeches: a bed moved, a number
  scraped off the board, a girth cut, tea offered. Allies are not saints; enemies are not stupid.
- Every one of the four can say no, change his mind, or be told no.

## Player starting authority

The player authors her name, what she looks like, why she sat the Trial, who taught her to fight (the
archetype), and everything she does. She is a woman, nineteen, ranked first. Everything else is hers.

## Major cast

Ages: **the player 19, Rowan 20, Aren 20, Felix 20, Gideon 22, Marr 23, Dunmore 50, Crane 54, Greaves 61.**

### Rowan Ashby (20) — the rival
**Role:** ranked second by four points; the fastest blade in the intake.
**Wants:** a rematch in front of everybody; the Founding Tourney and the stipend that goes with it.
**Fear:** going home second to a house that sold its furniture to send him.
**Secret:** House Ashby's last manor is mortgaged against that stipend. His mother wrote: *if the woman
ranks above you, we are finished.* He also held the door for the porters this morning.
**Voice:** clipped, sardonic, fencing words as insults, calls her "First" as a jab and later as a name.
Never says please or sorry; does both with his hands.
**Will not:** accept a thrown bout. Let him win on purpose and he never forgives it.
**Romance:** rivals to more; the sparring stays sharp after the kiss.

### Prince Aren Estravel (20) — the prince
**Role:** the Queen's second son, ranked third; the only one who defends her right to be there, and
never her.
**Wants:** to vote as himself and not as the Queen's son.
**Fear:** that his place was arranged too.
**Secrets:** the letter he burned on her first night said *Keep her in. The Crown needs this to work.
M.* His mother had the Trial notice read in the player's town. And the masters rescored his written
paper upward after the names were read; without it he would have ranked eighth.
**Voice:** formal complete sentences, precedent, understatement so dry it takes a second. "Noted." as a
full answer. No contractions when angry.
**Romance:** a man who has never been allowed to want anything, choosing to; asks permission in a full
sentence.

### Gideon Marsh (22) — the giant
**Role:** a smith's son from the northern hills, ranked fourth, the biggest man in the intake.
**Wants:** to keep his horse, and to vote the way he would have if he'd been awake.
**Fear:** going back to the forge as the one who failed.
**Secret:** his horse, armour and fees are paid by Lord Marshal Hargrave of the Old Houses. On the second
evening the Marshal's steward tells him the purse depends on his vote.
**Voice:** very short sentences, plain words, forge talk (heat, temper, true). Says your name when he
means it. "Other end."
**Romance:** gentle and certain; a man who asks before he helps, until one day he doesn't need to.

### Felix Lorne (20) — the scholar
**Role:** a clerk's son on a scholar's stipend, ranked fifth: the best written paper in forty years and
the worst sword.
**Wants:** to finish his history of Blackthorn; to be useful in the yard once, in front of Rowan.
**Fear:** that the rolls have been lying and he's the only one who has noticed.
**Secret:** he has found the gap: line seventeen of the intake thirty-one years back has no name, in
Greaves's hand, and the volume was rebound that year.
**Voice:** fast, numbered, digressive; cites the Charter by article and line; "Sorry. Short version:"
followed by a long version.
**Romance:** flustered and brave at the worst moment; confesses mid-argument, then cites his source.

### Margery Dunmore (50) — the armorer
**Role:** Mistress of the Armory for twenty years; the only other woman inside the walls.
**Wants:** the player through the Taking without having to say a word; Crane to say it out loud once.
**Fear:** watching it happen the same way twice.
**Secret:** she is Cadet Seventeen.
**Voice:** workshop terse; measurements and instructions; calls everyone "cadet", the prince included.
**Will not:** tell her story to someone asking out of curiosity; fight the player's battles.

### Sir Matthias Crane (54) — the Master-at-Arms
**Role:** runs the yard; voted to void her result; scored her blind bout the highest in twenty years.
**Wants:** the intake through first term with nobody carried off the sand.
**Fear:** that he is the same man he was at twenty-three.
**Secret:** he was in Seventeen's lance and said no.
**Voice:** barked, counted, "Again." Calls her cadet, exactly as he says it to Rowan.
**Will not:** favour her in the yard for any reason, including guilt.

### Lord Provost Anselm Greaves (61) — the Provost
**Role:** head of Blackthorn, former Master of the Rolls.
**Wants:** her lanceless on the seventh day and gone by the end of term, quietly, with thanks.
**Believes:** sincerely, that Blackthorn survives by changing nothing.
**Secrets:** he cut Seventeen's page and kept it in his locked cabinet; he mentioned the "fire risk" to
Marr.
**Voice:** long courteous sentences, "of course", "naturally"; regrets things instead of threatening
them, and the regret is the threat. Never raises his voice.

### Leopold Marr (23) — the senior
**Role:** Captain of the Cadet Watch, third year, the Provost's favourite pair of hands.
**Wants:** the Provost's goodwill, because he is failing his finals and needs a commission.
**Secret:** he ordered the bed moved, and he knows which two seniors went into the tack room.
**Voice:** hearty, "lads", nicknames, jokes with a knife folded inside; "tradition" as a whole argument.
**Will not:** cross the Provost, or fight anyone fairly if he can arrange not to.

Offstage: **Queen Merewen** (the Crown), **Lord Marshal Hargrave** (the Old Houses).

## Behavioral state (invisible)

- **Stamina** (restorable): the stair, the yard, the sea wall at night. Resting restores it.
- **Scrutiny** (descending is good): how hard the masters are looking for an excuse. Public violence and
  citing the Charter at a master raise it.
- **Rumour** (descending is good): what the hall is saying. Showing off and answering back raise it.

## Five promises

1. **The Taking** (finale) — seeded by a clean square of floor; paid off when four men stand up in the
   chapel one at a time and say it out loud.
2. **Second by four points** (rival) — seeded by a sword across Rowan's knees and the word "wrong";
   paid off by a rematch in front of the intake and what he does with the result.
3. **The number with no name** (mystery) — seeded by the unmarked sword; paid off by a page cut out of
   the rolls and a woman who has worked inside these walls ever since.
4. **What the Queen wrote** (relationship) — seeded by black flakes of a royal seal in the hearth; paid
   off by four words in his mother's hand and whether he obeys them.
5. **What a yes costs** (theme) — seeded by Gideon checking his horse's shoes twice a night; paid off
   by a lord's purse and a vote that costs him the horse, unless someone finds another way.

## Ending space (twelve)

- **Taken** (common) — three or four yes votes; relief more than triumph.
- **Sent Home With Thanks** (common) — nobody took you; never written as your failure.
- **The Road Down** (uncommon) — you walked out first, on your own terms.
- **Struck From the Rolls** (uncommon) — you struck a master; the Provost got his excuse.
- **First Seat** (rare) — taken, and the lance won the Tourney with you at its head.
- **Lanceless** (rare) — nobody took you and you rode anyway, alone.
- **Second to You** (rare) — Rowan.
- **Nothing Arranged** (rare) — Aren.
- **The Other End** (rare) — Gideon.
- **A Footnote** (rare) — Felix.
- **The Charter Stands** (unique) — you read the Charter back to the masters and the Taking fell.
- **Seventeen** (unique) — a name goes back into the rolls after thirty-one years.
