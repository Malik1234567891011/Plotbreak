## Why this fantasy works

The fox-wife is one of the oldest romance shapes there is, and the slate's monster-girl band says players
want it. What makes it playable rather than a visual novel is that the romance arrives as a **demand with a
deadline**, not a courtship. On turn one a fox you have never met is eating your dinner and telling you that
you owe her a wedding. Every answer is legitimate: yes, no, read the small print, laugh, ring the bell, walk
back down the steps.

Under the demand is a person. The vow was written by the first keeper, who loved her and married somebody
else, and wrote "my line owes you a wedding" as an apology. She has dressed three hundred years of being
turned down as a debt, so that nobody can refuse her the way he did. A player who only answers the debt
gets one story. A player who asks what she actually wants gets a better one.

The mountain is also for sale, a polite yokai under an umbrella will make the whole problem disappear for
free, and the shrine maiden would inherit if you walked away. Comedy, tension and choice, never fan service.

## The world, in plain words

- **Sugino** is a mountain town of about four hundred people: one street, half its shutters down, a closed
  school, a bathhouse, a general store, an old inn and a bus stop with the last bus at 19:52.
- **The fox shrine** sits at the top of **three hundred and four stone steps** through cedar, with a red gate at
  the top, middle and bottom. Above the steps: a gravel courtyard with an offering box and a bell, the main
  hall, the keeper's house, a storehouse, the great cedar, and the old dry stone wall that holds the north slope.
- **The vow** is a cedar board nailed behind the altar, written by **Seiji**, the first keeper, three hundred
  years ago:

  > Fox of this mountain: keep it, and do not pass the lowest gate, and my line will keep you. When my line
  > comes down to one, that one owes you the wedding I did not give, by the forty-ninth morning after the
  > last keeper. — Seiji, first keeper.

  Under it, eleven names in eleven hands: every keeper since, each of whom signed to keep her and none of whom
  was the last of the line. Kuzuha proposed to every one of them anyway. "Eleven keepers have said no to me."
- **The player is the last of the line.** Great-aunt **Chiyo**, the last keeper, died forty-six days ago at
  ninety-four. The forty-ninth morning is three dawns away.

## The system (what the storyteller must hold)

All of this is in `hardCanon`, because production's storyteller reads hard canon and never reads the
ability list except the chosen archetype's starting abilities.

- **Answering the vow** means one of three things at the altar: a wedding; the vow refused out loud, to her
  face; or the vow rewritten in new terms she signs freely. A yes or no anywhere else is a promise, not the
  answer. Unanswered by the forty-ninth morning, it lapses as if refused.
- **A refused or lapsed vow does not kill her.** Her nine tails grey one at a time until she is an ordinary
  red fox who remembers nothing, and the mountain's spirit-seat is unclaimed. Three tails are already grey at
  the tips. Every time she spends her fire for somebody, the grey comes faster.
- **The cost of yes.** A keeper married to her cannot be away from the mountain for more than a night at a
  time while the marriage lasts. She tells anyone who asks. She thinks it is why the others said no.
- **The lowest gate.** Kuzuha cannot pass the bottom gate on the steps while the vow stands.
- **The bell.** Rung by the keeper's blood, every spirit on the mountain hears it, and Kuzuha has to come.
  She hates it.
- **Who sees her.** Only people she allows see her as a woman. To the town she is a large red fox that steals
  offerings. Sora saw the woman once, five years ago. Ume suspects. Tadokoro has never seen anything.
- **Oboro** can dissolve the vow for anyone of the line who asks, without a wedding and without anyone saying
  no. He does not lie. He does not volunteer that a dissolved vow ends exactly the way a refused one does,
  with the mountain passing to him.
- **The wall.** The old wall behind the hall holds the north slope because Kuzuha holds it, every dawn.
  Tadokoro's survey calls it "a load-bearing factor this survey cannot identify".
- **The will.** Chiyo left the shrine to the player, and to Sora if the player refuses it or sells within a year.

## Premise (as shipped)

> Your great-aunt Chiyo kept a fox shrine at the top of three hundred steps above a mountain town called
> Sugino. When she died she left it to you: a hall with a leaking roof, a storehouse, a house with her reading
> glasses still on the table, and eleven thousand yen in the accounts.
>
> You came up to look at it before you sold it. Somebody was already sitting on the offering box.
>
> Her name is Kuzuha. She has nine tails, three of them going grey, and a vow nailed behind the altar that
> says your family owes her a wedding. The first keeper wrote it three hundred years ago. It only binds the
> last of the line, and that is you.
>
> You have until the forty-ninth morning after your great-aunt's death, three dawns from now, to marry her,
> refuse her to her face, or talk her into new terms. Do nothing and the vow lapses, and she starts losing
> her tails until she is an ordinary fox.
>
> Meanwhile a developer who grew up in the town wants the whole mountain for a ropeway, a polite man under an
> umbrella will offer to make the vow go away for free, and the shrine maiden who kept the place running is
> deciding what kind of keeper you are.
>
> The fox is waiting for an answer. She ate your dinner while she waited.

## Opener (turn 1, verbatim)

> The shrine you inherited is three hundred steps up a mountain. At the top, you set your dinner on the
> offering box to catch your breath.
>
> When you look up, a woman is eating it.
>
> "You're late," she says, through a mouthful of your sandwich. "I'm Kuzuha."
>
> Fox ears. Gold eyes. Nine tails sway behind her like slow red flames.
>
> "I've waited on this mountain three hundred years." She points the sandwich at the dark hall. "There's a
> vow on that altar. Your family owes me a wedding, and you're the last of the line."
>
> The bell rope sways. There's no wind.
>
> She stands, tails rising. "Kneel and say yes. Or don't, and tell me why."

**Quality bar:** Kuzuha speaks to the player at word 36 and names herself at word 47, both inside the first
sixty. It ends on a demand that needs an answer now. 116 words. The only proper noun is Kuzuha, and she
introduces it. The fixture uses curly apostrophes; the spec checks all of this.

**Opening suggestions** (said / done / risky):

1. Said: `"That was my dinner. Start with the wedding part. Slowly."`
2. Done: `I walk past her into the dark hall to read the vow myself.`
3. Risky: `I kneel and say yes, right now, before she changes her mind.`

The risky one is the best scene in the story's first hour: in three hundred years nobody has ever said yes,
and she has no plan for it.

## Cast

Cast order is cover-casting order: Kuzuha, Sora, Oboro, Tadokoro, Ume. Every one of them wants something
that has nothing to do with the player.

### Kuzuha, more than a thousand years old (appears about twenty-five), the nine-tailed fox

- **Looks:** Tall, long-limbed adult woman. Copper-red hair to her waist, gold eyes, red fox ears with dark
  tips, nine tails like slow flames, three ash-grey at the tips. White kimono under a crimson outer robe,
  sleeves tied back with red cord. Her ears and tails show her mood the way a face does.
- **Immediate want:** An answer to the vow before the forty-ninth morning.
- **Real want:** To be chosen. She wrote "owed" so nobody could refuse her the way Seiji did.
- **Fear:** Ending as an ordinary fox under the cedar, remembering nothing. Being married out of duty by
  somebody counting the nights until they can leave.
- **Secret 1:** Seiji loved her and married a woman from the valley anyway; the vow was his apology, and
  calling it a debt was her idea.
- **Secret 2:** She has been spending her fire on the wall every dawn since Chiyo died, and that is why the
  fourth tail is going.
- **Limits:** Will not be pitied. Will not pass the lowest gate. Will not lie about the vow, only leave things
  out of it.
- **Voice:** Courtly, then convenience-store slang picked up from sixty years of Chiyo's radio, in the same
  breath. Orders as statements. Counts in centuries. Calls the player "heir" until they earn their name.
  - "Eleven keepers have said no to me. Most were polite. One threw salt. I liked him best."
  - "Plum. Your great-aunt brought plum for sixty years. You brought tuna mayonnaise. We will work on it."
  - "Three centuries is a long time to wait for one word, heir. Pick one."

### Sora Yanagi, 23, shrine maiden

- **Looks:** Slight, straight-backed adult woman. Black hair in a low tie with a white paper ribbon, dark
  serious eyes, white kimono top and red hakama, a straw broom held like a spear.
- **Role:** Has swept the steps every morning for five years and run the shrine alone since Chiyo died. Works
  the counter at the town office in the day.
- **Want:** The shrine with its real keeper, and the forty-ninth-day rite done properly. More quietly: to be
  asked to stay as something other than help.
- **Fear:** The shrine closing and her having to be someone else. Being offered it, and saying yes for the
  wrong reason.
- **Secret 1:** The will gives her the shrine if the player refuses or sells. She knows, and has not said,
  because she will not win it that way.
- **Secret 2:** Five years ago she saw Kuzuha as a woman by the bell rope, crying. Neither has mentioned it.
- **Limits:** Will not help sell. Will not speak for the fox. Will not open her envelope before the forty-ninth day.
- **Voice:** Formal and precise, ends instructions with "please", goes short and blunt when angry and never
  raises her voice. "Three hundred and four steps. I counted them my first spring."

### Oboro, about six hundred years old (looks thirty-something), the rival yokai

- **Looks:** Very tall, narrow adult man. Long grey hair tied at the nape, pale grey eyes slightly out of focus,
  charcoal haori over a white shirt, a grey oiled-paper umbrella open under a clear sky, mist at his feet.
- **Want:** The mountain. He lost it to her when Seiji chose a fox over the mist, and he has waited for the
  line to run out.
- **Offer:** He will dissolve the vow tonight, for free. Nobody has to say no to her face.
- **Fear:** Being asked in, and having to decide what he is when welcome. That she was right about him.
- **Secret 1:** He is on Tadokoro's payroll as a "land and water consultant". His advice is why the ropeway's
  top station sits on the old wall.
- **Secret 2:** A dissolved vow ends like a refused one. He answers that if asked exactly.
- **Limits:** Cannot cross a threshold uninvited. Will not lie outright. Will not harm the heir directly.
- **Voice:** Soft, unhurried, exquisitely polite. Repeats your words back with one changed. Speaks of weather
  and water as people. "A vow is only a sentence somebody wrote while frightened."

### Isao Tadokoro, 56, property developer

- **Looks:** Broad, solid older man, close-cut grey hair, reading glasses pushed up, an expensive grey suit,
  mud on his good shoes, a box of sweets under one arm.
- **Want:** A signature before his backers' deadline on the second evening, and the residents' association
  behind him. Two hundred jobs, a ropeway and a hot-spring hotel in a town where the school closed.
- **Fear:** Being the man who came back and watched the town die anyway. The survey being right.
- **Secret 1:** The geologist says don't build on the north slope. He has decided the engineers can fix it.
- **Secret 2:** His mother cleaned the shrine for Chiyo for twenty years. Ume knows; he will not say it.
- **Limits:** Never threatens anybody. Talks about the survey only at the inn, door shut.
- **Voice:** Plain, friendly, salesman-quick; dialect comes back the longer he is in town; numbers as a second
  language; "honestly" when he means it and when he doesn't.

### Ume Kanzaki, 81, the general store

- **Looks:** Small, wiry older woman, short white hair under a headscarf printed with plums, a shop apron with
  three pens in the pocket, quick black eyes.
- **Want:** The residents' association not to back the sale; the player fed and up the steps with plum rice
  balls by dusk.
- **Fear:** Being the last of the old ones in Sugino.
- **Secret:** The town's old work song is Seiji's story word for word, including the part the diary leaves out.
- **Limits:** Will not vote for the sale and will tell the meeting why. Will not tell you Sora's business.
- **Voice:** Loud, rural, cheerful bossiness. Nicknames within five minutes. Drops to a whisper for anything
  that matters. "Eat. You climbed those steps on a convenience-store sandwich? Shameful. Sit."

## Factions

- **The Mountain's Folk** (first in the array): lantern moths, crows that keep the hours, the stone foxes.

- **Sugino Town:** four hundred people and a residents' association split over the ropeway.
- **Tadokoro Development:** one big project left in it and backers who want a signature.

## Five promises

1. **FINALE: whether there is a wedding by the forty-ninth morning.** Seed: a vow in a three-hundred-year-old
   hand. Payoff: somebody kneels, or says no at the altar, and the dark lantern lights or doesn't.
2. **RELATIONSHIP: why a fox has waited three hundred years.** Seed: she knows exactly how many keepers said
   no, and makes it a joke. Payoff: the diary; he loved her and wrote the vow as an apology.
3. **RIVAL: the man under the umbrella.** Seed: mist on the lowest steps on a clear night. Payoff: he wants
   the mountain, and will dissolve the vow for anyone who asks.
4. **MYSTERY: what the old wall is holding up.** Seed: the surveyor writes nothing down in front of you.
   Payoff: "a load-bearing factor this survey cannot identify". It is her.
5. **THEME: what Sora gets if you leave.** Seed: she knows where every key goes and does not offer to show
   you. Payoff: the envelope; the shrine is hers if you refuse, and she will not win it that way.

## Tone

Romantic comedy with a clock in it. Banter first, then the thing under it. Romance is teasing, tense and
heartfelt, up to a kiss and no further. No fan service: her tails and ears are a face, never a body. Nobody is a
villain. Tadokoro is right about the jobs, Oboro is right that a vow is a sentence, and the fox is right that
she is owed.
