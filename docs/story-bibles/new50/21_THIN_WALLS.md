# THIN WALLS

**Slate:** #21 · rivals to lovers · forced proximity · audience lean: both
**Genre:** romantic comedy, campus slice of life, light legal drama
**One-line fantasy:** You have finished second to the same woman for three years. On Sunday she moved into the flat next door, and the wall between your desks is one sheet of plasterboard.
**OOC evidence:** "Ex Moved in Next Door" and "She Thinks I Am in Love With Her" (the UGC proximity wave); Sharing an apartment with the popular girl (4.7M, #2 daily); our own Good Morning, Husband (64% take a turn), whose structure this copies.
**Title collision search (2026-11-22):** no manga, webtoon, anime or light novel called "Thin Walls" found. Nearest are "walls" (a WEBTOON Canvas series) and "I Want to Be a Wall" (light novel); neither shares a premise.

## Why this fantasy works

Rivals to lovers usually needs forty chapters to get two people alone. A thin wall does it on the first page, and it does it *symmetrically*: she can hear you exactly as well as you can hear her. Every night the wall hands each of them something the other did not mean to share, such as a rehearsed argument, a phone call home, a laugh at something stupid on a laptop, or crying at three in the morning. Each of them decides, every night, whether to use it.

That gives the romance a concrete moral engine instead of a meter. Using what you heard wins you the moot and costs you her. Telling the truth about the wall protects both of you and risks both of you. Admitting you want something besides first place is the hardest thing either of them can do, and it can be done through plasterboard before it is ever done face to face.

The academic stakes are real and finite: one prize, two people, nine days. The rivalry has a scoreboard (the rank list) and a final (the moot), so a player who never once flirts still has a complete game: win, lose, or get both of you disqualified.

## Story bible

The player is a final-year law student at **Aldmoor University**, ranked second in a year of 140. They have been second for three years, behind **Nadia Cho**, by less than half a mark every time. The current rank list reads 78.6, 78.2, 77.9.

In nine days, on Friday week at ten, the top two argue the **Final Moot** in the **Old Court** before a bench of three chaired by **Professor Ingrid Lund**. The winner is offered the **Arden Clerkship**: one paid year as clerk to an appeal judge, the job everyone in the year wants and only one person gets.

The moot problem is **Penrose v. Ashdown Lettings**, a nuisance case about a partition wall so thin it made a rented flat unlivable. Lund wrote it, and she has a sense of humour. Nadia argues for the tenant. The player argues for the landlord.

The moot has one hard rule, the **conferral rule**: counsel on opposite sides may not discuss the problem with each other before the moot. A proven breach disqualifies both of them, and the clerkship goes to the third-ranked student, **Elliot Ashby**.

The player lives in **Flat 3B, 14 Larkin Street**, a converted terrace across the road from a 24-hour café, with their flatmate **Sam Ferreira**. Forty years ago 3B's back bedroom and 3C's were one room; somebody split it with a single sheet of plasterboard. The player's desk is against it. On Sunday, Nadia moved into 3C, and her desk is against the other side.

She has been listening for three nights. Tonight, at 2:07, she knocks.

### What the wall carries

Voices at normal volume, word for word. A pen clicking. A kettle. Music. Laughter at a laptop. An alarm at 5:40. A phone call home in the careful voice people use for their parents. Crying, once, late. It never carries anything intimate, and nobody in this story listens in on anything a 13+ audience should not hear.

It is symmetrical. She heard the player's closing argument four times before she knocked. The player has not yet heard hers, because she rehearses in a whisper. The truce she offers protects her more than it protects the player, and a sharp player will notice.

### The truce

Nadia's three terms: nobody rehearses out loud after midnight; nobody uses anything they heard through the wall; nobody finds out they are neighbours. Coffee at seven at the café across the road, if the player agrees.

The truce is a romance device and a plot device at once. It is the only thing between both of them and the conferral rule, and it is the first time either of them has proposed anything to the other except a correction.

### Hard canon

- Everyone in this story is an adult. The player and Nadia are final-year students in their twenties.
- The player is second, by less than half a mark, for the third year running.
- The Final Moot is in nine days. One clerkship. The bench is Lund and two judges.
- The conferral rule disqualifies both finalists if proven, and the place goes to Elliot Ashby.
- The wall is one sheet of plasterboard. Each can hear the other speak at normal volume; neither can see through it.
- Nadia moved in on Sunday. Before tonight they had never spoken outside a seminar.
- Theo Cho is Nadia's older brother. He left the same faculty six years ago and runs the night shift at the café opposite.
- Lund already knows they are neighbours, from Nadia's hardship housing letter, and is waiting to see who tells her.

## Tone and pacing

- Quick, warm and competitive. Banter is the love language and both of them are good at it.
- Short beats, webnovel cadence. The face at the top of a beat is Nadia's whenever she is in the scene, including when she is only a voice through the wall.
- Romance is slow-burn and goes no further than a kiss. Tension comes from proximity, honesty and timing, never from bodies.
- Law is texture, not homework. One sharp legal line per scene beats a paragraph of doctrine.
- Nobody is a villain. Elliot is a rival who reads the rules more closely than anybody. Lund is strict and fair. Theo is kind and carrying something.
- Quiet scenes are allowed to be quiet. Two people working on opposite sides of a wall at four in the morning, saying nothing, is a scene.

## Player starting authority

The player authors their name, pronouns, background (archetype), why they want the clerkship, and how they feel about Nadia at the start: rival, grudge, quiet crush, or nothing at all. Any of those can change.

### Minimal hard canon for the player
- final-year law student, second in the year;
- lives in 3B with Sam;
- argues for the landlord in the moot;
- has never had a real conversation with Nadia before tonight.

## Opening scene (turn 1)

2:07 a.m. The player is reading their closing argument aloud for the fourth time. Three knocks on the wall by the desk. Nadia introduces herself through the plaster ("Also first place"), tells them their second point is good and that this is a problem for her, says she left a note under their front door at midnight, sets out the three terms, and asks: truce, or does she knock every time they say "respectfully"?

The player can accept, counter-offer, refuse, lie that they never heard a word, point out she has heard their whole case while they have heard none of hers, go round and knock on her actual door, put an ear to the wall, wake Sam, or ignore her. She reacts to the exact thing, through the wall.

### The opener as shipped

> 2:07 a.m. You're reading your closing argument aloud for the fourth time when somebody knocks on the wall beside your desk.
>
> Three knocks. From the other side.
>
> "Hi. It's Nadia. Next door. Also first place." Her voice comes through the plaster like she's sitting beside you. "Your second point is good. I've heard it four times. Now I think it's good too, which is a problem for me."
>
> A chair creaks.
>
> "There's a note under your front door. I wrote it at midnight, when I was nicer. Short version: nobody rehearses aloud after midnight, nobody uses what they hear through this wall, and nobody finds out we're neighbours."
>
> "So. Truce? Or do I knock every time you say 'respectfully'?"

119 words. Nadia speaks from word 28; the last line is a direct question. That is one word of margin under the 120-word cap: any later edit to the opener must cut a word for every word it adds.

Three suggestions, three different moves:
- **Said:** "Deal. But I want it in writing, and you're buying the coffee."
- **Done:** I go out onto the landing in my socks and knock on her actual door.
- **Risky:** "No deal. You've heard my whole argument, and I haven't heard a word of yours."

## Major cast

### Nadia Cho (22)
**Role:** first place, three years running; the voice on the other side of the wall.
**Appearance:** sharp jaw-length black hair, dark eyes, reading glasses pushed up into her hair and forgotten; grey university hoodie at 2 a.m., pressed black suit jacket in daylight. A red pen behind one ear, uncapped, which has ruined three collars.
**Want:** to win the Final Moot without using one word she heard through the wall.
**Need:** to lose one thing and find out she survives it.
**Fear:** that she is only first because she sleeps less than everybody else; turning into her brother, or into the parents who never forgave him.
**Secrets:** she saw the player's name on the post box when she viewed 3C and signed the lease that afternoon ("it was the rent"); a page at the back of her case notebook titled "Through the wall", none of it about the moot; a large firm has offered her a training contract her parents want her to take, answer due the day after the moot, and she will take it if she loses.
**Voice:** exact, quick, dry. Numbers her points ("Three terms."), keeps count of your concessions out loud, drops the courtroom register mid-sentence when something lands and picks it straight back up. Much braver through a wall than through a door.
**Will not:** be let win; talk about her parents through the wall (that needs an open door).

### Sam Ferreira (22)
**Role:** the player's flatmate in 3B; final-year sound engineering student.
**Appearance:** tall, loose-limbed, curly brown hair, big studio headphones worn round his neck at all times, cardigan with a burn on the sleeve, socks that never match.
**Want:** finish his final project, *A Building at Night*, by his listening party on the roof; get the two of them to admit it before he moves out.
**Secrets:** he has taken a job at a theatre in another city starting in five weeks and has not told the player (the lease renewal is unsigned under a takeaway menu); his project includes forty minutes recorded through the party wall of both of them rehearsing at each other, which he thinks is romantic and which is evidence of conferral.
**Voice:** loose, running, full of sound words (room tone, levels, hiss). Narrates the player's life back to them like a nature documentary. Goes short and plain the second something is actually wrong.
**Will not:** delete a recording without a very good argument; lie to Nadia for the player.

### Theo Cho (27)
**Role:** Nadia's older brother; night manager at the Lamplight, the 24-hour café across the road.
**Appearance:** broad and easy, black hair tied back, apron over a faded band shirt, flour on his forearms, a gentler version of his sister's face. An order pad with every regular's usual on the inside cover.
**Want:** get Nadia through the next nine days still sleeping; find out whether the player is good for her before she does.
**Secrets:** six years ago, before his own final moot, he was accused of conferral; Lund signed the complaint on another student's word; he left rather than answer it and has never told anyone, including Nadia, that he did not do it. He also told Nadia 3C was free, knowing whose flat was next to it.
**Voice:** slow, warm, short. Says prices and times. Answers a serious question with a food order, then the real answer a beat later while wiping something down.
**Will not:** talk about the faculty inside the faculty; take sides between the player and his sister out loud.

### Professor Ingrid Lund (61)
**Role:** Chair of Advocacy. Wrote the moot problem, chairs the bench, decides the clerkship. Lives on a narrowboat moored on the canal behind Larkin Street.
**Appearance:** short severe silver hair, rimless glasses, the same navy suit for a decade, a gold fountain pen she never caps and taps once on the desk when she has heard enough.
**Want:** one great moot before she retires, from the two people who could give it to her; a second funded clerkship out of the faculty board.
**Secrets:** she signed Nadia's hardship housing letter and has known since Monday that the finalists share a wall; she has asked the board for a second clerkship and will hear the day before the moot; six years ago she signed the complaint against Theo, and the student who made it withdrew the statement by letter after Theo had gone; she has never seen the withdrawal.
**Voice:** short declaratives, surname first, no filler. Asks one question and stops completely. Uses legal Latin once per conversation, correctly.
**Will not:** bend the conferral rule for anyone; discuss another student's file.

### Elliot Ashby (23)
**Role:** third in the year, three tenths of a mark behind the player; the only person who wins if either finalist is disqualified.
**Appearance:** fair hair neatly parted, open friendly face, navy cable-knit over a collared shirt, an expensive watch he never checks, a silver signet ring he turns with his thumb before the real question.
**Want:** proof of conferral before the moot; the clerkship, clean if possible.
**Secrets:** a complete draft complaint against both finalists, missing only the evidence, saved in a folder called "Not yet"; his father, a High Court judge, has told the whole family the clerkship is Elliot's.
**Voice:** easy and friendly, full of names and small details about you. Asks light questions in a row, and the dangerous one is always the third.
**Will not:** lie on a formal complaint; do anything to Nadia he would not do to the player.

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Focus** (good high): how much the player has left. Finals week. Rest restores it.
- **Exposure** (good low): how close the year is to knowing the finalists live next door and talk through the wall. Every wall conversation, door knock and public coffee raises it a little; it settles over quiet days.
- **Her Guard** (good low): how much of herself Nadia is holding back. Starts high. Living one wall apart wears it down by itself; needling, lying and using what you heard put it back up.

## Five promises

1. **Finale: who argues best on Friday week.** Seed: the rank list, 78.6 / 78.2 / 77.9. Payoff: three judges, the Old Court, and one of them on their feet last.
2. **Relationship: what she says when she thinks you cannot hear.** Seed: on the second night she says goodnight to the wall, as a joke. Payoff: the page at the back of her notebook titled "Through the wall".
3. **Rival: the third-place student who only needs one of you gone.** Seed: Elliot remembers your address. Payoff: a draft complaint with a blank where the evidence goes.
4. **Mystery: why her brother left the faculty.** Seed: Theo will not set foot in the faculty building, even to deliver coffee. Payoff: an old moot, a complaint Lund signed, and a statement withdrawn after he had gone.
5. **Theme: eleven centimetres of plasterboard.** Seed: one room split in two forty years ago; the radiator still runs under both desks. Payoff: whether the wall ends the story as a wall or as something you knock on on purpose.

## Endings

- **Second Again** (common): she wins the moot, fair and close. Whatever they are to each other survives or does not on its own terms.
- **First Place** (uncommon): the player wins with clean hands.
- **Her Argument, Your Mouth** (uncommon): the player wins with what they heard through the wall. She knows which sentence.
- **Conferral** (common): a complaint is proven, both are out, Elliot argues the moot against nobody. The two of them on the roof with nothing left to lose.
- **Two Clerks** (rare): honesty with Lund and a moot good enough to justify it; the board funds a second place.
- **Conceded** (rare): the player withdraws so she wins. She is furious.
- **Knock Twice** (rare): together, after the moot, whoever won.
- **Rivals for Life** (uncommon): no romance; the best rivalry either of them will ever have, and they mean to keep it.
- **Moved Out** (uncommon): the player leaves Larkin Street and the moot behind.
- **Second Hearing** (unique): Theo's old case is put back in front of Lund, and answered.
- **Eleven Centimetres** (unique): together, and the player never once used the wall against her.
- **Nothing Through the Wall** (common): nobody said it. The moot happens, the year ends, she moves out in June.
