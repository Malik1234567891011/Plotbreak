## Why this fantasy works

The hero-academy shelf is the biggest one OOC has. Its weakest openers explain the power system first. This one opens on the moment everyone remembers from those stories: the public test, the number, the laugh. Then it breaks the test.

The player gets the two things this genre sells in the first beat: being underestimated in public, and a hint that they are more than the number. From there the week is a rivalry with the number-one cadet, sparring with an instructor who knows something, and a public trial at the end where the player decides what to show.

Nothing about the power is fixed by the menu. The player knows almost nothing at the start. The world knows a little more and makes them work for it.

## Story bible

**Lodestar Academy** is a four-year college that trains and licenses heroes. It sits on **Gull Island** in a cold bay off the city of **Calder Bay**. Cadets come in at eighteen or older, after school. It takes 240 a year. Everyone in this story is an adult and is written as one.

On intake morning every cadet puts a palm on **the Gauge**. It is a glass column with a brass hand plate. It sends a measured pulse into the cadet and reads what their power pushes back, as a number from 0 to 10,000. The number decides everything:

- **the track:** Field (ranks 1–60), Support (61–180), Reserve (181–240);
- the dorm floor, the mess table, which agency scouts come to watch;
- where the cadet's name sits on **the Board**, the live ranking wall in the atrium.

The Gauge is owned and run by the **Ratings Bureau**, the national body that licenses heroes. The academy's funding depends on agencies and the Bureau trusting its numbers.

**The player's reading was 0.** A zero means automatic Support placement "pending review". Then the column under their hand went hot, then white, and burned to the ceiling. Nobody was hurt. The player's palm has no mark on it.

### What actually happened (author's truth, discoverable, never owed)

- The Gauge reads what a power **pushes out**. The player's power pushes nothing out on its own. When force is put into the player, the power **takes it in and gives it back later**, often all at once and not always where they aim.
- The Gauge's pulse went into the player and nothing came back, so the screen read 0. A backup dial behind the column read **below zero**, which it is not built to do, and its needle broke the stop pin. Then the drawn-in pulse came back out in one go and burned the coils.
- What the power becomes beyond that is for play to find. It is never "nothing".

### The first fire (the optional mystery)

Nineteen years ago the **previous** Gauge read zero for a cadet named **Ada Kole** and burned. The Bureau took her to its assessment floor in the capital for eighteen months. The record says she withdrew. Ines Valk was in that intake. Okon Hale, then deputy director, signed the transfer. Ada now runs the engine room of the ferry the player came in on.

A player who never pulls on this thread never meets it. It opens only through things the player chooses to do: going into the Rating Office and looking at the burned housing, telling Valk the truth and being on the seawall at night, or riding the ferry and letting the engineer see their band.

### Riku's number

Riku Amano is number one with 9,412. His sponsoring agency paid a Bureau technician to adjust the Gauge's calibration for his session. The real reading was about 7,100. The burned Gauge triggers an automatic audit of every session log, which will find the adjustment. Riku needs the fire to have been the player's fault, not the machine's.

### Trial Day

On the seventh morning of term any cadet may challenge their number in a public bout in **the Bowl**, the academy's sand arena. They name a sparring partner from the Board, anyone, and a panel of three re-scores them: Valk, Hale and Halvorsen. Agency scouts sit in the top row.

## Tone and pacing

- Anime hero-academy energy: fast, loud, funny, competitive.
- Powers are physical and legible. Say what they do in the room: the weight, the heat, the stopped ball.
- Fights are sparring, training and the occasional brawl. Injuries are bruises, scrapes and burns a medic fixes. Nobody dies. No gore.
- The institution is not evil. Hale believes the system protects people. The Bureau is careful, not cruel.
- Romance is optional, flirtatious, tense and heartfelt, and stops at a kiss. Never fan service.
- The player can lie, refuse, fight, wander off the island or ignore the week. The world keeps up.

## Player starting authority

The player authors their name, pronouns, look, and anything about their past that does not contradict the hard canon. The archetype sets how they got a place here. It does not set what the power is.

### Hard canon

- Lodestar Academy is a four-year hero licensing college on an island; every cadet is at least eighteen; the player is a nineteen-year-old first-year in an intake of 240.
- The Gauge rated the player 0, then caught fire in front of the whole intake. Nobody was hurt and the player's hand was unmarked.
- The player has a real power. It pushes nothing out on its own; when force goes into the player, it takes the force in and gives it back later. Nobody on the island knows this on intake morning, the player included; it comes out only through what happens in play.
- Nineteen years ago the previous Gauge read zero for Ada Kole and burned. The Bureau took her; the record says she withdrew. Valk and Hale were there.
- Riku Amano's number was adjusted upward by an arrangement between his sponsor and a Bureau technician; the audit of the burned Gauge will find it. Only Riku, his sponsor and that technician know; Halvorsen has found the offset and told nobody. The player learns it only by finding it out.
- Trial Day is on the seventh morning: any cadet may challenge their number in a public bout before a panel.
- Nobody on the island wants the player dead. The danger is being reclassified, taken to the Bureau's assessment floor, or quietly kept at zero.

## Cast

All seven are adults. Ages are fixed.

### Ines Valk, 41
**Role:** combat instructor for the first years. Ex-field hero.
**Power:** sets the weight of anything she is touching, from a feather to a truck. The Gauge rated her 410 because her power needs contact, which is why she distrusts it.
**Immediate want:** an answer for the fire, before the Director turns it into paperwork.
**Long-term want:** not to lose a second cadet to the Bureau.
**Fear:** that she will watch it happen again and say nothing again.
**Secrets:** she was in the hall nineteen years ago when the old Gauge burned for Ada Kole; she has ridden Ada's ferry every Sunday for eleven years and never once spoken to her about it.
**Voice:** clipped imperatives. "Cadet." "Again." Talks about what you did, never what you felt. Never repeats an order.
**Will not:** be flirted with by a cadet; lie to a cadet's face.

### Riku Amano, 20
**Role:** number one on the Board, 9,412. The rival.
**Power:** stillness. Anything moving within three metres of him slows to a stop.
**Immediate want:** the fire to be the player's fault, not the machine's.
**Long-term want:** to find out his real number and still be worth something.
**Fear:** being ordinary in public; his mother's noodle shop losing the sponsorship money.
**Secret:** his sponsor had his session recalibrated. The audit will find it.
**Voice:** grinning patter, nicknames ("Zero"), talks in rankings like a sports commentator. Drops it completely when nobody is filming.
**Will not:** hit someone who is already down; admit fear in front of the Field table.

### Sana Tsuda, 19
Called Mei in the first draft; renamed after editor review, because a Mei on a hero academy's support track is a famous name already.
**Role:** first-year medic cadet, Support track, rank 74. Rated first thing and put straight on the medic station beside the Gauge.
**Power:** feels an injury by touch and closes small ones. Each one leaves her with a borrowed ache for an hour.
**Immediate want:** to know what she saw.
**Long-term want:** the Field track as a field medic, which needs a field partner on Trial Day.
**Fear:** being kept on Support because it is useful to everyone except her.
**Secret:** she saw the backup dial read below zero and break its stop pin. She pocketed the pin.
**Voice:** dry, deadpan, medical shorthand. "Hold still." Checks the answer even after you give it.
**Will not:** treat someone who refuses treatment; lie on a medical record.

### Calla Reyes, 21
**Role:** rank 239 of 240, across the hall in the Reserve corridor, re-sitting her first year.
**Power:** repeats any sound she has heard, exactly.
**Immediate want:** to be your friend, loudly.
**Long-term want:** to stay on the island this time.
**Fear:** being sent home twice.
**Secret:** the Director's office told her this morning to keep an eye on you and report, in exchange for clearing her probation. She agreed before she met you.
**Voice:** rapid, funny, trades in rumours ("one pudding for a rumour, two for the truth"), does other people's voices back at them.
**Will not:** lie to you once she has decided you are hers; let anyone sit alone in the mess.

### Okon Hale, 58
**Role:** the Director.
**Power:** can speak into the ear of anyone on the island whose name he knows.
**Immediate want:** a signed statement that the fire was an equipment fault.
**Long-term want:** the academy's record: fourteen years, nobody lost.
**Fear:** being the man who signed it twice.
**Secrets:** he signed Ada Kole's transfer nineteen years ago; a standing Bureau order says any reading below zero must be reported within seventy-two hours, and he has not reported yours.
**Voice:** warm, measured, "we", "Let's be practical." Full names.
**Will not:** threaten a cadet outright; be shouted at in his own office; be flirted with by a cadet (one courteous sentence closes it).

### Dr. Noor Halvorsen, 34
**Role:** the Ratings Bureau's resident technician. Built the current Gauge.
**Power:** sees electrical current as colour. To her, the player's looked like a hole.
**Immediate want:** to scan the player again, properly.
**Long-term want:** to understand it before her bosses decide what it is.
**Fear:** her machine being the reason someone disappears.
**Secrets:** her preliminary report is written and unsent; she has already found the recalibration line on Riku's session.
**Voice:** fast, technical, units and figures, apologises for her own excitement.
**Will not:** scan anyone without consent; falsify a reading; entertain a cadet's flirting while their reading is on her bench.

### Ada Kole, 38
**Role:** the ferry's engineer. The cadet the old Gauge burned for.
**Power:** the same kind as the player's. She learned to hold it, and holding it costs her.
**Immediate want:** to be left alone.
**Long-term want:** for somebody to finish what she was not allowed to.
**Fear:** the white room on the Bureau's assessment floor.
**Secret:** in a storm six years ago she ran the ferry's engine with no fuel in it for forty minutes.
**Voice:** slow, few words, engine metaphors. "Breathe out first."
**Will not:** set foot on the academy side of the island; tell her story to anyone who has not asked her straight; be flirted with by a cadet.

## Five promises

1. **Your number (finale).** Seed: a single 0 on the Board in the same size type as every other name. Payoff: Trial Day in the Bowl, a panel of three, and whatever the player chooses to show them.
2. **What it does (mystery).** Seed: an unmarked palm on a machine that burned; a backup dial whose needle went the wrong way. Payoff: it takes in what is pushed into it and gives it back, and learning when is the whole of the training.
3. **Number one (rival).** Seed: Riku laughs first, every time, and watches the Bureau technician more than he watches the player. Payoff: a calibration line in a session log, and a number he has never been told.
4. **The first fire (mystery).** Seed: Valk is not surprised by the fire; she checks the player's palm like she has done it before. Payoff: Ada Kole, nineteen years on, in the engine room of the ferry.
5. **Somebody in your corner (relationship).** Seed: a medic cadet who saw the dial and will not say what it read until she trusts you. Payoff: somebody on the sand with the player on Trial Day, or in the stands, or nobody, by choice.

## Behavioral state (invisible)

- **Stamina** (goes up with rest): how much of the day is left in the player's body.
- **Scrutiny** (bad when high): how closely the people who run things are watching. A public fight raises it most.
- **Build-up** (bad when high): force the power has taken in and not given back. Taking hits raises it; it bleeds off slowly; high, things near the player get hot and crack.

## Ending space

- **A Number of Your Own** (common): a Field-track number earned on Trial Day.
- **Support Track** (common): the player takes the quiet track and makes it matter.
- **Sent Home on the Ferry** (common): the player hit staff and was expelled.
- **Rank Zero** (uncommon): the player refuses to perform and keeps the 0 on purpose.
- **The Quiet Floor** (uncommon): the Bureau's reclassification order goes through. Reachable without a mistake.
- **Off the Island** (uncommon): the player walks away.
- **One of Ours** (uncommon): Valk's cadet, on the academy's own terms.
- **Number One** (rare): the player takes the top of the Board from Riku.
- **Field Partners** (rare): the player and Sana pass Trial Day as a pair.
- **The Gauge Goes Dark** (rare): the rating system's failures go public and the Bureau switches the Gauge off.
- **Two Zeros** (unique): Ada Kole comes up to the academy side of the island for Trial Day, the first time in nineteen years.
