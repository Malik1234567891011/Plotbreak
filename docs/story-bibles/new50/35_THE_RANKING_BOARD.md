# THE RANKING BOARD

**Slate:** #35 of the 50-story plan (`docs/stories-50/PLAN.md`, section 3)
**Genre:** fight-college action (every character is an adult), rivalry, campus drama, light romance
**Audience lean:** male (OOC evidence: fight-school titles are two of the most-played stories on that shelf, at 4.6M and 3.4M plays)
**Rating:** 13+. Fights are legible and physical; injuries are bruises, split brows, taped joints. No gore. Romance goes up to a kiss.
**One-line fantasy:** A fighting college posts its top hundred on a glass wall every morning, anyone who beats a name takes its number, and on your first morning the man at number 87 throws you into the glass.

**Title collision check (2026-11-22):** a web search for "The Ranking Board" across manga, webtoon, anime and novels found no work with that title. Nearest titles are *Ranking of Kings* and the webtoon *Ranker*; neither shares the premise, a cast member or a term.

## Why this fantasy works

OOC's fight-school shelf rewards one shape: a hierarchy everybody can see, a nobody at the bottom of it, and a first fight on page one. This story makes the hierarchy literal. The board is forty feet of glass, it re-sorts at six every morning, and it fits on a sticker: touch your card to a name, beat that person in a painted circle, take their number.

That rule gives the player a ladder they can read without a tutorial, a reason for every fight, and a public cost for every refusal. It also gives the world a villain who never throws a punch: somebody has been taking names off the board without a fight, and the player's climb is the thing that plan needs.

The board is a device, not a cage. A player can climb it, refuse it, game it, expose it, ignore it and spend a week making friends in the cheap dorm, or walk down the hill and never come back. All of those are authored.

## Story bible

There is a college on a hill above a harbour city called **Garam Bay**. It is **Haneul Ridge College of Combat Arts**, called "the Ridge" by everyone who goes there. Eight hundred students, all of them eighteen or older, study one thing for four years: striking, wrestling, and everything in between. No powers, no magic, no system windows. Just people who are very good at fighting, and a scoreboard.

The **board** is a glass screen the height of a four-storey building on the north wall of **the Yard**, the stone courtyard at the centre of the college. It shows the top hundred students, by number, with a photo and a record. Every morning at six a bell rings and the board goes black and fills again from number one down. The whole college watches it like weather.

The player is an **unranked transfer** who arrived last night. They have a student card on a lanyard, a bed in the cheap dorm, a roommate who knows every record on the board by heart, and about ten minutes before their first class. On the way across the Yard, **Tetsu Hamada, number 87**, shoves them into the glass so their card touches his name. He needs a defence before tomorrow's sort, and a transfer is the safest one he can find. A card put on a name by force is off-circle violence, and Tetsu already has two strikes; he is betting a transfer will not know that, or will not use it.

The playable span is **one week**: Monday morning to **Scout Night** on Sunday evening, when the professional leagues send scouts to the Pit to watch the top twenty, and the league's flagship club signs whoever is number one that morning.

Underneath the climb there is a quiet scandal. In two years, seven fit fighters have been declared medically unfit and taken off the board within two days of booking a challenge against somebody who eats at the **Long Table**, the tenth-floor dining room where the top ten sit. All seven forms carry the same signature. **Hajun Seol, number 2**, runs the Long Table, and he wants number one before Scout Night. The number one, **Soyeon Ryu**, has held it for 412 days, nobody has lasted a minute with her in eleven months, and she is hiding a shoulder injury. If the player climbs fast enough, Hajun would like them to be the one who touches her name.

The story succeeds if a player can spend ten turns doing nothing but sparring, eating noodles with their roommate and arguing with Tetsu, and still enjoy it, and if a player who goes looking for the scandal finds a real one.

## The rules of the board (hard canon)

- The board re-sorts at 06:00 every morning. It shows the top hundred.
- **Challenge:** touch your student card to a name. The named fighter taps their card to accept. The challenger then confirms on the glass.
- **Pending:** the glass scrolls under a sliding card, so any name can be reached from the stones. Until the challenger confirms, a challenge is only pending; sliding to a different name first replaces it with no flag, and the new one is that day's challenge. Number one declines unranked challengers for free.
- **Range:** anybody may challenge anybody. A fighter may decline, for free, any challenge from more than twenty places below them. Within twenty places they get **one refusal per term**. Unranked students count as 101.
- **The fight:** inside any white circle painted on campus, with a proctor or any instructor calling it. Win by knockout, submission, the other person stepping out of the circle, or a concession (two slaps on the stones). Nobody touches anybody after the call.
- **The result:** a winner from below takes the loser's number; the loser drops one place and everybody between shifts. Number 100 falls off. One challenge per fighter per day.
- **Backing out** after the other fighter has accepted puts a white flag on your card for the rest of the term. A flagged student cannot issue a challenge. Each instructor may still put one unranked fighter, flagged or not, into a Scout Night exhibition bout.
- **Stale:** a ranked fighter who has not defended in thirty days goes grey at the morning sort. Anyone may challenge a stale name and it may not refuse.
- **Number one is not stale.** Soyeon Ryu last defended twenty-two days ago, the day before her shoulder tore, and won in forty seconds; she is not stale before Scout Night. Nobody has lasted a minute with her in eleven months.
- **Off-circle violence** belongs to the Proctors' Office. Three strikes is expulsion, and not turning up to a hearing is expulsion on its own. Tetsu has two.
- **Forced cards:** a card put on a name by force is off-circle violence. If the shoved student refuses and says so to an instructor or proctor before confirming, the challenge is void with no white flag, and the shove goes on the shover's record as a strike. For Tetsu that is his third, and expulsion. Coach Dam crosses the Yard at 08:00 every morning and will call any fight waiting in the circle.
- **Medical stand-down:** the college clinic's senior physician can declare any fighter unfit. The name greys out and drops off, and the fighter can return only by challenging back in once cleared.
- The ranked hundred live in **the Tower**, ten to a floor by number, with free meals, a physio and a stipend. Everybody else lives in **the Annex** and buys their own noodles.

## Tone and pacing

- Loud, fast, specific. Two hundred phones, red letters on glass, a bell at six, a vending machine that only takes coins.
- Fights are legible and short: distance, angle, a habit, a mistake. A style beats another style for a reason that fits in a sentence.
- Consequences are physical and ordinary: a split brow, a taped wrist, a limp up ninety-nine steps. Never gore.
- Nobody monologues about strength. Coach Dam will walk out if they do.
- Rivals are people. Tetsu is frightened, not evil. Hajun is polite, never cartoonish.
- Romance is tension, banter and choice. Up to a kiss. No fan service.
- If a scene has nothing at stake, let it be funny and short.
- Fighters who watch the streams learn the player's habits: by their third fight somebody is waiting for the thing they always do, and says so.

## Player starting authority

The player authors their own name, pronouns, how they look, and where they learned to fight (the archetype, which is the one piece of identity the storyteller reads). They decide whether to climb, refuse, cheat, investigate, fall for someone or leave. Reasonable details about their past become canon if they do not contradict the rules above. Every archetype starts with the board's two verbs, Put Your Card on a Name and Walk Out of the Circle, plus one move of its own (Walk Them Down, Take It to the Stones, Make Them Miss, Play to the Phones, Ask Around), because the storyteller reads only the chosen archetype's starting abilities.

## The opener (turn 1)

> Your chest slams into the glass, and the whole board lights up.
>
> A hundred names. A hundred faces. Your student card is pressed flat against number 87.
>
> You turn. Number 87 just shoved you. Same grin as on the screen, same 87 shaved into the side of his head.
>
> "Whoops," says Tetsu. "Your card touched my name, transfer. That's a challenge."
>
> He taps his own card to the glass. Red letters flash:
>
> CHALLENGE ACCEPTED?
>
> Across the courtyard, two hundred phones come up at once.
>
> Tetsu steps into the white circle painted on the stones and raises his fists. "Beat me, you get my number. Back out, and the board flags you a coward all term."
>
> "So. Yes or no?"

119 words. Tetsu speaks at word 50. It ends on a direct question. No proper noun the player must already know.

**Suggestions** (said, done, risky):

1. *Said:* "I didn't touch your name. You threw me at it. No."
2. *Done:* I press my card back to the glass and step into the circle.
3. *Risky:* I slide my card up the glass, past his name, all the way to number one.

The third one is legal (anybody may challenge anybody; the glass scrolls under a sliding card, and the challenge on Tetsu is only pending until confirmed, so this replaces it with no flag) and free for number one to decline. It is also the loudest thing a transfer has done in the Yard in a year, and Soyeon Ryu sees it.

## Major cast

Everyone at the Ridge is eighteen or older. Ages are stated and fixed.

### Tetsu Hamada, 22, number 87
**Role:** the man who shoved you; opening rival, possible ally.
**Look:** bleached buzzcut with 87 shaved into the left side, re-shaved every Sunday; a scar through one eyebrow; a Tower tracksuit a size too small.
**Want now:** a defence before six tomorrow, or he goes stale.
**Want under it:** to stay in the Tower. The stipend is the only reason he can stay enrolled, and his family's fish stall at the bottom of the steps is barely holding.
**Fear:** being nobody again, back behind the stall.
**Secret:** his training partner Gwon Seokjin was stood down last spring and left. Tetsu thinks he injured Gwon in sparring. He did not; the form was false.
**Limits:** two discipline strikes, so he will not fight outside a circle. He will not hit somebody who is down.
**Voice:** loud, short, locker-room. Calls you "transfer" until you earn a name. Talks about himself as "Eighty-seven" when he is cocky and drops the number when he is scared.

### Soyeon Ryu, 21, number 1
**Role:** the summit; the most watched person in the college.
**Look:** black hair cropped at the jaw with one thin braid at the nape; a training jacket zipped to the chin in every weather; her card worn backwards so the gold 1 faces her chest.
**Want now:** reach Scout Night still number one.
**Want under it:** one real fight with somebody who is not afraid of her.
**Fear:** pity; being carried.
**Secret 1:** a three-week-old tear in her left shoulder that she tapes herself with blue tape the clinic does not stock. She used her one refusal of the term on number 3 because of it.
**Secret 2:** she suspects the Long Table is behind the stand-downs and has said nothing, because speaking up would put her own shoulder in front of the clinic.
**Limits:** will not throw a fight, will not accept a favour she did not ask for.
**Voice:** very few words. Answers with a measurement or a question. Never raises her voice. When she is hurt she talks about food.

### Coach Dam Gyeongsuk, 58, instructor
**Role:** runs the Boiler Room, the basement gym under the Annex where the unranked train.
**Look:** grey hair cropped to the skull, a nose broken and set crooked twice, a coach's whistle on a cord that nobody has ever heard her blow.
**Want now:** one fighter who climbs the right way before she retires at the end of term.
**Fear:** that the board was her fault.
**Secret 1:** she helped design the board thirty years ago as a student, to give nobodies a ladder.
**Secret 2:** she coached Hajun Seol when he was unranked. He left her for the Long Table.
**Limits:** will not coach anyone who takes a Long Table fight. Will not set foot in the Tower.
**Voice:** gruff imperatives and kitchen words. Calls everyone by their dorm room number ("Four-Twelve"). Ends instructions with "Understood?" and does not wait.

### Minho Tak, 19, unranked, your roommate
**Role:** roommate in Annex room 4-12; the stats brain; comic relief with a spine.
**Look:** round glasses, a hoodie with the sleeves chewed, a tablet in a bright orange case covered in stickers of fighters he likes.
**Want now:** for his new roommate not to get destroyed on day one.
**Want under it:** to step into a circle once.
**Fear:** being found out.
**Secret:** he runs BoardWatch, the anonymous account that clips and posts every challenge. His spreadsheet noticed the stand-down pattern months ago and he posted nothing.
**Limits:** will not lie about a number. Will not post anything about you without asking.
**Voice:** fast, numbers and asides, "okay okay okay", catastrophe and hope in the same breath.

### Hajun Seol, 23, number 2
**Role:** runs the Long Table; the antagonist who never throws the first punch.
**Look:** a pressed white shirt with the sleeves rolled exactly twice, never a mark on it; a silver pen he clicks once before every offer.
**Want:** number one on Scout Night.
**Fear:** being, forever, the man in the eleven-second clip (Soyeon beat him in eleven seconds in their first year; it is the most watched video in the college).
**Secret 1:** the Long Table arranges stand-downs through the senior physician, Dr. Kwan. Seven names in two years.
**Secret 2:** he knows about Soyeon's shoulder because the Tower physio, who eats at the Long Table, watched her favour the arm on a stretch. Kwan will not stand down number one on paper in Scout Night week, because the league's own doctors review that and would pull the other seven forms. A shoulder that goes in a filmed fight needs no form: she is stood down on the footage, and if the challenger won, Kwan stands the winner down on Saturday night. Either way Hajun is number one at Sunday's sort.
**Offer:** soft fights into the top twenty by Saturday, a Tower room and food; in return, on Saturday morning, the fighter touches their card to number one, inside twenty places of her and with her one refusal spent.
**Limits:** never fights outside a circle, never touches anybody first, never tells an outright lie (he leaves things out).
**Voice:** warm and unhurried. Uses your name often. Speaks in offers, never threats. Says "we" when he means himself.

### Nari Hwang, 24, sports-medicine intern
**Role:** runs the Ice Room, the college clinic's treatment floor; patches you up; the whistleblower who has not blown.
**Look:** clinic tabard with an ice-cube pin, hair in a clip, a roll of athletic tape worn on her wrist like a bracelet.
**Want:** to graduate clean and undo one signature.
**Fear:** that she is the kind of person who signs things.
**Secret 1:** she kept copies of three stand-down forms for fighters she examined and cleared the same week.
**Secret 2:** she countersigned Gwon Seokjin's stand-down last April because Dr. Kwan told her to.
**Limits:** will not clear a fighter who is hurt. Will not sign another form she has not checked.
**Voice:** brisk and exact in clinical words, never graphic, then a joke. Tells you to sit down and hold still.

### Offstage names the storyteller may use

- **Gwon Seokjin, 23**, Tetsu's former partner, once number 31, stood down last spring, now fights for cash at Pier Six.
- **Bora Kim, 21**, number 14 until Wednesday's sort, when she is stood down: the third this term and the eighth in two years. Eats breakfast in the Annex and says she feels fine.

## Places

- **The Yard** — stone courtyard, the board on the north wall, the big white circle under it, benches, a coin-only vending machine. Start.
- **The Annex** — the unranked dorm, 1970s concrete, room 4-12, a shared kitchen with a noodle shelf.
- **The Boiler Room** — Coach Dam's basement gym under the Annex. Taped heavy bags, the boiler, a sign: NO BOARD TALK.
- **The Tower** — the ranked dorm, ten floors by number, a lobby with a card gate, a gym and a physio.
- **The Tenth Floor** — the Long Table's dining room. Open by invitation.
- **The Tower Roof** — laundry lines, the whole bay below, Soyeon alone at dawn.
- **The Pit** — the sunken arena for top-twenty challenges and Scout Night. Stone tiers, a lighting rig, stream cameras.
- **The Ice Room** — the college clinic: ice baths, treatment tables, a locked filing room.
- **The Proctors' Office** — under the board. Hearings, the challenge log, the server that runs the glass.
- **The Ninety-Nine Steps** — stone stairs from the college gate down to the city. Roadwork at dawn; fights nobody records.
- **The Night Market** — the bottom of the steps: the noodle place that closes at two, the Hamada fish stall, Nari's flat upstairs.
- **Pier Six** — off campus. Cash fights, no board, no proctors. Where stood-down fighters go.

## Behavioural state (invisible)

- **Wind** (good high): the breath left in the day. Rest restores it.
- **Heat** (good low): how closely the Proctors' Office is watching. Fights outside a circle and break-ins raise it.
- **Hurt** (good low): what the week has done to the body. Heals slowly; fighting through it adds to it.

## Factions

- **The Proctors' Office** — the rules. Public violence costs standing here first.
- **The Hundred** — the ranked, as a body. Their respect is earned in circles.
- **The Annex** — the unranked. The player's people if they want them.
- **The Long Table** — Hajun's top-ten court.

## Five promises

1. **Finale — Scout Night.** Seed: a countdown in the corner of the board. Payoff: the Pit full to the top row, and whatever number the player earned standing under the lights.
2. **Rival — Number 87.** Seed: a number shaved into a man's head, freshly done. Payoff: Tetsu tells you about Gwon and asks for something he has never asked anyone.
3. **Relationship — Number One.** Seed: a card worn backwards so the gold 1 faces her chest. Payoff: a shoulder she has been taping alone, and the fight she has wanted for a year.
4. **Mystery — The stand-downs.** Seed: a name greys out at six, and the fighter is eating breakfast saying she is fine. Payoff: seven forms, one signature, a long table on the tenth floor.
5. **Theme — What the board can't count.** Seed: Coach Dam's sign, NO BOARD TALK. Payoff: the player chooses something the glass has no column for: Minho in a circle, a bowl saved in the Annex kitchen, or Tetsu keeping his room.

## Emergent routes

Climb clean with Coach Dam; climb fast on the Long Table's soft fights; say yes to Hajun and mean no; refuse the board and get to the Pit through an exhibition; expose the stand-downs to the proctors or to every phone in the Yard; keep the proof as leverage; protect Soyeon; challenge Soyeon; fall for Soyeon or for Nari; turn Tetsu from bully to ally; get Minho into a circle; get expelled; walk down the steps and fight at Pier Six.

- **The Hundred and First** — Unique. You beat number one, the Long Table is exposed, and you refuse the number.
- **The Board Goes Dark** — Rare. The stand-downs come out and the glass is switched off for an inquiry.
- **Her Corner** — Rare. You stand in Soyeon's corner on Scout Night instead of across from her.
- **A Number of Your Own** — Common. You are on the board somewhere, and you earned every place.
- **Eighty-Seven** — Uncommon. Tetsu keeps his room, and it is partly your doing.
- **Seated** — Uncommon. You took the Long Table's deal and you have a number you did not earn.
- **Number Two Moves Up** — Common. You did the Table's job and Hajun has number one.
