## The pitch

You are three weeks into an internship in the press office of the agency that manages the city's heroes. Your job is
coffee, clipboards and holding the water at the side of the stage. This morning the number one hero, Ace Valor, holds
a press conference to bury a scandal: a gossip streamer has photos of him on a rooftop at three in the morning with
the city's most wanted villain. The agency hired an actress to stand beside him as his girlfriend. She never shows.

So when the reporter asks the question, Ace grabs the nearest hand. Yours.

"Smile. You're my girlfriend for thirty days." Lower: "Please."

## Why this fantasy works

It is Good Morning, Husband's structural bet moved into a superhero city: the relationship already exists on turn
one, the player did not choose it, and the whole game is deciding what to do with it. Fake dating adds the thing a
marriage does not have: an audience. Every scene has two versions, the one for the cameras and the one after the
door closes, and the player is the only person who sees both sides of the number one hero.

The romance is slow on purpose. Grabbing the player's hand at the press conference is the only time Ace ever touches
them without asking, and he is mortified about it; from then on he asks before he touches the player's hand, every
single time, even on camera. His feet leave the ground by an inch when he is flustered and he cannot stop it. That tell is the romance meter the
player can actually see, and it never needs a number.

The love triangle is honest. Shade, the villain who noticed you, flirts like a stage villain and never lies to you.
He is also your roommate's missing brother, which neither of them knows you know.

Thirty turns of fake dates, a bad interview, noodles in the Narrows and one argument about a breakup press release
that was written before you met is a complete way to play this. The mystery (what happened at Ferris Street) is
there for a player who pulls on it, and it drives the ending if they do.

## Playable span

Thirty days, from the press conference at 11:05 on day 1 to the Founders' Gala on the night of day 30, when the city
council votes on Ace's contract. Day 31, the date already printed on the breakup press release, is the last step. The
narrator's clock reads "Day N" for two weeks and then "Week N", so every world event says its own day out loud.

## The world, in plain words

- **Calder City** licenses its heroes. A licensed hero works under a registered hero name, keeps their legal name
  sealed, and is ranked every Monday on the public **Hero Ranking** by rescues, public votes and sponsor reach.
- About one person in fifty is born with a power. The law says every power is registered at eighteen and only a
  licensed hero may use one in public.
- **Vantage Hero Management** manages eleven licensed heroes from Vantage Tower, forty floors of glass on the central
  plaza. Ace is most of its income.
- The **Guardian Contract** makes one hero the city's official hero and pays their agency for it. The council votes
  on renewing Ace's on the night of the Founders' Gala, day 30.
- **The Narrows** is the player's neighbourhood: walk-ups, washing lines, a laundromat, a noodle stand that never
  closes. Heroes fight over its roofs and the people who live there pay for the windows.
- **Ferris Street**, four years ago: a nine-storey block collapsed. The famous footage shows Ace flying forty people
  out. There is a bronze statue of him holding a beam over his head.

## The hidden truth (what the mystery pays out)

- At Ferris Street the building only stayed up because an unlicensed man in the basement, **Rafe Quinn**, made the
  building's own shadow solid and held it as a brace for four minutes, holding his breath until he passed out.
- Vantage cut him out of the footage because a man making shadows solid in a basement looked frightening on the news
  and a golden flier did not. **Mira Solano** made that call and signed the incident report that listed Rafe as a
  "suspected looter found in the basement". That line is why his hero licence was refused.
- Rafe became **Shade**. He robs the sponsors whose logos are on Ace's suit, returns most of what he takes, and has
  never hurt a bystander.
- The rooftop meeting: Ace went to ask Rafe to tell the truth together. Rafe refused to be rescued by the man who
  took the credit, and gave him a deadline: on gala night at half past nine he will play the uncut footage on every
  public screen in the city, unless Ace has said it first. The envelope in the rooftop photos held a statement Ace had
  written and signed, naming Rafe as the man who held Ferris Street up; Rafe would not take it.
- **Silver Kite** saw the brace at Ferris Street as a rookie and said nothing. Four years later she sent Dex Holloway
  the anonymous text that put him on the right street at three in the morning, because she wanted the truth out. She
  did not expect a fake girlfriend.
- **Jo Quinn**, the player's roommate, is Rafe's younger sister. She has not seen him in four years and does not know
  he is Shade. He has lived four streets away the whole time.
- **The stairwell:** Ace did not grab the nearest hand by accident. That morning, in a grey hoodie and glasses he does
  not need, he hid in the stairwell before the conference, and the player talked to him for five minutes about the
  broken vending machine without recognising him. He chose the one person in the building who had treated him like a
  person.

## Tone and pacing

- Superhero romantic comedy: bright, quick, warm, with a real secret underneath.
- Two versions of every scene: the one for the cameras and the one after the door closes.
- Hand-holding is an event. Romance goes at most as far as a kiss. Nobody's body is described at length.
- Ace's floating is the barometer: he floats when he is flustered, happy or about to say something true.
- Fights are fast and end in rescues and wreckage, never in described injuries.
- Nobody is a villain in the moral sense. Mira protects a lie that seemed small when she told it; Shade is angry for a
  good reason; Dex wants the truth; Kite wants to win fairly.
- Present tense, second person, webnovel cadence.
- Saying no does not end the story. If the player tells the cameras it is fake, refuses the agreement or walks out,
  the month goes on without the contract: Mira switches to damage control, Dex wants the exclusive, Shade's card still
  turns up in the bag, Jo's rent is still due on the fifth, and Ace still comes to apologise in person. (This line is
  in `toneGuide`, because the production storyteller never reads quest-step `directorNotes`.)

## Cast

Every one of them wants something that has nothing to do with the player.

### Ace Valor (Nathaniel "Nate" Ward), 27, the number one hero

- **Role:** Number one for four years. The player's fake boyfriend for thirty days.
- **Power:** He can fly, and he can lift anything he can get both arms around.
- **Looks:** Tall, broad-shouldered, sun-gold hair the agency styles and he flattens the second he is off camera, warm
  brown eyes. On duty: a white-and-gold flight suit with a high collar, gold piping down the arms and a short navy
  cape. Off duty: a grey hoodie, glasses he does not need, odd socks. The fixture's `appearance` is the on-duty look
  only, because the portrait and the opening panel are drawn from it.
- **Tell:** His boots drift an inch off the ground when he is nervous, and he cannot stop it. In a waist-up portrait it
  shows as the short cape drifting up behind him, which is his `visualHook`.
- **Immediate want:** Get through thirty days without the rooftop story becoming the Ferris Street story.
- **Long-term want:** One person in the city who calls him by his real name and means the man, not the poster.
- **Fear:** That without the number he is nobody anyone would grab by the hand. Basements, since Ferris Street.
- **Secrets:** Ferris Street; the stairwell; his name is Nate and nobody has used it in eight years.
- **Boundaries:** After the grab on the press stage, which he apologises for the first moment they are alone, never
  touches the player without asking. Will not lie about Ferris Street to the player; he leaves
  the room instead. Will not hit anyone without powers.
- **Voice:** Two voices. On camera: smooth, short heroic lines the agency wrote ("Nobody falls today."). Off camera:
  halting, over-polite, asks permission for small things, says please and sorry more than anyone in the city.

### Mira Solano, 36, his manager

- **Role:** Head of talent at Vantage. Ace's manager since he was nineteen. Author of the thirty-day plan.
- **Looks:** Sharp black bob, red-framed glasses, an uncreased charcoal suit, three phones, and a ring binder with
  thirty coloured tabs, one for each day.
- **Motive:** She built the number one hero and has thirty days to keep him there. The contract vote decides whether
  Vantage survives.
- **Fear:** Being the villain in the story she wrote. The day Ace stops needing her.
- **Secrets:** She cut Rafe Quinn out of the Ferris Street footage and signed the report that called him a looter. She
  had the player's background check done in forty minutes on day 1.
- **Boundaries:** Will not lie about money or the contract; every clause means what it says. Will not threaten Jo or
  the player's family; she considers that amateur.
- **Voice:** Clipped. Times to the minute, numbered options, no adjectives. Dry jokes with no change of expression.

### Shade (Rafe Quinn), 28, the villain who noticed you

- **Role:** Number one on the most-wanted list.
- **Power:** He can make any shadow solid as steel for as long as he holds his breath.
- **Looks:** Lean, messy black hair, grey eyes, a lopsided smile, a long black coat with the collar up, fingerless
  gloves, a matte black half-mask he takes off when he wants you to trust him.
- **Motive:** He wants the city to know who held the building up, and he wants Ace to be the one who says it. He wants
  his sister back and is terrified she will look at him like a criminal.
- **Fear:** Jo finding out what he became. Basements. Being right about Ace and having to go through with it.
- **Secrets:** He is Jo's brother. He has a copy of the uncut footage and a way onto every public screen in the city,
  and will use it at half past nine on gala night.
- **Boundaries:** Never hurts a bystander. Never lies to the player. Never comes in through a front door.
- **Voice:** Theatrical and wry. Narrates his own entrances in stage directions, makes every request a deal with a
  price, calls the player "Thirty Days". Goes very quiet when anyone mentions basements.

### Jo Quinn, 24, your roommate

- **Role:** The player's best friend since university and flatmate for two years. Night-shift paramedic.
- **Looks:** Sturdy and square-shouldered, choppy dark ponytail, tired brown eyes, crooked grin, navy uniform with
  reflective stripes, trauma shears clipped to her chest pocket that she uses for everything, including snacks.
- **Motive:** Make rent on day 5. Find out what the player has got into. Stop the photographers sleeping on the
  laundromat steps.
- **Fear:** Losing the flat. Losing the player to a world with cameras in it. Finding out her brother is dead, or does
  not want her.
- **Secrets:** A gossip site offered her five thousand for photos inside the player's room, and she has not said no.
  A shoebox under her bed holds her brother's photo, his lighter and his refused licence.
- **Boundaries:** Will not be lied to about something big twice. Will not take money from a hero or from Vantage.
- **Voice:** Paramedic shorthand and gallows humour. Triages out loud. Calls heroes "flying billboards".

### Dex Holloway, 31, the gossip streamer

- **Role:** Broke the rooftop photos. Two hundred thousand people watch his stream. Sure the player is fake.
- **Looks:** Lanky, shaved head, neat goatee, round tinted glasses, loud patterned shirt under a battered press vest,
  a phone on a motorised gimbal that swivels to follow whoever he is talking about.
- **Motive:** He was a real reporter once; the paper that fired him was owned by a Vantage sponsor. He has chased
  Ferris Street for three years.
- **Fear:** Being right and nobody caring. Being wrong on camera.
- **Secrets:** The rooftop tip came from an anonymous text he has traced to Silver Kite's agency. He has a file with a
  witness who saw a black brace under the building and four minutes missing from every official timeline.
- **Boundaries:** Will not publish where the player lives. Will not run a story about Jo. Will not fake a photo; he
  will crop one.
- **Voice:** Stream patter. Talks to "chat" mid-sentence, says "receipts" about evidence, trades a quote for a quote.

### Silver Kite, 29, the number two hero

- **Role:** Number two for four years. Ace's oldest friend in the business, and the other half of the couple the
  tabloids invented.
- **Power:** She rides the wind on silver wing panels along her arms.
- **Looks:** Tall, athletic, silver-white undercut with a long braid, amber eyes, a scar through one eyebrow, a sleek
  silver-and-teal flight suit.
- **Motive:** Make Ace tell the truth before the vote. Take number one fairly, one day, and never because he fell.
- **Fear:** Winning the wrong way. Watching Ace shrink for the rest of his career.
- **Secrets:** She sent Dex the tip. She saw the brace at Ferris Street and said nothing.
- **Boundaries:** Will not date Ace. Will not lie to his face, though she will go behind his back. Does not fight
  people without powers.
- **Voice:** Short, blunt, talks in weather ("Forecast: you're going to get hurt"). Competitive about small talk.
