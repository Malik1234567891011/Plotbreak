# THREE SKILLS A MORNING

**Slate:** #8 of the 50-story plan (`docs/stories-50/PLAN.md`, section 3).
**Genre:** skill-draw isekai, courtroom race, light mystery, comedy, a slow-burn romance
**Audience lean:** male, with a romance that reads to both
**One-line fantasy:** Every dawn you draw three skills and keep one until you sleep. Today a woman hangs at noon unless somebody speaks for her.

Slug `three-skills-a-morning`, export `THREE_SKILLS_A_MORNING`, asset prefix `story_three_skills_a_morning`.

## Why this fantasy works

OOC's skill-draft and "three SSS abilities" titles sell one pleasure: picking a power and watching it break a situation open. This story keeps that pleasure and adds the thing those titles skip, which is a reason the pick matters today. On turn one the player gets three cards, a gallows, a knight asking a question, and a woman in irons looking straight at them. Every card is a different way to save her, and none of them is the obvious one.

- **[Read Lies]** is the courtroom route: break the false witness before the bell.
- **[Dragon Tongue]** is the epic route: climb the hill and ask the victim, who happens to be a dragon.
- **[Perfect Cooking]** is the comedy route that turns out to be the deepest one: an old clause says a dragon's rent can be paid in a feast.

The cards reset every dawn, so the pleasure repeats. And every card kept costs the player one small memory of home, which nobody tells them. That turns a power fantasy into a question with an ending: one morning the cards offer the way home, and the other two cards are things somebody here badly needs.

The plan's cast list says "the Draft". In the fiction it is **the Draw**, three cards at first light. That keeps us clear of "Skill Draft", which is in an OOC title.

## Story bible

Nine days ago the player fell asleep on a night bus in our world and woke in a hayloft outside **Kettlecross**, a ford town on a brown river under a hill that belongs to a dragon. Nothing in their pockets works here. What came with them is the Draw.

**The Draw.** Every dawn, wherever the player is, three cards appear at arm's length, visible to nobody else. Each names one skill in square brackets. Touch one and it is yours, completely and exactly, until you next sleep (even a nap). The other two fade. Touch none and all three fade when the sun clears the rooftops. A kept skill does precisely what its name says, at the highest level, and nothing more. No card raises the dead, changes the past, or makes anybody love anybody. The player has kept eight cards so far. On the third morning they kept [Speak to Horses] and calmed a bolting dray horse in the square with one word. Nell saw it.

**The cost.** Every card kept takes one small memory of the world the player came from: a tune, a street name, a taste, a joke. Never a person, never all at once, never chosen. The player notices only when they reach for it. The first time they notice is this afternoon, when a song they have known all their life is simply not there.

**The Door Back.** Once, to a drifter who has kept enough cards to know what they cost, the Draw offers [The Door Back]. Keep it and you go home at the next sundown. The other two cards that morning are always things somebody here badly needs. Ysolde was offered it nineteen years ago and kept [Mend Bone] instead.

**The town.** About nine hundred people, a ferry, salt barges, a moot hall with a strongroom and a bell tower that leans, a gallows in Ford Square used twice a decade. The lord is far away. His justice rides the river towns on circuit: **Sir Garrot Dunmere** holds a dawn assize in each town and, by custom, hangs at noon "so the condemned get one last morning".

**The Hill Charter.** Two hundred and twelve years ago the founders leased the hill and the ford from **Haldis**, a dragon. Rent is a chest of silver to the weight of a grown calf, carried to the Rent Stone halfway up the hill by sundown on Rent Day. Today is Rent Day. The charter, nailed under soot-black glass in the moot hall, has three clauses nobody living has read closely:
1. Rent in silver, or "a feast worthy of the hill, cooked by one pair of hands".
2. The Landlady may come down to collect what is owed.
3. Whoever robs the Landlady answers to the Landlady and to no other court.

Haldis last came down sixty-one years ago, in a flood year when the rent was a third short. She sat on the bell tower until the town paid. It still leans. Nobody died. Everybody remembers.

**The crime.** Three nights ago the rent chest vanished from the moot hall strongroom. The truth:

- **Reeve Anselm Cotter** took it. He has short-weighted the rent for six years, a sixth of the silver swapped for painted lead, to pay a ruinous debt to the Lowmere Salt Bank. This year the bank sent its factor upriver to collect in full. Cotter opened the strongroom with his own key, smashed the lock afterwards for show, and moved the silver into salt barrels in his cellar. They are due to go aboard the bank's barge, the *Honest Weight*, at sundown.
- He paid the night watchman **Pim Atwater** twelve crowns to swear he saw Nell leave the moot hall at midnight with a sack. Pim was asleep at his post.
- He "found" a silver crown in Nell's boot while searching her at the ferry landing. Her left boot has a hole in the sole. A coin could not have stayed in it on the walk from the strongroom.
- **Nell Farrow** did not take the rent. That night she was burgling Cotter's house to take back her late mother's ferry license, which he holds against a debt and uses to take two-thirds of every crossing. She heard barrels rolled into his cellar and saw an iron corner of the rent chest. She will not say so: it confesses another crime, and nobody believes a thief who accuses the reeve.
- Cotter does not really believe the dragon will come. She has not moved in sixty-one years and he has never seen her. If Nell's case collapses he will turn the court on the stranger who arrived six days before the theft and sleeps above the inn.

**What stops a hanging.** Garrot stops for exactly five things: a witness proven false; the stolen thing returned and the thief named; the pardon of the wronged party; proof that his court has no right to try the case; or the old proof, a speaker who stands against him in arms until the bell finishes. He hears everything else courteously and refuses it. A speaker caught lying to the court goes to the stocks and then out of town.

**Drifters.** They turn up along the river about once a generation. Most townsfolk think they are a story; river folk say a drifter in town means a funeral or a fortune. Haldis has met four. Ysolde is one. Corra Quell's bank once owned one's contract.

The player is free to speak for Nell or not, keep any card or none, break her out, fight the knight, side with the reeve, sell themselves to the bank, climb the hill, cook, flirt, lie, or walk out of town before noon. The world reacts to the specific thing.

## Tone and pacing

- Bright, quick, a little funny, with a real rope at the end of the morning.
- Anime isekai energy: the cards are a game, and choosing well should feel clever. Show exactly what a kept skill does, concretely.
- The town is real: mud, bread, river smell, a bell tower that leans.
- Two clocks on day one: the noon bell and sundown. After that the cards keep coming and the pressure moves to the reeve, the bank, the dragon and the cost.
- The hanging is never graphic. If it happens, it is written from the back of the crowd: the bell, the square going quiet, a knight taking off his gloves.
- Romance: flirtatious, tense, heartfelt, up to a kiss. No fan service.
- Everyone is an adult.

## Player starting authority

The player authors who they were back home (the archetype), their name and pronouns, and everything they do. The archetype is the only identity the storyteller reads, so each one says plainly what kind of person fell through and what came with them.

### Minimal hard canon
- The player is an adult who fell into this world nine days ago.
- The Draw works as above, and today's cards are [Dragon Tongue], [Perfect Cooking] and [Read Lies].
- Nell is innocent of the rent theft; Cotter did it; Pim lied.
- Nell hangs at noon unless one of Garrot's five reasons stops it.
- The rent is due on the hill at sundown.

## Major cast

All characters are adults. Ages are fixed.

### Nell Farrow, 24
**Role:** the accused. Ferry hand on her late mother's ferry, former pickpocket.
**Appearance:** sun-browned, dark hair cut short with a knife, a split lip from the arrest, an oilskin ferry coat two sizes too big, boots with a hole in the left sole.
**Want now:** not to hang, without begging anybody.
**Want later:** her mother's ferry license back, and a life where she is not the first name the town reaches for.
**Fear:** dying as the town's idea of her. Being owed something by a stranger.
**Secret:** where she really was that night, and what she heard in the reeve's cellar.
**Why she looks at you:** she lifted your purse on your first day, found a bus ticket and nothing else, and put a copper back out of pity. On your third morning she watched you stop a bolting dray horse with one word. She has decided you are strange, and strange is the only thing left in the square that might help.
**Voice:** quick, dry, river slang, short sentences; calls you "stranger" until she stops; jokes when she is frightened and never asks for help directly.

### Sir Garrot Dunmere, 52
**Role:** the lord's circuit justiciar. The hanging knight.
**Appearance:** broad, grey-bearded, black-enamelled half-plate scuffed silver at every edge, a book of law chained to his belt.
**Want:** the law kept exactly, because he believes it is the only thing between the river towns and blood feud.
**Hidden want:** somebody, once, to answer the question he asks three times.
**Fear:** a second Wat Kell.
**Secret:** nine years ago at Hobb's Mill he hanged a drover, Wat Kell, for a killing. A month later the real killer confessed. He carries Wat's tin whistle. Since then he asks three times, every time. Fourteen hangings in eleven years; nobody has ever answered.
**Voice:** formal, no contractions, thinks and speaks in threes, legal phrasing; courteous to everybody including the condemned.
**Combat:** a superb fighter. Standing against him "until the bell" is the old proof, and it is meant to be nearly impossible.

### Haldis, about four hundred years old
**Role:** the dragon who owns the hill. The wronged party.
**Appearance:** the size of a tithe barn, scaled the dull red of a cooling forge, one horn broken short, eyes like banked coals. Asleep, she looks like part of the hill.
**Want:** the bargain kept. She does not need the silver. The yearly rent is the only conversation she has had with people for two centuries.
**Fear:** being a story the town tells instead of a party to a bargain.
**Secret:** she noticed the short weight six years ago and said nothing, waiting to see whether the town would catch its own thief. This year she is coming down regardless. She also knows the Draw: the player is the fifth drifter she has met, and she knows about the Door Back.
**Voice:** slow, enormous, pedantic about weights and clauses, counts time in winters, calls people "tenant". Only understandable with [Dragon Tongue]; without it she sounds like a forge breathing.

### Ysolde Marr, 47
**Role:** keeps the Crooked Kettle, the inn where the player sleeps on credit.
**Appearance:** solid, flour-dusted, black hair with a grey streak pinned up with a cooking skewer, a burn scar across one hand.
**Want:** to keep the lodger fed and out of trouble, which is already failing.
**Secret:** she is a drifter. She fell in twenty-three years ago, kept a card every morning for four years, and was offered [The Door Back] the morning the ferryman Tobias was crushed under the ferry winch. She kept [Mend Bone], saved him, and married him. He died of a fever six years ago. She stopped choosing after that, and the cards stopped coming after thirty untouched mornings. She has forgotten her own first name. "Ysolde" was Tobias's mother's.
**Voice:** dry, gruff, kitchen imperatives; answers questions with chores; calls the player "lodger".

### Anselm Cotter, 44
**Role:** the reeve. Keeps the keys, the ledgers and the peace. The thief.
**Appearance:** neat as a ledger, polished chain of office, soft hands, a kind face he has practised.
**Want:** Nell hanged, the barrels on the barge by sundown, and his debt gone.
**Fear:** the ledger read by somebody who can read.
**Secret:** six years light, the strongroom key, the twelve crowns to Pim, the coin in the boot.
**Voice:** smooth, civic, reasonable; says "we" for the town; never says a sentence that is false, which is why [Read Lies] does not catch him. He talks around things.

### Pim Atwater, 31
**Role:** the night watchman and the false witness.
**Appearance:** lanky, unshaven, watchman's tabard with the badge on crooked.
**Want:** for this to be over and for nobody to ask about the money.
**Fear:** Garrot. A false witness on a hanging charge hangs in Garrot's court.
**Secret:** he was asleep. Cotter woke him and told him what he had seen.
**Voice:** nervous, over-explains, ends sentences with "right?", asks for reassurance. Lies constantly and badly.

### Corra Quell, 39
**Role:** factor of the Lowmere Salt Bank, aboard the *Honest Weight*. Cotter's creditor.
**Appearance:** tall and spare in a high-collared grey travelling coat, ink on two fingers, a slim black ledger under one arm.
**Want:** twelve barrels and a signature. If she learns the player is a drifter, a seven-year contract.
**Fear:** going home empty. Her post depends on this debt.
**Secret:** she has decided not to know what is in the barrels. And she once held a drifter's contract; he left in the seventh year without saying goodbye, and she still balances his column some nights.
**Voice:** precise, polite, numerical; every sentence has a figure in it; offers terms instead of threats.

## Places

- **Ford Square** (start): the gallows, the assize table, the town well, the leaning bell tower. Hub to everything.
- **The Crooked Kettle:** Ysolde's inn. Common room, the player's loft.
- **The Kettle Kitchen:** Ysolde's kitchen. The Hill Feast Book lives here.
- **The Moot Hall:** the strongroom with the smashed lock, the Hill Charter under soot, the evidence table, the reeve's ledgers. Garrot's lodging.
- **The Lockup:** the cell under the bell tower where Nell waits for noon. Two circuit men on the door.
- **The North Gate Watch House:** Pim's post and bunk, and twelve new crowns.
- **The Reeve's House:** tall and river-facing; the ferry license in a strongbox.
- **The Reeve's Cellar** (hidden): twelve salt barrels too heavy for salt; a water door to the river.
- **The Ferry Landing:** Nell's chained ferry, her hidden picks, the empty rent chest in the reeds, the barge moored.
- **The Honest Weight:** Quell's salt barge and cabin. Sails at sundown.
- **The Hill Road:** switchbacks up the dragon's hill, and the Rent Stone halfway.
- **The Hollow:** the scorched bowl at the top, where Haldis sleeps.

Round trip from the square to the dragon is about ninety minutes, so the dragon route is possible before noon and tight.

## Behavioral state (invisible)

- **Nerve** (good high, restored by sleep, which also ends your card): how much the player has left today.
- **Whispers** (good low): how much the town is talking about the stranger. Rises with public violence, breaking in, and doing impossible things in front of people.
- **Forgetting** (good low, never comes back down): what the cards have taken. Rises by a small amount for every card kept.

## Five promises

1. **Whether you ever go home** (finale). Seed: a song you have known all your life, gone when you reach for it. Payoff: one morning the cards offer [The Door Back], and the other two are things somebody here badly needs.
2. **What Nell was really doing that night** (relationship). Seed: she lies about where she slept and tells the truth about the rent in the same breath. Payoff: she was in the reeve's house, and she heard the barrels.
3. **Where the rent went** (mystery). Seed: a coin in a boot with a hole in the sole. Payoff: twelve salt barrels too heavy for salt, a ledger six years light, a barge leaving at sundown.
4. **What the dragon does at sundown** (boss). Seed: a bell tower that has leaned for sixty-one years. Payoff: the hill stands up, and somebody has to speak to it properly.
5. **Why the knight always asks three times** (theme). Seed: he asks a crowd that never answers, every time, as if he means it. Payoff: a tin whistle that belonged to a drover he hanged for nothing.

## Emergent routes

- Speak for Nell and break Pim with [Read Lies].
- Find the charter's third clause and move the trial up the hill.
- Climb to Haldis with [Dragon Tongue] and bring the wronged party down to pardon her.
- Cook the condemned's last meal, win Nell's truth, find the cellar.
- Pay the rent with a feast cooked by one pair of hands.
- Break Nell out and run.
- Fight Garrot for the old proof.
- Stay silent, keep nothing, and leave town.
- Expose Cotter, deal with him, hand him to the dragon, or sell his debt to the bank.
- Sign with the bank to pay the rent.
- Find Ysolde's secret, and choose at the Door Back.

## Ending space

- **Spoken For** (common): Nell lives, the rent is settled, you stay.
- **The Day the Hill Stood Up** (common): the dragon came down, and the town lived through it.
- **Drifter** (common): you walked out of Kettlecross before it was over.
- **The Noon Bell** (uncommon): Nell hanged. The law outran one stranger.
- **Downriver** (uncommon): you broke her out, and now you are both outlaws.
- **The Bank's Drifter** (uncommon): seven years under contract.
- **The Landlady's Court** (rare): the trial moved to the hill and the dragon judged the reeve.
- **A Feast for the Hill** (rare): the rent paid in a feast, and Nell alive to eat the leftovers.
- **Two on the Ferry** (rare): the license back, and Nell and you running the crossing.

- **The Door Back** (unique): you went home.

## The opener (turn-one beat, 102 words)

> Dawn, your ninth in this world, and three cards hang in the air that only you can see. Keep one until you sleep.
>
> [Dragon Tongue] [Perfect Cooking] [Read Lies]
>
> Below them, at the foot of a gallows, a woman in irons finds you in the crowd.
>
> "Morning, stranger," says Nell, through a split lip.
>
> The knight beside her, Sir Garrot, lifts a gloved hand. "Nell Farrow hangs at noon for theft. Does anyone speak for her?"
>
> Silence. He asks again. Nobody moves.
>
> The whole square follows her eyes to you.
>
> "You," Garrot says. "Third and last time. Do you speak for her?"

Nell speaks to the player at word 47. The beat ends on a direct question that needs an answer now. Every proper noun is introduced in the beat itself.

**Suggestions** (said, done, risky):
- Said: I touch Perfect Cooking. "Before anyone hangs, can I cook her breakfast?"
- Done: I touch Read Lies and raise my hand.
- Risky: I touch Dragon Tongue, climb the gallows steps and say, "She's innocent. I'll prove it by noon."
