## Premise

Eighteen years ago a war ended in a single night when a city of four million came apart, and the pilot everybody blames for it died inside his machine.

The machine did not. It has stood behind armoured glass on a neutral station ever since — sealed, warm, and refusing to open — while the two governments that nearly ended the world sign the same treaty on the same day every year and call it peace.

You are on that station for the anniversary. So is most of the press.

At eight minutes past one it turns its eyes on. It tears out of its braces without firing a shot, empties the plaza, walks the length of it to where you happen to be standing, and kneels.

The cockpit opens. A voice comes out of it and says you are the pilot.

You have no idea why. Neither does anybody else, and by tonight several people who run things will need an answer more urgently than you do.

Some of them want the machine. Some of them want you. One of them already knows what actually happened that night and has spent eighteen years keeping it secret.

You do not have to get in. Something underneath the armistice has started moving again, and it has noticed you either way.


## Opening location

The Memorial Plaza: Vast orbital memorial hall, curved viewport with a blue-grey industrial planet beyond, polished floor, walls of engraved names, a temporary stage and press risers, a huge black mech behind armoured glass at the far end. Solemn, crowded, cold light.
