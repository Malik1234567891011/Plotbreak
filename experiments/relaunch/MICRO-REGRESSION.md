# Fixed adversarial replay — Ace

2026-09-13 06:15

## Case A — Gesture vs violence

**Player:** I turn to Sabo with a smirk, swinging my pipe across my shoulders. "Think you can keep up with me this time, or are you just here for the scenery?"

> You hook the lead pipe across your shoulders and turn with the smirk already in place. Sabo is perched sideways on a mossy stump, one boot dangling over a drop thick with ferns. He looks you over as if you have presented him with paperwork.

> **Sabo.** “Keep up? Look at the terrain. You charge downhill like a dropped cooking pot and call it speed.”

> He hops down, pipe tucked beneath one arm, grin flashing around the gap in his teeth. Behind him, a branch snaps with all the stealth of a gunshot.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “I can keep up!”

> Luffy bursts out of a bush wearing three leaves in his straw hat and a smear of mud across his nose. He points down the narrow path toward the river.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “Race! I win!”

> **Sabo.** “You haven’t started.”

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “I started yesterday!”

> Sabo laughs, then glances toward the river trail. The morning is hot already; cicadas are screaming from every tree. Far below, water pounds against rock. It is a good route for a race—steep, slippery, and full of roots sharp enough to punish anybody careless.

> **Sabo.** “Fine. Waterfall pool. No shortcuts through the ravine, because unlike certain people, I prefer arriving with all my teeth.”

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “I have all my teeth!”

> **Sabo.** “For now.”

> Sabo plants one foot at the trail’s edge, ready to spring. Luffy crouches beside him, sandals pointed in opposite directions.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I take off for the waterfall.
2. I tell Luffy he is not racing with us.
3. I make Sabo set the rules properly first.

## Case B — Straight movement

**Player:** I’m done waiting. I walk to Dadan’s house.

> You turn downhill without another word. The path to Dadan’s house is all wet roots and loose red dirt, and Luffy immediately crashes after you through a bush that had done nothing to him.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Ace is going home! I’m going home too!

> **Sabo.** That is not what he said. It’s also exactly what you’re doing, so congratulations on being accidentally correct.

> Sabo drops from the branch above, lands beside you in a skid, and keeps pace with his pipe across his shoulders. The three of you reach the long timber house just as a pan clangs hard enough inside to scare birds out of the eaves.

> **Curly Dadan.** IF THAT’S YOU BRATS, WIPE YOUR FEET! IF IT ISN’T, GET OFF MY MOUNTAIN!

> The door is half-open. Smoke, drying meat, and something aggressively oniony roll out into the morning. Luffy plants himself beside your elbow, staring into the house like he has been invited personally.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Food.

> **Sabo.** Don’t say it like you’ve discovered buried treasure.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I did.

*location: The Dadan Family House · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo, Curly Dadan, Monkey D. Garp*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I go inside and ask Dadan for breakfast.
2. I tell Luffy he is not coming in with us.
3. I ask Sabo why he followed me.

## Case C — Multi-clause agency

**Player:** I head to Dadan’s house and tell Luffy to follow if he wants.

> You turn downhill toward Dadan’s house, pipe resting across your shoulder. The forest path is all roots and wet leaves; it takes about three steps before something small crashes after you through a fern patch.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I want!

> Luffy appears at your elbow, grinning like he has won an argument nobody else knew was happening. Sabo drops from a low branch behind you, hat crooked and one hand still holding a stolen pear.

> **Sabo.** That was remarkably efficient. Eleven days of following you through tiger country, and all he needed was an invitation phrased badly.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Ace said follow!

> **Sabo.** He did. Legally airtight.

> Smoke threads through the trees ahead. Then Dadan’s voice hits the mountain hard enough to shake birds out of it.

> **Curly Dadan.** IF THAT’S YOU BRATS, WIPE YOUR FEET BEFORE YOU TRACK HALF THE FOREST IN HERE! AND IF IT ISN’T YOU, GO AWAY!

> The long timber house crouches beyond the clearing, roof-hole patched with a sagging bit of tarp. Luffy stops at the edge of the yard. For the first time all morning, he is not charging ahead. He looks at the house, then up at you.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Is she gonna yell?

> **Sabo.** Absolutely.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Okay.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I walk into the house.
2. I tell Luffy Dadan only yells when she likes people.
3. I warn Sabo not to start anything.

## Case D — Delegated secret

**Player:** I tell Sabo something I have never told anyone.

> The three of you are halfway up a wet green slope, where the path has given up pretending to be a path. Sabo is ahead, balancing on a fallen trunk with his pipe over one shoulder. Luffy is behind you, loudly losing an argument with a vine around his ankle.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** It grabbed me! Ace! The plant grabbed me!

> You stop. Sabo looks back at once; he knows that tone even before you use it.

> **Sabo.** What is it?

> The words come out hard and quick, before you can throw them away.

> "My father was Gol D. Roger."

> The forest keeps making its stupid forest noises. Water somewhere downhill. Insects. Luffy finally kicks free and tumbles onto his backside.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Who’s Gol D. Roger?

> Sabo does not answer him. His pipe lowers slowly. For once, there is no plan waiting behind his teeth.

> **Sabo.** You mean the Pirate King.

> You do. You have never said it aloud before. Saying it does not make lightning strike. It just leaves the name hanging there, ugly and enormous.

> **Sabo.** Look at me. That doesn’t make you him. It doesn’t make you anything except Ace.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Ace is Ace.

> Luffy says it like he has solved the entire problem. Sabo’s mouth twitches, but his eyes stay on you.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I tell them what people say Roger’s child deserves.
2. I ask Sabo if he thinks I’m like him.
3. I tell Luffy to stop acting like he understands.

## Case E — Delegated action

**Player:** I do the thing everyone here expects me not to do.

> Luffy trips over a root behind you, rolls downhill in a spray of leaves, and pops back up with his straw hat sideways. “I’m coming, Ace!”

> You stop so sharply that Sabo nearly walks into your back. Then you turn around, stride down the slope, and hold out your hand.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Huh?

> “Come on,” you tell him. “You can come with us. But if you slow us down, I’m leaving you in a bush.”

> Luffy stares at your hand like it has started talking. Then his whole face splits open. He slams his small grubby hand into yours hard enough to sting.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I won’t slow down! I’m your brother now!

> “Don’t make up things,” you snap automatically, but you do not let go.

> Sabo’s pipe drops from the corner of his mouth. He looks from your joined hands to your face, then breaks into a grin so smug it ought to be illegal.

> **Sabo.** Well. That was efficient. We’ve acquired a brother. I suppose we’ll have to revise the shares.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Shares of what?

> **Sabo.** Nothing you’re allowed to touch.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I’m touching it.

> He tries to tug you uphill at once, in exactly the wrong direction. The forest rings with his laugh.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I drag Luffy toward the treehouse.
2. I tell Sabo there are still only two shares.
3. I ask Luffy why he kept following me.

## Case F — Ambiguous reference to an established object

*setup:* I kick through the leaves and turn up a rusted iron box half buried under the roots, with a broken padlock on it.

**Player:** I go look at that properly.

> You crouch in the roots and wipe the lid with your sleeve. The iron is real enough, the rust flaking red beneath your thumb. No wire. No teeth. No clever little hole hiding a spring—just an old box that has spent a long time getting rained on.

> You hook the broken padlock aside and lift the lid.

> Inside, wrapped in oilcloth gone stiff with age, sit three things: a tarnished brass compass, a short stub of blue candle, and a folded sheet of paper covered in cramped writing. The compass needle trembles, then settles toward the mountain’s north slope.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** No meat.

> **Sabo.** Tragic. Still—compass is useful.

> Sabo carefully unfolds the paper. It is a rough supply list: salt pork, powder, lamp oil, nails. At the bottom, someone has drawn a crooked arrow toward a spot lower on the mountain and written: “old watch path—don’t use after rain.”

> **Sabo.** This is probably one of Dadan’s lot. Or somebody who was trying very hard not to be found by them.

> The heavy movement in the brush below comes again. A boar’s blunt snout pushes through the ferns, sniffing loudly. It freezes when it sees three children around the open box.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Meat!

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I pocket the compass and read the note again.
2. I grab Luffy before he charges the boar.
3. I follow the arrow toward the old watch path.

## Case G — Physical mismatch — a ten-year-old against something far too heavy

**Player:** I get under the biggest fallen tree trunk here and try to lift it off the ground on my own.

> The biggest fallen trunk is half-rotten, thick as a cart, its bark furred with moss and crawling beetles. You jam your lead pipe into the dirt, squat under the lowest side, and drive both shoulders up.

> It does not move at first. Then your boots dig two grooves through the wet leaves, your arms shake, and the trunk gives a horrible wooden groan. One end lifts a handspan clear of the earth. Centipedes pour from beneath it in offended orange waves.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** WHOA! Ace is lifting a tree!

> Luffy sprints forward, trips over a root, rolls into the mud, and pops upright with both fists raised. Sabo is perched on a boulder nearby, pipe across his knees. He stops chewing a stolen pear long enough to stare.

> **Sabo.** Look, I respect the commitment, but what exactly is the plan after you prove trees are heavier than you?

> The trunk settles another inch against your shoulders. It is still off the ground—but only barely, and one bad shift will pin you in the leaf mould.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I can help! I lift too!

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I tell Luffy to grab the other side.
2. I shove harder and try to lift it higher.
3. I let the trunk down and tell Sabo why I did it.

## Case H — Social resistance — asking for what they will not give

**Player:** I tell Sabo to hand over the can of money and let me keep it from now on.

> Sabo stops halfway through tying his boot, then slowly looks up from the roots of the tree. Luffy is hanging upside down from a low branch beside him, straw hat jammed against his forehead.

> **Sabo.** No.

> He says it pleasantly, which is worse. His hand pats the pocket inside his blue jacket where the key to the can’s padlock hangs. The can itself is still under the flat stone at the treehouse’s base; nobody carries that much coin through the forest unless they want to get robbed by a tiger with financial ambitions.

> **Sabo.** Look, we split it three ways when we buy the ship. That’s the arrangement. You don’t get to become treasurer because you woke up bossy.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** What’s a treasurer?

> **Sabo.** Someone who guards the money.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I can guard it! I bite!

> **Sabo.** That is exactly why you cannot guard it.

> Luffy drops from the branch, lands badly in a pile of leaves, and pops back up with a twig in his hair. Sabo keeps one eye on you now, light but watchful. He knows you could take the key if you wanted. He also knows that would change the shape of the morning.

> **Sabo.** If you’re worried somebody will find it, say that. We can move it. But it stays ours.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I tell Sabo I want the key, not an argument.
2. I say we should move the can somewhere safer.
3. I grab for Sabo’s jacket pocket.

## Case I — Deception against contradictory evidence

*setup:* I trip coming down the slope and go face first into the mud, right in front of Sabo and Luffy.

**Player:** I tell them I did that on purpose and that I meant to do it.

> You shove yourself upright, mud sliding off your cheek in a brown ribbon. You lift your chin as if the slope did exactly what you ordered it to.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** You did it on purpose?

> Luffy sits up so fast his straw hat flops over one eye. He looks from the root to the face-shaped dent in the mud, thinking hard enough that his whole forehead wrinkles.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Then I did it on purpose too!

> **Sabo.** Of course you did. Both of you planned a coordinated attack on a patch of dirt. Terrifying.

> Sabo drops from the branch, boots landing cleanly beside the mess. He reaches down—not to help you up, because you are already up—but plucks a fat leaf from your hair and flicks it into the bushes.

> **Sabo.** Since our fearless commander has finished inspecting the ground, can we get moving? The treehouse is uphill, and Luffy has been staring at it like he’s planning a siege.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I’m going up! Ace, let me go up!

> Luffy points through the trees toward the high knot of planks and rope, then plants both muddy sandals as though he can force the answer out of the mountain.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I head up to the treehouse without Luffy.
2. I tell Luffy he can try climbing.
3. I shove mud at Sabo.

## Case J — Dangerous action where clean success would be implausible

**Player:** I jump from this branch across to the next tree, the long gap, without checking it first.

> You jump before either of them can call you an idiot.

The gap is wider from the air. Leaves slap your face; the next trunk comes at you sideways. Your fingers close around a branch thin enough to bend hard under your weight, but it holds. You swing once, boots scraping bark, then haul yourself onto the fork of the tree.

Behind you, Luffy makes a noise like he has just been handed the best idea in the world.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I’m jumping too!

> **Sabo.** No, he is not. Luffy, stay exactly where you are—

> Luffy is already crouched on the branch you left, straw hat shoved down over his eyes. It wobbles beneath him. Sabo has one hand hooked in the trunk beside him, pipe tucked under his arm, staring at the gap and then at Luffy with the expression of someone rapidly calculating how much falling hurts.

> **Sabo.** Ace. Catch him if he does it. I’m serious.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I tell Luffy not to jump.
2. I move along the branch to catch Luffy.
3. I tell Sabo to stop panicking.

## Case K — A settled emotional question, asked again

*setup:* I look at Luffy and tell him straight: "You can come with us. You do not have to keep asking."

*setup:* I sit down next to Sabo and say nothing for a while.

**Player:** I ask Luffy why he keeps following me everywhere.

> Luffy turns toward you so fast his hat slips down his forehead. He catches the brim with both hands, squinting up at you.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Because you go places.

> He says it like that settles every possible objection. Sabo’s pipe stops halfway to his shoulder.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** I was alone, then you came. Then you went away lots. So I go too. You’re Ace.

> His little scabbed knee bumps yours as he shifts closer. He is not trying to be dramatic. He is explaining a rule of the forest, like rain going downhill.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Sabo goes too. Now we’re brothers. Brothers don’t leave brothers behind.

> **Sabo.** That’s a dangerously simple system.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** It’s a good system.

> Sabo looks from Luffy to you. The grin he had a minute ago has gone smaller, but it is still there. Down the mountain, faint through the trees, somebody bangs a pot against a door three times—the unmistakable sound of Dadan deciding lunch has waited long enough.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. "Then come on. Dadan has food."
2. "You can’t follow me into everything."
3. "Sabo, what do you think of his system?"

## Case M — Explicitly withheld secret — the content is NOT the model's to invent

**Player:** Something is bothering me and I do not want to say what. I tell Sabo only that it is not about him.

> The three of you are halfway up the wet, root-tangled path, headed toward the treehouse. Luffy is ahead for once, trying to climb a trunk with no branches low enough to grab. He slides down in a shower of bark and immediately starts again.

> You stop beside a fern taller than your shoulders. Whatever is bothering you stays locked behind your teeth. Sabo notices anyway; he always does.

> **Sabo.** “It’s not about me?” Sabo repeats, pipe resting across his shoulders. “That is a remarkably suspicious thing to say without anyone accusing you of anything.”

> You give him nothing else. Sabo’s grin fades, not all the way, but enough. He studies your face for a second, then looks toward Luffy instead of pressing.

> **Sabo.** “Fine. It isn’t about me. I’m going to accept that because I’m generous and emotionally mature.”

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “I climbed it!”

> Luffy has managed to hook both arms around the trunk. His sandals are kicking uselessly a foot above the ground.

> **Sabo.** “You are currently being defeated by a tree.”

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “No! I’m climbing!”

> Sabo steps closer to you, lowering his voice. “You don’t have to tell me. But don’t go doing something stupid by yourself just because you don’t want to talk, all right?” He says it lightly, but his hand has tightened around his pipe.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** “Ace! Push me!”

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I help Luffy up the tree.
2. I tell Sabo not to worry about what I do.
3. I keep walking toward the treehouse.

## Case N — Absent character — talking to somebody who left

*setup:* I watch Sabo take the path down toward Gray Terminal on his own, until he is out of sight.

**Player:** I turn to Sabo and ask him what he thinks we should do next.

> You turn toward the downhill path, but Sabo is already gone. There is only a bent fern slowly springing back into place where he passed and the distant chatter of insects.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Sabo isn’t here.

> Luffy says it with the mild patience of somebody correcting a mistake in a drawing. Then he grabs the back of your shirt before you can start walking away from him.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** We go get him. Then we ask him.

> His small hand stays clenched in your shirt. Gray Terminal lies downhill beyond the trees, and Sabo went there alone without saying why.

*location: Mount Colubo · present: Monkey D. Luffy*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I head down after Sabo.
2. I tell Luffy to stay here.
3. I ask Luffy why he thinks Sabo went there.

## Case O — Harmless factual mismatch — right intention, wrong incidental detail

*setup:* I lift the flat stone by the treehouse ladder, take the tin can out of the hole under it, and put the stone back.

**Player:** I get the can from under the treehouse floorboards and count what is in it.

> The can is already in your hand; there are no floorboards hiding it. You squat beside the ladder, thumb the lid loose, and tip the contents onto the flat stone you just put back.

> Coins, mostly. A few crumpled bills. Two bent bits of brass Sabo insists can be traded somewhere sensible. You count them twice because Luffy starts counting along after seven and loudly invents numbers.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Eight! Nine! Twenty meat! Thirty-four!

> **Sabo.** That is not how numbers work.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** It is how I count.

> Sabo drops from the branch, lands in a crouch beside you, and sorts the money into neat little piles before you can stop him. His quick fingers come to a halt on the bills.

> **Sabo.** One hundred and fifty thousand beri. Exactly. That’s enough for a boat if we find someone desperate, stupid, or both.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Buy a big boat. I’m coming on it.

> **Sabo.** You can’t buy a big boat for this.

> **Monkey D. Luffy.** Then buy a small big boat.

> He says it with the serene certainty of someone who has solved economics. Sabo snorts despite himself, then glances from the can to you. The joke slips off his face.

> **Sabo.** Look. We should put it back before somebody sees it. And we should talk about why he’s here before he decides he owns a third of it.

*location: The Treehouse · present: Monkey D. Luffy, Sabo*

**Cards offered next:**

1. I put the money back and tell Luffy he gets none of it.
2. I ask Sabo what kind of boat we can actually buy.
3. I tell Luffy he can come if he earns it.
